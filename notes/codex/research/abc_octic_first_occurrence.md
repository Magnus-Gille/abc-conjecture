# First occurrences suffice to test the octic family for abc violations

For the primitive octic triples \((a_N,b_N,c_N)\) indexed by odd \(N\ge3\), let \(R_N=\operatorname{rad}(a_Nb_Nc_N)=\operatorname{rad}(T_N)\). In the signed slope coordinates \(T_N=2|stDU|\sqrt{YV}\), where \(Y=s^2+2t^2, V=(s-t)^2+t^2, D=s^2-2t^2, U=(s+t)^2+t^2\). Write \(f(p)\) for the least admissible index where \(p\mid T_N\). For the point \(A=(1,1)\) on \(E:v^2=u^3-8u^2+8u\), let \(r_p\) be its reduction order and \(d_p\) the formal depth of \([r_p]A\). Define
\[
 P_N=\sum_{p>7,\ f(p)=N}(d_p-1)\log p.
\]
The family contains infinitely many fixed-exponent abc violations if and only if \(P_N\ge\delta N^2\) for infinitely many \(N\) and one fixed \(\delta>0\). In this criterion one may replace \(d_p\) by \(v_p(T_N)\). Thus an accumulation of primes inherited from earlier indices cannot cause a fixed-exponent failure unless a fixed-exponent failure already occurs in the first-occurrence sums. The value of the limsup of these sums is unresolved. [Family and height estimates](abc_octic_full_mixed_orbit.md) · [Whole-radical formula](abc_octic_whole_radical_tail.md)

## Primes that can occur in the factor \(U\)

At any occurrence \(p\mid U_M\) with \(p>7\), reduction at a place above \(p\) gives \([M]A=\pm[\tau]A\) for \(\tau=\sqrt{-2}\). The ordinate squared of \([\tau]A\) is \(-49/8\), so rationality of the reduction implies that \(p\) splits in \(K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-2})\). Also \(r=r_p\mid M^2+2\) is odd. The full module \(\mathbb Z[\tau]A\) after reduction equals the cyclic group generated by \(A\), since \(\tau\) acts there by the integer \(h=\pm M\). Its annihilator is \(\mathfrak a=(r,\tau-h)\), of norm \(r\), with \(h^2\equiv-2\bmod r\). At any fixed odd \(r\) there are at most \(2^{\omega(r)}\) such ideals, by Hensel lifting and the Chinese remainder theorem.

The ring \(\mathbb Z[\tau]\) is principal. Let \(\alpha\) generate one of these ideals. The point \([\alpha]A\) reduces to the identity at every assigned place, with formal depth exactly \(d_p\). Indeed \([r]A=[r/\alpha][\alpha]A\), and the differential of \([r/\alpha]\) is a unit there. This holds because \(p\nmid r\) and the differentials of \([r/\alpha]\) and \([\alpha]\) are integral with product \(r\). To see \(p\nmid r\), the reduction group has even order less than \(2p\) by rational two-torsion and Hasse's bound. The abscissa height of \([\alpha]A\) is \(2r\widehat h(A)+O(1)\). Summing nonnegative finite denominator valuations over the quadratic field bounds the sum of \(d_p\log p\) assigned to this ideal by \(O(r)\). Consequently all primes of rank \(r\) that *ever* divide the factor \(U\) have total first-depth mass \(O(2^{\omega(r)}r)=O(r^{1+o(1)})\). These estimates are uniform in the index of occurrence. [CM height and endomorphisms](abc_octic_mixed_cm_prime_mass.md)

If \(p\mid U_N\) and \(p\) occurred at an earlier admissible \(M<N\), then \(r_p\le2N\). At an earlier ordinary factor (any of the five factors other than \(U\)), \(r_p\) divides one of \(M,2M,M-1,M+1\). If the earlier factor was \(U_M\), then \([M]A=\pm[N]A\) at a common place above \(p\), so \(r_p\mid N-M\) or \(r_p\mid N+M\). Since \(r_p\mid N^2+2\), the number of possible ranks is \(N^{o(1)}\). The preceding bound gives only \(O(N^{1+o(1)})\) total first-depth mass from all those inherited primes. In particular essentially all of \(\log U_N\asymp N^2\) comes from primes making their first appearance anywhere in the family: the total additional formal lifting is \(O(\log N)\), as in the whole-radical formula. This statement counts full multiplicities; control of their excess is the open issue.

## The other factors

At each ordinary factor of \(T_N\) one has \(r_p\mid k\) for some \(k\in\{N,2N,N-1,N+1\}\). Over all these ranks, the primes that can ever divide \(U\) have total first-depth mass \(O(N^{1+o(1)})\), by the same height estimate and the divisor bound. Consider the remaining primes of rank \(r\). They all occur by index \(2r+1\), since the point giving the slope then is \([r+1]A\), which reduces to the simple pole \(A\) of the slope function. Thus \(p\mid t_{2r+1}\). Their least admissible occurrence \(m\) is ordinary and satisfies
\[
 3\le m\le2r+1,\qquad r\mid 2m\ \text{or}\ r\mid m-1\ \text{or}\ r\mid m+1.
\]
There are at most an absolute constant number of such \(m\) at any fixed \(r\), and \(m\ge r/2\). Let \(\mathcal M_r\) be this finite candidate set, with only odd indices retained.

Write \(L_N=\sum_{p>7,\,p\mid T_N}(d_p-1)\log p\). The preceding estimates give
\[
 P_N\le L_N\le P_N+O(N^{1+o(1)})+
 \sum_{k\in\{N,2N,N-1,N+1\}}\ \sum_{r\mid k}\ \sum_{m\in\mathcal M_r,\,m\le N}P_m.
\]
For each \(k\), \(\sum_{r\mid k}r^2\le\zeta(2)k^2\). Using \(m\le 2r+1\) and the bounded number of candidates, the total weight \(\sum m^2\) on the right is \(O(N^2)\) with an absolute constant. Terms with \(m\) below any fixed threshold contribute \(O(1)\), since then \(r\le2m\). It follows that \(P_m=o(m^2)\) implies \(L_N=o(N^2)\); quantitatively \(\limsup L_N/N^2\ll\limsup P_N/N^2\). The reverse inequality is immediate. The whole-radical formula gives \(\log c_N-\log R_N=L_N+o(N^2)\), \(\log c_N\asymp N^2\), and a total \(O(\log N)\) error in replacing \(d_p\) by \(v_p(T_N)\) for \(p>7\). Together these prove the criterion.
