# The first square in the paired quartic on the elliptic slice

For odd n≥3 and prime bases 3<p≤10⁹, the first square in the paired factor F₊F₋ occurs at **n=209**. Exactly 191² divides F₊ at this index and 191 does not divide F₋. Indeed the positive coprime parameters satisfy x≡18, y≡36 modulo 191, and
\[
 F_+/191^2\equiv27,\quad F_-\equiv83,\quad (F_+F_-)/191^2\equiv140\pmod {191}.
\]
Direct rational arithmetic on the original curve and an integer sieve verify that this is the only pair in the entire rectangle of odd n≤209 and 3<p≤10⁹. Additionally, complete proven factorization of each F₊ and F₋ for odd n≤13 establishes absence of any square with base p>3 there, without a prime bound. [Integer verification](abc_eisenstein_slice_paired_verify.py) · [Billion-prime-bound output](abc_eisenstein_slice_paired_verify_1b.txt)

## Passing to the isogenous curve

Use the [elliptic slice](abc_eisenstein_twelfth_elliptic_slice.md) and its 2-isogeny onto v²=u³−576u. The image Q of P is (−8,−64). Its abscissa after translation is u=X+12+36/X, so X(nP)=−2(x/y)² gives
\[
 u(nQ)=-2\frac{(x^2-3y^2)^2}{x^2y^2},\qquad
 J(u)=u^2+336u+576,\qquad 4F_+F_-=(xy)^4J(u(nQ)).
\]
If p>3 divides xy it divides neither quartic, by primitivity. At any zero of J in the finite residue field, u is a simple root and v is nonzero: the quadratic discriminant is 192²·3, and none of the three two-torsion abscissas 0,24,−24 can be a root away from 2,3. Consequently local evaluations of J at a root have exactly the same p-adic valuation as F₊F₋. The two quartics cannot vanish together modulo p>3: their difference is 24xy(x²−3y²), and at x²=3y² the common term F₀ is −12x²y².

## Complete periods through one million

All 78,496 primes 3<p≤10⁶ were tested. Among them 2,777 have an odd multiple of Q on J=0 modulo p. Exactly 2,623 have two odd classes and 154 have four; 2,474 of the visiting primes are 23 modulo 24 and the remaining 303 are 1 modulo 24. All 5,862 odd root classes lift uniquely to square divisibility. The least positive odd representative of every lifted class has valuation exactly two. None already has a square at the least positive odd representative of its original class modulo p. The two roots of J are −168±96√3. For each finite-field target, find its ordinate if it exists and compute its discrete logarithm in the subgroup generated by Q by baby-step giant-step. The negative ordinate corresponds to the negative of the same logarithm. The point order r is obtained by removing prime factors from a CM annihilating multiple checked explicitly on Q. The odd classes have ambient index period T=lcm(2,r). Write A(n)=J(u(nQ)). At n=h+jT the translation by TQ lies in the formal group. Modulo p² its parameter adds linearly in j, so
\[
 A(h+jT)/p\equiv A(h)/p+j[A(h+T)-A(h)]/p\pmod p.
\]
The code verifies the nonzero slope and computes A(n) modulo p³ from the division-polynomial identity u(nQ)=−8−ψₙ₋₁ψₙ₊₁/ψₙ², with unit denominator at the visited points. For example at p=191 and h=17 the initial value and slope are 73 and 158 modulo 191. The unique digit is 8, giving 17+8·24=209. The complete square support at this prime is n≡209 or 4,375 modulo 4,584.

The earliest odd index with **two** distinct square prime bases through 10⁶ is **3,484,049**, for p=191 and 311. The respective residues A(n)/p² are 89 and 173 modulo their primes. Enumerating the archived classes through n≤10⁸ gives 105,286 distinct indices, with 58 containing at least two primes. [Finite collision check](abc_eisenstein_slice_paired_summary.py)

## Independent early-index exhaustion

For every odd n≤209, compute nP over the rationals, extract the positive integers x,y, and evaluate both quartics as integers. Any p>3 dividing F₊F₋ divides U on this primitive twelfth-power conic and so is ±1 modulo 24 by the [projective order argument](abc_eisenstein_power_conic.md). Sieve all 12,710,873 primes in those two classes through 10⁹ and multiply them to form a squarefree integer D of 360,636,573 bits. Set g=gcd(D,∏ₙ|F₊F₋|). For each single value f=|F₊F₋|, put h=gcd(g,f). Then gcd(h,f/h) is precisely the product of the allowed primes occurring to exponent at least two. This test is independent of the finite-field discrete logarithms and Hensel lifts.

The orbit and collision scripts use Python's standard library. The integer verification additionally uses python-flint.

~~~sh
python abc_eisenstein_slice_paired_quartic.py 1000000 > /tmp/paired-classes.csv
python abc_eisenstein_slice_paired_summary.py /tmp/paired-classes.csv
python abc_eisenstein_slice_paired_verify.py 1000000000 209
~~~
