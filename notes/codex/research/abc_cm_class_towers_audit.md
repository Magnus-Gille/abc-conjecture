# Two class-group components in the full CM torsion field

Take \(E:y^2=x^3+16x^2+32x\), \(R=(-8,16)\), \(K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-2})\), and a rational prime \(p>7\) split as \(\pi\bar\pi\) in \(K\). Write \(d=\operatorname{ord}(R\bmod p)\) and \(e=v_p(B_d)\), where \(B_d^2\) is the reduced abscissa denominator of \([d]R\). The following conclusions hold even when \(d\) is composite.

For \(m\ge1\), take \(F_m=K(E[p^m])\), \(G_m=\operatorname{Gal}(F_m/K)\) and \(s=\min(m,e-1)\). For either \(\alpha\in\{\pi,\bar\pi\}\), put \(V_{\alpha,m}=E[\alpha^m]\). Then

\[
\operatorname{Hom}_{G_m}(\operatorname{Cl}(F_m),V_{\alpha,m})
\simeq \mathbb Z/p^s\mathbb Z .
\]

Together these two groups give a quotient of the class group isomorphic to \((\mathbb Z/p^s\mathbb Z)^2\). In particular \(p^{2s}\mid h(F_m)\). For \(e=1\), the entire \(p\)-primary component of the class group belonging to either of the two tame characters is zero. For \(e>1\), each is a nonzero cyclic module over the group ring of the \(p\)-Sylow subgroup of \(G_m\).

## Cohomological descent through both division towers

Fix \(V=V_{\pi,m}\). The full CM action makes \(G_m\) an abelian subgroup of \((\mathcal O/p^m)^\times\). Let \(v\mid p\) be the place where \([\pi]\) has nonunit differential. The formal series for \([\pi]\) has reduction of initial degree \(p\), and its linear coefficient has valuation one. Weierstrass preparation of \([\pi](t)/t\) gives an Eisenstein polynomial of degree \(p-1\) even after unramified base change. Therefore an element of inertia acts on \(V\) by a scalar \(u\not\equiv1\pmod p\). For a central group element, its action on the coefficient module induces identity on group cohomology. Since \(u-1\) is a unit, \(H^i(G_m,V)=0\) for \(i>0\). Restriction identifies \(H^1(K,V)\) with \(H^1(F_m,V)^{G_m}\).

By unramified class field theory the stated Hom corresponds to classes whose restrictions to \(F_m\) are unramified. For a local inertia group \(I\) over \(K\), a class trivial on \(I\cap G_{F_m}\) comes by inflation from the finite image \(J\subset G_m\) of \(I\). At \(v\), \(J\) contains the above central scalar, so \(H^1(J,V)=0\). At the other place over \(p\), \(J\) acts trivially on \(V\) since \([\pi^m]\) is étale. The inertia restriction there is a homomorphism \(J\to V\) equivariant under a Frobenius lift. The source action by conjugation is trivial because \(G_m\) is abelian. Frobenius on \(V\) acts by a scalar whose reduction differs from one. Indeed \(\#E(\mathbb F_p)\) is even and strictly less than \(2p\) by rational two-torsion and Hasse, so \(p\nmid\#E(\mathbb F_p)\). This forces the homomorphism to be zero.

Odd places away from \(p\) have good reduction and \(J=1\). At the dyadic place potential good reduction makes inertia finite on the 3-adic Tate module, and the kernel of reduction mod 3 is torsion-free. Hence \(K(E[3])\) kills this inertia and gives good reduction. The group \(J\) has order dividing 48, prime to \(p\). Thus at every place unramifiedness after restriction to \(F_m\) descends to unramifiedness over \(K\). The same proof works for \(K(E[\pi^m])\) in place of \(F_m\).

## Strict Selmer localization

At odd good places away from \(p\), Kummer and unramified local conditions agree by finite étale reduction and Lang's theorem. At the dyadic place both conditions vanish. The reduction group has no \(p\)-torsion by the order bound of five in good reduction, or the additive nonsingular group of order two and geometric component group of order at most four in additive reduction. The formal kernel is pro-2. Thus \(E(K_2)/[\pi^m]E(K_2)=0\), while the Frobenius coinvariants on \(V^{I_2}\) have the same size as their invariants \(V^{G_{K_2}}=0\). At the place over \(p\) where \([\pi]\) is étale, the same local conditions vanish by nonanomalous reduction and the unit differential. At \(v\) unramified cohomology vanishes since \(V^I=0\).

Consequently the required group is the kernel of localization at \(v\) on the \(\pi^m\)-isogeny Selmer group over \(K\). Since \([K:\mathbb Q]=2\) is prime to \(p\), restriction identifies the rational \(p^m\)-Selmer group with the conjugation invariants of the \(p^m\)-Selmer group over \(K\). The Kummer condition descends by restriction and corestriction on local \(H^1(-,E)\). Complex conjugation exchanges the \(\pi^m\) and \(\bar\pi^m\) summands and the latter has zero local Kummer group at \(v\). Thus the desired kernel is the strict rational \(p^m\)-Selmer group.

The rational curve has rank one with generator \(R\) modulo its 2-torsion, and trivial Tate–Shafarevich group by the proved BSD formula in this conductor range and the curve data. Hence the rational Selmer group is cyclic of order \(p^m\), generated by the Kummer class of \(R\). Since \(p\nmid d\), multiplication by \(d\) is a unit on the local quotient. The point \([d]R\) has formal depth \(e\). Under the formal logarithm its image in \(p\mathbb Z_p/p^{m+1}\mathbb Z_p\) has additive order \(p^{m-s}\). The strict kernel has order \(p^s\). ## Tame components

Write \(G_m=D\times\Gamma\), where \(|D|\) is prime to \(p\) and \(\Gamma\) is a \(p\)-group. The two torsion actions restrict to distinct Teichmüller characters of \(D\), since inertia at \(v\) acts nontrivially modulo \(p\) on one and trivially on the other. Their idempotents are defined over \(\mathbb Z_p\). A generator of either group of homomorphisms has an image of order \(p^s\), and each homomorphism factors through its corresponding idempotent. The two quotient maps together therefore have image of order \(p^{2s}\).

For the corresponding tame component \(A=e_\chi\operatorname{Cl}(F_m)\{p\}\), its minimal number of \(\mathbb Z_p[\Gamma]\)-generators is the dimension of \(\operatorname{Hom}_{G_m}(\operatorname{Cl}(F_m),V[p])\) over \(\mathbb F_p\), by Nakayama's lemma. The latter is precisely the subgroup killed by \(p\) in the computed cyclic Hom. This proves both tame-component assertions. The conclusion concerns these two characters and leaves the other characters unrestricted.
