# Exact exponent two on four fifths of the mixed-orbit multipliers

Let \(m\) vary through the positive odd multipliers on the orbit of \((7,4)\), so its ambient index is \(N=3m\). On \(E':y^2=x^3+16x^2+32x\) put \(R_0=(-8,16)\), and write \(B_N^2\) for the reduced denominator of \(x([N]R_0)\). The mixed norm satisfies \(Y_{3m}=81A_m^2\), with \(A_m=B_{3m}/9\). [Denominator formula](abc_octic_mixed_denominator_characters.md)

There is an explicit periodic set of odd \(m\) with relative natural density **0.8017231569469653…**, on every one of which some prime has valuation exactly one in \(A_m\), hence exactly two in \(Y_{3m}\). In particular \(Y_{3m}\) fails cubefulness on this entire set. The primes in the certificate have independently verified first depths of exactly one.

For each of 95 distinct odd primes \(q\le1000\), the verifier gives a distinct prime \(p>1000\), with \(r=\operatorname{ord}(R_0\bmod p)\) satisfying \(r/\gcd(r,3)=q\). For example the triples \((q,p,r)\) include \((3,13411,9),(5,294241,15),(7,117041,7)\). Each prime is good and \(R_0\) integral. At \(p\), the multiplication formulas have no cancellation, so \(v_p(B_r)=v_p(\psi_r(R_0))\). The verifier checks \(\psi_r(R_0)=0\bmod p\) and \(\psi_r(R_0)\ne0\bmod p^2\). It checks the exact order by the division polynomials at \(r/d\) for the primes \(d\mid r\).

It follows that \(p\mid A_m\) exactly when \(q\mid m\). At these indices the formal logarithm on the good curve gives \(v_p(A_m)=1+v_p(3m/r)=1+v_p(m)\). Thus the certificate proves the exact exponent one precisely for \(q\mid m,\ p\nmid m\). The Chinese remainder theorem makes these events independent since the 190 primes are distinct. The relative density of their union among odd \(m\) is exactly
\[
 1-\prod_{(q,p)}\left(1-\frac{p-1}{pq}\right)>\frac45.
\]
The verifier checks this inequality in exact rational arithmetic. It uses only standard-library Python. [Witnesses and verifier](abc_octic_mixed_simple_density.py)

The logarithmic contribution of the certified primes of depth one at index \(m\) is \(\sum_{(q,p)}\mathbf1_{q\mid m,\ p\nmid m}\log p\). Its mean over odd indices tends to 16.665475672…, and its maximum is 1104.986819218… . The maximum is attained on infinitely many indices by the Chinese remainder theorem. The product attained there has 480 decimal digits. Since \(\log A_m\asymp m^2\), even this maximum is a vanishing fraction of the logarithm. Except for the finite list of first ranks, these simple primes are inherited from earlier indices. Proving unboundedly many distinct first ranks with depth one would give infinitely many distinct elliptic non-Wieferich primes for this fixed rational point.

## All odd ambient indices

The same verifier certifies an exact-exponent-two prime in \(Y_N\) on a periodic set of relative density **0.6682972047199796…** among all odd ambient indices \(N\). This exceeds two thirds. On the third of odd indices with \(3\mid N\), use the preceding 95 witnesses. On the remaining two thirds, take the independently checked list of 54 pairs \((q,p)\) for which \(R_0\) has exact order \(q\) modulo \(p\) and first depth one. The source and witness sets in this second list are disjoint. Conditional on \(3\nmid N\), their union has density \(1-\prod(1-(p-1)/(pq))=0.6015842286064866\ldots\). The weighted mean of the two conditional densities gives the displayed bound.

## Why the residue-character tables cannot recognize depth one by themselves

Fix an inert prime index \(\ell>3\). Every prime of \(A_\ell\) is split in \(K=\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-2})\) and has a generator \(\pi\) above it satisfying \(\pi\equiv1\bmod\ell\mathcal O_K\), by the CM Frobenius calculation. Let \(D\) be any positive integer coprime to \(A_\ell\). The unit residue of \(A_\ell\) modulo \(D\), even together with this CM norm restriction on every prime, is also represented by integers with all prime exponents in \(\{2,3\}\).

To see this, for every \(p^e\parallel A_\ell\) fix its generator \(\pi\). Both \(\pi^e\) and its inverse are units modulo \(D\ell\mathcal O_K\) and are one modulo \(\ell\). The prime ideal theorem in ray classes supplies degree-one prime ideals generated by elements in each of these two residue classes; the ring has class number one. One may choose different rational primes at every selection, since the classes have positive density and the residue-degree-two primes have density zero. Write their rational norms as \(p_+,p_-\), respectively. Then \(p_+^3p_-^2\equiv p^e\bmod D\) and both rational primes have the required CM norm form. Taking the product proves the assertion. The same construction works independently for pairwise coprime factors when testing finitely many of their unit residues. This observation concerns congruence and norm-shape information. The replacement integers are not asserted to be elliptic denominator values. [CM norm calculation](abc_octic_mixed_seed_prime_orbit.md)

The formal group also limits what a multiplicative relation among finitely many denominator values can say at a new prime. If \(p\) has first rank \(n\) and initial depth \(e\), then \(v_p(B_{k_i n})=e+v_p(k_i)\). At \(p>\max k_i\), the whole vector of these valuations is \((e,\ldots,e)\). In particular the valuation of \(\prod_i B_{k_i n}^{c_i}\) is \(e\sum_i c_i\). Its vanishing imposes the same condition for every positive initial depth. Requiring this product to be a \(q\)-th power can impose divisibility of \(e\sum_i c_i\) by \(q\), but finitely many such divisibility tests give the same local outcome for \(e=1\) and for \(e=1+\operatorname{lcm}(q_1,\ldots,q_j)\).

## The largest prime divisor of a cubeful multiplier

Every odd multiplier \(m>1\) with \(Y_{3m}\) cubeful must have a prime divisor **at least 191**. For each odd prime \(q<191\), the certificate gives a good prime \(p>q\) of first depth one, whose exact ambient order \(r\) satisfies \(r/\gcd(3,r)=q\). The additional rows are \((53,9623953,53)\), \((101,1102921,303)\), \((109,3064427,327)\), \((151,8186774249,151)\), \((157,18925409,471)\), \((167,4522027,501)\), \((173,1976699,519)\), and \((181,3147953,543)\). At \(q=3\) one may also use \(p=11\), where \(\psi_9(R_0)=110\bmod121\) and \(\psi_3(R_0)\ne0\bmod11\).

If the largest prime \(q\mid m\) is in this range, its witness prime satisfies \(p\nmid m\). As \(q\mid m\), that witness has \(v_p(Y_{3m})=2\), contradicting cubefulness. The seed \(m=1\) has \(Y_3=81\). The bound excludes all other indices supported on primes below 191, regardless of their exponents. It does not decide the case of largest prime 191.

More generally, a nontrivial cubeful multiplier exists **if and only if a cubeful prime multiplier exists**. If \(m>1\) is such a multiplier, put \(q=P^+(m)\), its largest prime divisor. For every \(1\le j\le v_q(m)\), the multiplier \(q^j\) must itself give a cubeful norm. In fact a prime of valuation two in \(Y_{3q^j}\) would have odd ambient order \(r\mid3q^j\). The ranks dividing three contribute only the seed prime 3, to valuation at least four. Thus \(q\mid r\). At the good prime in question the rational 2-torsion and the odd-order point give \(2q\mid\#E'(\mathbf F_p)\), whence \(p\ge(\sqrt{2q}-1)^2>q\) for \(q\ge7\). The cases \(q=3,5\) have already been excluded by the certificate. A prime \(p>q\) cannot divide \(m/q^j\), so its deficient valuation at \(q^j\) would persist at \(m\). Taking \(j=1\) proves the equivalence; the reverse direction uses the prime multiplier itself. If cubeful multipliers occur with unbounded largest prime, their largest primes therefore already give an unbounded sequence of cubeful multipliers.
