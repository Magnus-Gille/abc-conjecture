# A split-prime certificate for the rational slope on the square cover

Put \(t=\sqrt3\), \(K=\mathbb Q(t)\), and \(\delta=1+t\). On the quartic
\[
 \delta W^2=(r^2+3)(r^2+2tr-3)
\]
suppose \(r\in\mathbb Q\) is an original square-denominator slope. Reduction at 47 and a formal parameter on the rank-one curve \(E/K:Y^2=X^3+9(2+t)X\) prove **\(r=1\)**. This gives \(n=\pm1\) on the original orbit. The sole auxiliary prime required to constrain the original index is 577. ## Choosing the sign of the slope

Write \(r=x/y\) with \(x,y\) odd and coprime, \(3\nmid x\). The conditions are \(r^2+3=h_0^2\) and \(x^4-18x^2y^2+9y^4=-8a^2\). The algebraic integer \(F=x^2+2txy-3y^2\) thus has norm \(-8a^2\). A prime ideal away from 2 dividing both \(F\) and its conjugate would divide \(4txy\), contrary to primitivity and \(3\nmid x\). Therefore valuations away from 2 are even. The valuation of \(F\) at \(\delta\) is odd since 2 ramifies and \(N(F)=-8a^2\). The class number of \(K\) is one by the Minkowski bound, and the fundamental unit is \(\varepsilon=2+t\). The possible classes are \(\pm\delta,\pm\varepsilon\delta\). Reduction at the prime \((t)\) gives \(F\equiv1\), \(\delta\equiv1\), and \(\varepsilon\equiv-1\pmod t\). Hence the possible classes are \(\delta\) and \(-\varepsilon\delta\). Conjugation interchanges them because \(\overline\delta=-\delta/\varepsilon\). Replacing \(x\) by \(-x\) if necessary puts \(F\) in the class of \(\delta\), and multiplication by \((h_0/y)^2\) gives the stated cover.

## The two residue discs

On \(E_0:y^2=x^3+36x^2+36x\), the point \(P=(-2,8)\) has order 24 modulo 577. Since \(8(6-x(nP))=(4h_0)^2\), the residue of this quantity must be a square or zero whenever \(nP\) is finite. For the odd indices modulo 24 finite-field addition leaves exactly \(1,11,13,23\), so \(n\equiv\pm1\pmod{12}\). Modulo 47 the order of \(P\) is 12. Thus \(nP=\pm P\) in reduction, forcing \(r\equiv\pm1\pmod{47}\) from \(x(nP)=-2r^2\). The prime splits in \(K\). At \(r=-1\) the cover gives \(W^2=-8\), a nonsquare modulo 47. At \(r=1\) the two roots are \(W=\pm2(t-1)\), both nonzero in either residue field.

Use the following Weierstrass transformation, with \(u=1/(r-1)\) and \(z=Wu^2/[2(t-1)]\). Put
\[
 \beta=t/2+5/4,\quad \gamma=-t/4-13/32,\quad
 h=45t/32+153/64,\quad c=3t/4+21/16.
\]
Define \(U=2(z+u^2+\beta u+\gamma)\), \(V=(2u+\beta)U+h\), and \(X=4(U+c),Y=8V\). Direct expansion gives \(Y^2=X^3+9(2+t)X\). The rational inverse has \(r=1+2U/(V-\beta U-h)\). On the positive branch as \(u\to\infty\), one has \(U\sim4u^2,V\sim8u^3\), giving the origin and
\[
             r-1=-4z_E+O(z_E^2),\qquad z_E=-X/Y.
\]
The coefficients are integral at both places above 47 by the inverse formula and the good-reduction Weierstrass expansions at the origin. On the negative branch, \(z^2=(u^2+\beta u+\gamma)^2+hu-297/1024-9t/64\) gives \(U=-h/u+O(1/u^2)\). The point at infinity is then \(D=(4c,-8h)\). Consequently each embedding of a lift lies in the residue disc of \(O\) or \(D\). The involution \(W\mapsto-W\) interchanges the two discs at each embedding and preserves \(r\).

## The Mordell–Weil rank

The 2-isogenous pair has equations \(Y^2=X(X^2+B)\) with \(B=9\varepsilon\) and \(B'=-36\varepsilon\). All finite valuations of either coefficient are divisible by four, including those over 2 and 3 by ramification. If \(v(X)\) were odd, \(2v(X)\ne v(B)\) and \(v(Y^2)=v(X)+\min(2v(X),v(B))\) would be odd. Thus both two-isogeny descent images consist of classes of units, using class number one. The two exceptional abscissas contribute the unit class of the corresponding coefficient and the trivial class. Among the four unit squareclasses \(\pm1,\pm\varepsilon\), the original curve admits at most two because \(B\) is totally positive and nonzero abscissas are positive at both real embeddings. The two-isogeny rank formula gives \(2^{\rm rank}\le2\cdot4/4=2\). The nonzero formal logarithm of \(12D\) below gives equality.

## Excluding the mixed discs

Each group \(E(\mathbb F_{47})\) has order 48. The orders of \(D\) in the two factors are 3 and 12. A nonzero 3-torsion point would have \(X^2/A=-1\pm2t/3\) with \(A=9(2+t)\), by the 3-division polynomial. The right side changes sign between the real embeddings, while \(A\) is totally positive, proving \(E(K)[3]=0\).

Multiply both reductions by 16. Their image is a quotient of \(E(K)/3E(K)\), which has order 3 by the rank bound and absence of rational 3-torsion. It is generated by the image of \(16D\), whose two components are nonzero. Thus neither mixed pair \((O,D)\) nor \((D,O)\) can be the reductions of a global point. The involution reduces the two remaining choices to \((O,O)\).

At both places \(12D\) lies in the formal group. The table computes \(2D,3D,6D,12D\) in Jacobian coordinates, where \(z_E=-XZ/Y\). The formal logarithm has the same leading digit as \(z_E\) throughout \(47\mathbb Z_{47}\), so the logarithm vector of \(12D\) has direction \((26,24)\) after division by 47 and reduction. Both components are units and they differ.

For a global point \(Q\) in the joint identity disc the logarithm vector is a rational multiple of that of \(12D\), because rank is one, \(12D\) has nonzero formal logarithm, and the logarithm kills torsion. If \(r\ne1\), the expansion of \(r-1\) at the origin makes the two logarithms have the same valuation and the same leading digit in \(\mathbb Q_{47}\), since \(r\) is rational and the linear coefficient \(-4\) is the same. They cannot lie on the specified line. This proves \(r=1\). Its two possible \(W\) are the seed pair, and comparison of \(x(nP)\) with \(x(P)\) gives \(n=\pm1\) because \(P\) has infinite order.
