# Odd prime valuations at inert indices from signed CM Frobenius

Let \(B_r^2\) be the reduced abscissa denominator of \([r]R\) on \(E':y^2=x^3+16x^2+32x\), where \(R=(-8,16)\). For every prime index \(r\equiv5\bmod8\), at least one prime \(p\) has odd \(v_p(B_r)\) and satisfies the bound in the last column.

| Prime index | Lower bound for every \(p\mid B_r\) | Lower bound for some \(p\) of odd valuation |
|---|---:|---:|
| \(r\equiv5\bmod24\) | \(6r^2-4r+1\) | \(12r^2-4r+1\) |
| \(r\equiv13\bmod24\) | \(18r^2-8r+1\) | \(54r^2+12r+1\) |

Both bounds in the middle column are attained by simple divisors, at \(r=149,\ p=132611\) and \(r=733,\ p=9665339\), respectively. The first bound in the last column is attained at \(r=5\), where \(B_5=281\). If \(B_r\) is squareful, the odd valuation in the last column is at least three. This gives a logarithmic contribution of order at least \(\log r\), while \(\log B_r\) grows quadratically in \(r\). ## Which generator gives Frobenius?

Put \(K=\mathbf Q(\tau)\), \(\tau=\sqrt{-2}\), and \(\mathcal O=\mathbf Z[\tau]\). For any prime \(p\mid B_r\), the prime splits in \(K\) and the reduction of \(R\) has annihilator \((r)\). This uses the inertness of \(r\), the exact ordinary reduction order \(r\) of this affine point, and the CM action. A Frobenius generator and its conjugate therefore both have the form \(\pi=A+B\tau\) with \(A=1+ra,\ B=rb,\ b\ne0\) and \(p=A^2+2B^2\). We take \(b>0\) when listing norms. [CM at inert prime indices](abc_odd_rank_quadratic_large_gaps.md)

For odd \(A\) define \(\chi(A,B)\) as the product of two signs. The first is \(+1\) at \(A\equiv1,3\bmod8\) and \(-1\) otherwise. The second is \(+1\) at \(4\mid B\) and \(-1\) otherwise. Then
\[
                             \chi(A,B)=+1
\]
for the signed Frobenius. Indeed \(E'\) is isomorphic over \(\mathbf Q\) to \(y^2=x^3+4x^2+2x\), of conductor \(256\). For its weight-two CM Hecke character the conductor-discriminant formula gives ideal conductor of norm \(256/8=32\), necessarily \((\tau^5)\). Class number one and the CM Frobenius ideal property make the quotient of the Hecke character on a principal ideal by its generator a unit sign, for the CM type fixing the chosen embedding. It depends only on the generator modulo \(\tau^5\). These units have coordinates \(A\bmod8,\ B\bmod4\), with \(A\) odd. The displayed sign is multiplicative on all 16 units. This group is generated by \(-1,1+\tau,3+2\tau\), and the finite sign takes value \(-1\) at each. At \(-1\) this follows from the trivial ideal. The other two generate primes above 3 and 17, where direct point counts give traces \(-2,-6\). This determines the character. [Conductor check for this model](abc_octic_dyadic_adjacent_powers.py) · [Finite group and trace verification](abc_odd_rank_signed_frobenius.py)

When \(b\) is even, \(p\equiv1\bmod8\), so \(E'[2]\) is rational over \(\mathbf F_p\). The odd-order point \(R\) is a double. The halving criterion at the cubic roots \(0,-8\pm4\sqrt2\) makes \(\sqrt2\) a square. A square root of \(-1\) is itself a square modulo this \(p\), so either reduction of \(\tau\) is also a square. Since \(\tau\equiv-A/B\bmod\pi\), quadratic reciprocity gives
\[
  1=\left(\frac{\tau\bmod\pi}{p}\right)
    =\left(\frac{A}{p}\right)\left(\frac{B}{p}\right)
    =\left(\frac{2}{|A|}\right).
\]
Here \(\gcd(A,B)=1\), \(p\equiv2B^2\bmod|A|\), and \(p\equiv A^2\) modulo the odd part of \(|B|\). Both \(-1\) and 2 have quadratic character one at \(p\). Thus \(A\equiv1,7\bmod8\) when \(b\) is even. Combining this with \(\chi=1\) at \(r\equiv5\bmod8\) gives
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
b\bmod4 & a\bmod8\\ \hline
0 & 0\\
2 & 6\\
1,3 & 4,6.
\end{array}
\]

## Retaining the conjugate prime orientation

On \(E:v^2=u^3-8u^2+8u\) take \(W=(-2\tau,8)\). The two-isogeny \(\phi:E\to E'\) with kernel \(\{O,J\}\) gives \(\phi(W)=R\), and \(\bar W=W+J\). For odd \(r\), if \(p\mid B_r\), then at one of the conjugate places \([r]W\) reduces to \(O\) and at the other to \(J\). This follows from \(\phi([r]W)=[r]R\) and the conjugation formula. At the place above \(O\), étaleness of \(\phi\) on the formal group makes the abscissa denominator exponent of \([r]W\), after taking its square root, exactly \(v_p(B_r)\). Conversely every odd prime ideal at which \([r]W\) reduces to \(O\) lies over a prime dividing \(B_r\). [Isogeny formulas](abc_octic_mixed_denominator_characters.md)

Write \(D_n=2^{1-n^2}\psi_n(W)\) and \(T_n=D_n\) for odd \(n\), \(T_n=D_n/2\) for even \(n\). The first five \(T_n\), starting at zero, are \(0,1,1,-1-2\tau,2\). Homogeneity of the division recurrences gives
\[
 T_{2k}=T_k(T_{k+2}T_{k-1}^2-T_{k-2}T_{k+1}^2).
\]
For \(T_{2k+1}\) use \(T_{k+2}T_k^3-T_{k-1}T_{k+1}^3\), multiplying the summand with even subscripts by 16. This proves integrality. Modulo 3 the sequence has period 12, and modulo 8 it has period 16. The verifier generates a full period from the recurrences, repeats it cyclically, and tests both identities on every residue, proving the periods by strong induction. Writing \(D_r=d+e\tau\), for every \(r\equiv13\bmod24\) it gives
\[
 D_r\equiv1\bmod3,\qquad
 d\equiv1\bmod8,\qquad e\equiv2\bmod4.
\]

Since \(W\) is integral at odd primes of good reduction, the valuation of \(\psi_r(W)\) at those places gives its abscissa denominator exponent after taking square roots. The displayed dyadic residue makes \(D_r\) a dyadic unit. Consequently \(\gamma=-D_r\) is the product of the oriented Frobenius generators above the \(p\mid B_r\), to exponents \(v_p(B_r)\). Both sides have the same ideal factorization, and \(\chi(\gamma)=+1\) fixes the unit sign. We have \(\gamma\equiv-1\bmod3\), \(N\gamma=B_r\). Independently \(B_r\equiv1\bmod8\) for all the indices here. Indeed the full mixed slope has \(B_r^2=s^2+2t^2\) with \(s\) odd and \(4\mid t\). The two coprime conjugate factors \(s\pm t\tau\) are squares up to sign. Writing one as \(\pm(u+v\tau)^2\) gives \(u\) odd, \(v\) even, and \(B_r=u^2+2v^2\equiv1\bmod8\). ## The small norm possibilities

Let \(c=a^2+2b^2\), so \(p=cr^2+2ar+1\). In addition to the table for \(a\bmod8\), reject norm values divisible by 3. They exceed 3 since \(b\ne0\). At \(r\equiv5\bmod24\), the only surviving \((c,2a,b)\) through \(c=16\) are \((6,-4,1),(12,-4,2)\). The full slope fourth-power classification gives that \(B_r\) is nonsquare at every prime index here. If the only odd exponent were on the first candidate its prime would be \(3\bmod8\), contradicting \(B_r\equiv1\bmod8\). The higher coefficients \(c\ge17\) give \(p\ge (r\sqrt c-1)^2>12r^2-4r+1\) for \(r\ge5\). This proves both claims in the first row. [Nonsquare classification](abc_octic_mixed_elliptic_chabauty_11.md)

At \(r\equiv13\bmod24\), the only surviving triples through \(c=56\) are
\[
       (18,-8,1),\ (22,-4,3),\ (34,8,3),\ (54,12,3).
\]
For \(c\ge57,\ r\ge13\), the bound \((r\sqrt c-1)^2>54r^2+12r+1\) applies. The first three norms are all \(3\bmod8\). Modulo 3, the first norm is \(-1\); each oriented generator for the second and third is \(-1\) in \(\mathcal O/(3)\), in either conjugation. Suppose that all valuations outside the first three candidates are even. In \(\mathcal O/(3)\simeq\mathbf F_3\times\mathbf F_3\), every unit has square one. Since \(B_r=N\gamma\equiv1\bmod3\), the exponent on the first candidate is even. Since \(\gamma\equiv-1\bmod3\), the sum of the exponents on the second and third candidates is odd. Their norms are both \(3\bmod8\), giving \(B_r\equiv3\bmod8\), a contradiction. Missing or composite candidates are assigned exponent zero. This proves both conclusions for the second row.

The sharp examples for the least divisors are certified by trial-division primality tests and \(\psi_{149}(R)/132611\equiv14816\bmod132611\), \(\psi_{733}(R)/9665339\equiv4109457\bmod9665339\), calculated by evaluating the division polynomials modulo the squares of those primes.

## A congruence at the moving prime index

Let \(s+t\tau=\varepsilon(u+v\tau)^2\) be the primitive representation used above, \(\varepsilon=\pm1\). Normalize \(u+v\tau\) by \(\chi(u,v)=+1\), which fixes its sign. Every prime ideal factor has a generator of sign \(\chi=+1\) congruent to \(1\bmod(r)\), for either orientation at its rational prime. The unit ambiguity is therefore trivial and \(u+v\tau\equiv1\bmod(r)\). It follows that \(r\mid t\), \(s\equiv\varepsilon\bmod r\), and
\[
 B_r\equiv\varepsilon s\pmod{r^2},\qquad
 v_r(B_r-\varepsilon s)=2v_r(t).
\]
Indeed \(B_r-\varepsilon s=4v^2\), \(t=2\varepsilon uv\), and \(r\nmid u\).

On \(E\), put \(A=(1,1)\) and \(Q=[(r+1)/2]A\). Its slope \(s/t=(v(Q)+u(Q))/(2(u(Q)-1))\) reduces to infinity since \(r\mid t\). The two simple poles are \(O,A\). At the inert good prime \(r\), CM gives \(\#E(\mathbf F_r)=r+1\), so \([2]Q=O\) on reduction. As \(A\) has nonzero ordinate modulo \(r\), it follows that \(Q\) reduces to \(O\). Its slope has valuation equal to minus the formal depth there, and doubling preserves that depth. Consequently \(v_r(t)\) is the square-root abscissa denominator exponent of \([r+1]A\) at \(r\). ## A rational Ward congruence at 3

For every positive odd \(n\) with \(3\nmid n\), one also has
\[
                        nB_n\equiv(-1)^{(n-1)/2}\pmod{27}.
\]
The sequence \(\psi_n(R)\bmod27\) has period 27. The verifier proves this by testing the two division-polynomial recurrences on the cyclic array. At odd indices \(B_n=(-1)^{(n-1)/2}2^{-5(n^2-1)/4}\psi_n(R)\). This follows at odd places by good reduction, at 2 by homogeneity with scale \(2^{5/4}\) and the initial even terms of positive valuation, and at the real place by the division-polynomial sign on the bounded oval, as in the denominator calculation. The scale-to-the-fourth is 32 and for \(R=(-8,16)\) the initial four scaled valuations are \(0,5/4,0,9/4\). Substitution on \(n\bmod216\) proves the congruence.
