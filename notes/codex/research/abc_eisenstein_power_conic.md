# A coefficient-three conic and an equivalent restricted abc inequality

Fix a positive multiple \(m\) of six and a positive integer \(L\). Rational abc is equivalent to abc restricted to primitive positive triples of the form
\[
                     U^2+3V^2=W^m,\qquad L\mid V .
\]
This restriction allows arbitrary \(W\). Moreover, in the same family one can construct infinitely many triples with \(W\) supported on small primes, prescribed prime powers in \(V\), and an explicit sublinear radical saving, as described below. Equivalence is asserted for the first restriction, without the additional smoothness condition.

## Transferring an arbitrary primitive triple

First suppose \(L=1\), and start from \(a+b=c\) with \(a,b>0\), \((a,b)=1\), \(R=\operatorname{rad}(abc)\). Take \(Y\) to be the unique even entry and \(X\) an odd entry coprime to 3. Such an odd entry exists by pairwise coprimality. The third entry is \(X+Y\) or \(|X-Y|\). Set \(h=\max(X,Y)\), so \(c/2\le h\le c\). Exclude the single input \((1,1,2)\) if necessary. Put
\[
       P_m+Q_m\sqrt{-3}=(X+Y\sqrt{-3})^m,\qquad W=X^2+3Y^2 .
\]
The norm gives \(P_m^2+3Q_m^2=W^m\), with \(W\) odd and coprime to 3. The two ideals generated by \(X\pm Y\sqrt{-3}\) in the Eisenstein integers are coprime. A common prime ideal would divide \(2X,2Y\sqrt{-3}\); its residue characteristic cannot be 2 or 3 by \(W\), and any other residue characteristic would divide both \(X,Y\). No rational prime thus divides both \(P_m,Q_m\). Also \(3\nmid P_m\). Both coordinates are nonzero: if either vanished then the powered principal ideal would equal its conjugate, contrary to coprimality since \(W>1\). Therefore \(U=|P_m|\), \(V=|Q_m|\) give a primitive positive triple.

The coefficient \(Q_m(X,Y)\) is divisible *as an integer polynomial* by \(XY(X-Y)(X+Y)\). It vanishes on both coordinate axes for even \(m\), and \((1\pm\sqrt{-3})^3=-8\) gives vanishing on both diagonals for \(3\mid m\). All four linear polynomials are distinct primitive factors. In particular the product of the three linear forms giving the input entries divides \(Q_m\), and the complementary homogeneous factor has degree \(m-3\). This gives
\[
  \operatorname{rad}(V)\ll_m R h^{m-3},\qquad
  \operatorname{rad}(3U V W)\ll_m R h^{2m-1},\qquad
  \frac{W^m}{\operatorname{rad}(3U V W)}\gg_m\frac{c}{R}.
\]
Here \(|P_m|\le W^{m/2}\ll_m h^m\) and \(h^2\le W\le4h^2\). Thus an infinite input sequence with \(\log(c/R)\ge\delta\log c\) would give a fixed positive exponent in the displayed family, because \(\log(W^m)=2m\log c+O_m(1)\).

To handle any fixed \(L\), choose \(r\ge1\) divisible by \(\varphi(L)\) and at least the largest exponent occurring in \(L\), taking \(r=1\) for \(L=1\). First replace the input by
\[
              a^r+(c^r-a^r)=c^r .
\]
Its prime radical is at most \(R\, r c^{r-1}\), since \(c-a=b\) and \((c^r-a^r)/(c-a)\le r c^{r-1}\). Every \(p^j\|L\) divides one of these three entries: use the power \(r\) if \(p\mid ac\), and Euler's theorem modulo \(p^j\) otherwise. Consequently \(L\) divides their product. The coefficient \(Q_m\) in the subsequent construction is divisible by the entire product. All height exponents and constants still depend only on \(m,L\). This proves the claimed converse implication to general abc. The forward implication is immediate by restriction.

For instance \(1+2=3\) gives \((1+2\sqrt{-3})^6=253+1260\sqrt{-3}\), hence \(253^2+3\cdot1260^2=13^6\).

## Concentrating all primes of the largest entry in one congruence class

Fix any \(N=2^i3^j\) with \(i,j\ge1\). The equivalence above continues to hold after requiring **every prime divisor of \(W\) to be \(1\bmod N\)**. The parameters \(m,L,N\) are fixed before testing the restricted abc inequality. In particular the allowed class for the prime divisors of the largest entry can have arbitrarily small fixed natural density among primes.

Put \(k=N/6\), \(s=x^k,\ t=y^k\), where \(|x|,|y|\) are respectively an odd entry and the even entry of the input. Choose a sign of \(x\), if necessary, so that \(s\not\equiv-t\bmod 3\). This is automatic if \(k\) is even, and possible if it is odd, because \((x,y)=1\). Take
\[
 \omega=(-1+\sqrt{-3})/2,\qquad
 \alpha=s+\omega t,\qquad W=N_{\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-3})/\mathbf Q}(\alpha)=s^2-st+t^2.
\]
Since \(t\) is even, \(\alpha\) has integral coefficients in the basis \(1,\sqrt{-3}\). The positive definite norm is comparable to \(h^{2k}\) for \(h=\max(|x|,|y|)\), and is coprime to 6. Its factors \(\alpha,\bar\alpha\) are coprime as ideals: any common prime away from 3 divides their difference \(t\sqrt{-3}\) and hence also \(s\). Thus \(\alpha^m=U_0+V_0\sqrt{-3}\) again gives the primitive positive triple \(U^2+3V^2=W^m\) with \(U=|U_0|,\ V=|V_0|\).

In fact \(W=\Phi_N(x,y)\), a homogeneous cyclotomic value: the roots of \(\Phi_6(Z^k)\) are precisely the primitive \(N\)-th roots because the prime factors of \(k\) are 2 and 3. More directly, any \(p\mid W\) is prime to \(6xy\). The element \((x/y)^k\bmod p\) has order 6, since it satisfies \(\Phi_6\) and \(p\nmid6\). The order of \(x/y\bmod p\) must then be \(6k=N\), by considering its valuations at 2 and 3 and excluding every other prime factor. Thus \(p\equiv1\bmod N\).

For the height transfer, \(F_m(s,t)=2^m V_0\), viewed as a polynomial in \(s,t\), lies in \(\mathbf Z[s,t]\) and is divisible by \(st(s-t)(s+t)\). At \(t=0\) the parameter is rational, at \(s=0\) it is \(\omega t\), at \(s=t\) it is \((1+\omega)t\), and at \(s=-t\) it is \((\omega-1)t\). The sixth powers of all four directions are rational, as are their \(m\)-th powers. Therefore
\[
 F_m(x^k,y^k)=x^ky^k(x^{2k}-y^{2k})\,H_{m-4}(x^k,y^k)
\]
for a fixed integral homogeneous polynomial \(H_{m-4}\) of degree \(m-4\). The third input entry is \(|x+y|\) or \(|x-y|\), which divides \(x^{2k}-y^{2k}\) with quotient of size \(O_k(h^{2k-1})\). Hence
\[
 \operatorname{rad}(V)\ll_{m,k} R h^{km-2k-1},\qquad
 \operatorname{rad}(3U V W)\ll_{m,k}R h^{2km-1}.
\]
Here \(|U|\le W^{m/2}\ll_{m,k}h^{km}\). The raw output \(W^m\asymp h^{2km}\) thus preserves the original height-to-radical ratio up to a fixed multiplicative constant. The input product divides \(2^mV\); to impose \(L\mid V\) use the preceding preliminary power construction with \(2^mL\) in place of \(L\). This establishes the stronger equivalence. The same congruence class can also be imposed in the unconditional construction below.

There is a simultaneous restriction on the square entry. If \(p\mid U\), then \(p\) is odd, because \(m\) is even, \(V_0\) is even, and \(W\) is odd. Also \(p\nmid3W\). The projective ratio \(z=\alpha/\bar\alpha\) belongs modulo \(p\) to the norm-one group of order \(p-(-3/p)\). The equation \(U_0=0\bmod p\) gives \(z^m=-1\bmod p\), so its order has exact 2-adic valuation \(1+v_2(m)\). When \(p\equiv1\bmod3\) the group has order \(p-1\), and when \(p\equiv-1\bmod3\) it has order \(p+1\). Thus every prime divisor of \(U\) is \(1\) or \(-1\bmod 3\cdot2^{1+v_2(m)}\). This proof applies also to the primitive triples in the following section. In particular, rational abc is equivalent to its restriction to \(U^2+3V^2=W^{12}\) with \(24\mid V\), all primes in \(W\) equal to \(1\bmod24\), and all primes in \(U\) equal to \(\pm1\bmod24\).

For an example with these moduli, \(x=1,y=2,k=4\) gives \(W=241\) and \(\alpha=-7+8\sqrt{-3}\). Using \(m=12\) gives
\[
      153602028347041^2+3\cdot70226335539360^2=241^{12}.
\]
The prime factors of the first square root are \(23,47,73,17183,113279\); their residue classes modulo 24 are \(-1,-1,1,-1,-1\).

For these particular moduli no preliminary power is needed. One has \(8\mid y^4/2\), so the coefficient of \(\sqrt{-3}\) in \(\alpha^{12}\) is divisible by 8. It is also divisible by 3, since the coefficient in \((b+a\sqrt{-3})^3\) is \(3a(b^2-a^2)\). Consequently the direct transfer has \(\log(W^{12})=96\log c+O(1)\) and preserves \(\log(c/R)\) up to a fixed additive loss.

The restriction on the prime factors of \(U\) also follows from the equation itself for every primitive solution with \(6\mid m\). An even \(U\) with odd \(V\) gives \(W^m=3\bmod4\), and odd \(U,V\) give \(v_2(W^m)=2\). Both are impossible. Thus \(U,W\) are odd and \(V\) is even. Primitivity gives \(3\nmid UW\). The ideals generated by \(U\pm V\sqrt{-3}\) are coprime, and their product is the \(m\)-th power of \((W)\). As the Eisenstein integers are principal, \(U+V\sqrt{-3}=\zeta\beta^m\) for a unit \(\zeta\) and an integral \(\beta\). Modulo \(2\mathcal O\) the left side is 1, as is \(\beta^m\) since the residue group has order three. Among the six units only \(\pm1\) reduce to 1, so \(\zeta=\bar\zeta\). The preceding order argument therefore applies to \(\beta/\bar\beta\) at every \(p\mid U\).

## Prescribing many primes on this same family

Let \(T(C)=\sqrt{\log C}/\log\log C\), where now \(C=W^m\). For each fixed \(m,N\) as above, every \(0<c_0<2(\pi/e)^{1/4}/\sqrt{m\varphi(N)}\) and \(0<\rho<1/2\), there are infinitely many primitive triples with all primes dividing \(W\) congruent to 1 modulo \(N\), satisfying
\[
 \log\frac{C}{\operatorname{rad}(3U V W)}\ge c_0T(C),\qquad
 p^{\lfloor\rho\log\log C\rfloor}\mid V
          \quad\text{for every prime }p\le c_0T(C).
\]
In particular any fixed \(L\) divides \(V\) eventually. Every prime divisor of \(W\) is \(O_{m,N}(\log C)\).

Here are the modifications to the [growing-rank lattice proof](abc_square_conic_growing_primes.md). For rank \(d\), take \(D=\prod_{p\le d}p\) and the first \(d\) primes \(\ell_i>d\) congruent to 1 modulo \(N\). They split in \(\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-3})\) because \(3\mid N\). Its maximal order is norm Euclidean. Choose generators \(\eta_i\) of the selected prime ideals that belong to \(\mathbf Z[\sqrt{-3}]\): modulo 2 the maximal order has residue field \(\mathbf F_4\), and the sixth roots of unity in the maximal order cover all three nonzero classes, so a generator can be taken to be 1 modulo \(2\mathcal O\). Their complex absolute values are exactly \(\sqrt{\ell_i}\). Use weights \(w_i=\log\ell_i\).

Write \(\kappa=\sqrt{e/\pi}\), \(H=\kappa\varphi(N)d^2(\log d)^2\), \(L_d=\sqrt{2d/\pi}\,\Gamma(2+d/2)^{1/d}\), \(U_d=H^d/(L_d^d\prod_i w_i)\), and \(E=\prod_{p\mid D}(1+1/p)\). Choose \(k=\lfloor\log(U_d/(4E))/\log D\rfloor\) and the largest \(t\ge0\) with \(M=D^k3^t\le U_d/(4E)\). The projective congruence kernel modulo \(2M\) has index at most \(2ME\): at every unramified \(p\) the local order is \(p^{j-1}(p-(-3/p))\), using the inert order \(3\cdot2^{j-1}\) at 2, and at 3 the ramified order is \(3^j\). The packing estimate in the cited proof gives a nonzero vector \(z\) in this kernel with \(\sum_i |z_i| w_i\le H\). Replace negative powers by conjugate positive powers and call the resulting integral parameter \(\alpha=x+y\sqrt{-3}\). Projective scalarity modulo \(2M\) implies \(M\mid y\) by expressing \(\sqrt{-3}\) in the integral basis. As only one prime ideal over each used \(\ell_i\) divides \(\alpha\), its ideal is coprime to its conjugate, and \(W=N(\alpha)=\prod_i\ell_i^{|z_i|}\) is odd and prime to 3.

Use \(P+Q\sqrt{-3}=\alpha^m\), \(U=|P|,V=|Q|\). These again give a primitive positive triple, \(M\mid Q\), \(\log C\le mH\), and \(2\sqrt3|P Q|\le C\). Hence
\[
    \frac{C}{\operatorname{rad}(3U V W)}
                \ge\frac{2}{\sqrt3}\frac{M}{D\prod_i\ell_i}.
\]
The prime number theorem in the class 1 modulo \(N\) gives \(\sum_i\log\ell_i=d(\log d+\log\log d+\log\varphi(N)-1)+o(d)\), by the same integration and removal of primes at most \(d\) as in the original proof. The factor \(\varphi(N)\) in \(H\) compensates for the changed prime density. In particular \(\log(M/(D\prod_i\ell_i))=(1+o(1))d\), \(k=(1+o(1))\log d\), and \(T(C)\le(\sqrt{m\kappa\varphi(N)}/2+o(1))d\). Moreover \(y\ne0\), so \(W\ge3M^2\). Since \(\max\ell_i=(\varphi(N)+o(1))d\log d\) and \(\log M=(1+o(1))d\log d\), the smoothness assertion follows. The guaranteed radical gain is \(o(\log C)\). These estimates leave the fixed-exponent inequality on the restricted family open.
