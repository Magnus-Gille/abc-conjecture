# Prime census for the marked factor \(2X_n+Z_n\)

On \(E:v^2=u^3-8u^2+8u\), put \(A=(1,1)\), write \(u(nA)=X_n/Z_n\) in lowest terms with \(Z_n>0\), and set \(F_n=2X_n+Z_n\). Each certificate row holds the index, proven prime factors with exact multiplicities, and residual composite cofactors. Their product recovers \(F_n\). Odd \(n>1\) gives the projective-slope factorization
\[
F_n=((s+t)^2+t^2)(3s^2-8st+6t^2).
\]
For even \(n=2m\), write \(a/b=u(mA)\). The duplication formula gives
\[
2(a^2-8b^2)^2+4ab(a^2-8ab+8b^2)
=2(a-2b)(a-4b)(a^2+8ab+8b^2).
\]
Taking gcds against the reduced \(F_n\) handles normalization. Further gcds come from the homogeneous abscissa differences of \([u]A\) and \([v\tau]A\) at representations \(u^2+2v^2=d\mid n^2+2,\ u\equiv\pm nv\bmod d\). The verifier checks the factor products directly.

## Exact inherited increments

Let \(p>7\) divide \(F_n\), let \(r\) be the order of \(A\bmod p\), and write \(d_p\) for the formal depth of \([r]A\). Such \(p\) splits in \(\mathbb Q(\tau)\), with \(\tau^2=-2\). The endomorphism with tangent multiplier \(\tau\) has \([\tau]A=(-1/2,-7\tau/4)\). Select the place using the square root
\[
\lambda=\tau\in\mathbb Z_p,\qquad \lambda\equiv-4v(nA)/7\bmod p.
\]
The ordinate at the target is a unit, so \([n-\tau]A\) has formal depth \(e=v_p(F_n)\) there. Applying \([r]\) to this formal point adds \(v_p(r)\) to its depth. Applying \([n-\tau]\) to the formal point \([r]A\) adds \(v_p(n-\lambda)\), by the isometric formal logarithm. Commutation gives
\[
e=d_p-v_p(r)+v_p(n-\lambda).
\]
For \(p>7\) the group order is even and strictly below \(2p\), so \(v_p(r)=0\). The roots \(\pm\lambda\bmod p\) are distinct. Consequently
\[
e-d_p=
\begin{cases}
v_p(n^2+2),&p\mid n^2+2\ \text{and}\ 7n+4v(nA)\equiv0\bmod p,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
For a fixed prime with a marked residue the indices with increment at least \(k\ge1\) lie in exactly two classes modulo \(rp^k\). The signed target determines one class modulo \(r\), and the matching root \(\lambda\) determines one modulo \(p^k\). Changing sign gives the second class. Distinctness follows from the nonzero target ordinate. At \(p=19\) the first increment classes are \(\pm25\bmod209\).

Checking the prime divisors of \(n^2+2\) gives all inherited good-prime increments through \(n=100\), independently of the large composite cofactors. In the order \((n,p,r)\) they are
\[
(25,19,11),\ (27,43,17),\ (41,11,9),\ (44,17,6),\
(58,11,9),\ (58,17,6),\ (80,97,22).
\]
Each has \(d_p=1,\ e=2\). The verifier checks \(\psi_r(A)\bmod p^2\) and \(F_n\bmod p^3\) at each.

At 2, \(u(2A)=49/4\) puts \(2A\) in the formal group and odd multiples reduce to smooth \(A\bmod2\). Thus \(v_2(F_n)=1\) for even \(n\) and zero for odd \(n\). At 3 the rank is 3 and the depth is 2 by \(\psi_3(A)=-45\). For \(n\equiv\pm1\bmod3\) the ordinate is \(\pm1\), giving \(\lambda\equiv\mp1\bmod3\). The general depth identity gives \(v_3(F_n)=1\) on these two classes, and \(3\mid n\) puts \(nA\) at the identity on reduction, giving \(v_3(F_n)=0\). The ordinate square \(-49/8\) is a nonsquare modulo 5. At 7 the marked point has abscissa 3 and ordinate zero, whereas \(2A\bmod7=(0,0)\) is the only nonzero two-torsion point of the subgroup generated by \(A\). Hence 5 and 7 never divide \(F_n\).
