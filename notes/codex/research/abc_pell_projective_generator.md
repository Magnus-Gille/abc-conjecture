# Exact lifting at a moving prime with a small auxiliary Pell norm

Write \(\mathcal O=\mathbb Z[\sqrt2]\), \(\varepsilon=1+\sqrt2\), and associate \(\alpha=b+a\sqrt2\) to the parameter \([a:b]\). Its norm is \(J(a,b)=b^2-2a^2\). Let \(F(a,b)\) be a fixed primitive separable integral binary form coprime over \(\mathbb Q\) to \(J\). Exclude 2, the primes of the discriminant of \(F\) and of the resultant of \(F,J\), and any primes fixed by the chosen normalization. Hence every projective root of \(F\bmod p\) is simple and has unit norm. The definite full rank-eleven form of degree 100 satisfies these assumptions. [Construction and proof of separability](abc_cm_twist_sharp_depth.md)

For every sufficiently large remaining \(p\), there is an element \(\eta\) such that

* either \(\eta=\varepsilon\), or \(|N\eta|=\ell\) for a split rational prime \(\ell\le100(\log p)^2\);
* its projective order \(m\) modulo \(p\) divides \(p-(2/p)\), and \(\eta^m\) is nonscalar modulo \(p^2\);
* the absolute values of both real embeddings of \(\eta\) are at most \(\varepsilon\sqrt{|N\eta|}\).

Here “scalar” means an element of \((\mathbb Z/p^s)^\times\) inside \((\mathcal O/p^s)^\times\). If \(\alpha_0\) is any integral representative of a root of \(F\bmod p\), primitive at \(p\), then for **each** \(e\ge2\) there is \(0\le j<2p^{e-1}\) such that the primitive coefficient pair of \(\alpha_0\eta^{mj}\) satisfies \(v_p(F(a,b))=e\).

## Finding a nontrivial projective lift

Put \(G_s=(\mathcal O/p^s)^\times/(\mathbb Z/p^s)^\times\). The ring modulo \(p\) is split or inert according to \((2/p)\), so \(G_1\) is cyclic of size \(p-(2/p)\). The reduction \(G_s\to G_1\) is surjective with kernel of size \(p^{s-1}\), as follows by counting units in these unramified rings. Thus the elements of \(G_2\) of order prime to \(p\) form a subgroup of size \(p-(2/p)\le p+1\). If \(\varepsilon\) is outside it, choose \(\eta=\varepsilon\).

Otherwise put \(L=\log p\), \(Y=100L^2\) and take the \(n\) odd split primes \(\ell\le Y\). The prime number theorem for the classes \(1,7\bmod8\) gives \(n\sim Y/(2\log Y)\). Each such prime has a prime ideal generator \(\eta_\ell\) of absolute norm \(\ell\). Indeed \(\mathbb Z[\sqrt2]\) is norm Euclidean: rounding both coefficients to the nearest integers gives a remainder ratio of absolute norm at most \(1/2\). Multiplication by a suitable integral power of the unit \(\varepsilon\) balances any nonzero element \(\gamma\), placing its first absolute real embedding in \([\sqrt{|N\gamma|},\varepsilon\sqrt{|N\gamma|})\) and its second at most \(\sqrt{|N\gamma|}\).

Suppose every chosen generator lies in the prime-to-\(p\) subgroup of \(G_2\). Take products from subsets of exactly \(r=\lfloor(2L-\log100)/\log Y\rfloor\) primes and balance each product by a power of \(\varepsilon\). Their projective classes still belong to the subgroup under this supposition. Each has absolute norm at most \(p^2/100\). Its coefficients are bounded by \(2\sqrt{|N\gamma|}\), by adding and subtracting the two real embeddings. Consequently the determinant of two coefficient vectors has absolute value at most \(8p^2/100<p^2\). It cannot vanish for different subsets: rational proportionality makes the ratio of their distinct squarefree absolute norms a rational square. A shared projective class modulo \(p^2\) would make the nonzero determinant divisible by \(p^2\).

These subsets outnumber \(p+1\). With \(w=\log L\), \(r=L/(w+\log10)+O(1)\), \(n/r=(25+o(1))L\), and \(r/n\to0\). Stirling's estimate gives
\[
  \log\binom nr
    =r[\log(n/r)+1+o(1)]
    =L+\left(1+\log(5/2)+o(1)\right)L/w.
\]
This contradicts the size of the subgroup. Choose a generator outside it and balance it; multiplication by \(\varepsilon\) preserves nonmembership in this case. Its projective order \(m\) modulo \(p\) is prime to \(p\), so \(\eta^m\) belongs to the reduction kernel and is nontrivial modulo \(p^2\). All the asserted size bounds follow.

## Exact valuation and primitivity

The kernel of \(G_s\to G_1\) is generated by the class of \(\eta^m\) for every \(s\ge2\). After dividing by its scalar coefficient over \(\mathbb Z_p\), this element is \(1+t\sqrt2\) with \(v_p(t)=1\). If \(v_p(t)=d\ge1\), the coefficient of \(\sqrt2\) in \((1+t\sqrt2)^p\) has valuation \(d+1\) by the binomial expansion, and its scalar coefficient is a unit. Thus its successive \(p\)-powers have projective depths \(1,2,\ldots\), proving that its order modulo \(p^s\) is \(p^{s-1}\).

The projective parameter classes of unit norm over \(\mathbb Z/p^s\) are in bijection with \(G_s\): two unit coefficient vectors give the same parameter precisely when they differ by an invertible rational scalar modulo \(p^s\). Multiplication by \(\alpha_0\) therefore identifies the full reduction kernel with the lifts of its parameter class. In a local affine chart, the simple root has a unique lift solving \(F=0\bmod p^s\) for every \(s\), by Hensel's lemma. Homogeneity preserves divisibility under invertible scalars. At \(s=e\) choose \(0\le j_0<p^{e-1}\) hitting that lift. The classes of \(j_0\) and \(j_0+p^{e-1}\) differ modulo \(p^{e+1}\), so at most one hits the unique root at this additional precision. Choose the other. This proves the asserted exact valuation, including if the starting root lies in the chart with \(b=0\bmod p\).

The resulting coefficients may be divided by their ordinary gcd. This gcd is prime to \(p\), since \(p\nmid N(\alpha_0\eta^{mj})\), and division cannot enlarge the support of the integral norm. When \(\alpha_0\) is a unit, the pair is already primitive: in the nonunit case \((\eta)\) is supported on exactly one of the two ideals over \(\ell\), whereas a common coefficient factor would require both. In general
\[
 \operatorname{rad}|J(a,b)|\le\operatorname{rad}|N\alpha_0|\,
                              \max(1,|N\eta|).
\]

## Starting representatives and the height of the triple

If the root occurs at a power of \(\varepsilon\), choose \(\alpha_0=\varepsilon^n\) with \(0\le n<p+2\), reducing the original exponent modulo the projective order of \(\varepsilon\bmod p\). Thus \(|N\alpha_0|=1\) and its log coefficient height is \(O(p)\). Infinitely many different primes occur in this way for every definite positive-degree \(F\) as above. Otherwise let \(S\) be the finite set of all prime divisors of \(F(a_n,b_n)\) for \(\varepsilon^n=b_n+a_n\sqrt2\), and set \(M=\prod_{q\in S}q^{1+v_q(F(0,1))}\). Taking \(n\) through positive multiples of the order of \(\varepsilon\bmod M\) fixes all valuations at \(S\) at their values in \(F(0,1)\ne0\). Definiteness makes \(|F(a_n,b_n)|\) unbounded on that progression, a contradiction.

Alternatively take **any** \(p\) for which \(F\bmod p\) has a projective root. The integral pairs reducing to that line form a determinant-\(p\) lattice. Applying Minkowski to the square \(|a|,|b|\le2\sqrt p\) gives a nonzero pair in this box; for \(p>4\) it cannot reduce to the zero vector. Divide by its coefficient gcd to obtain \(\alpha_0\) with \(|N\alpha_0|\le12p\), prime to \(p\). Consequently this choice costs at most \(12p\) extra in the radical of the norm.

In both cases \(\log\max(2,|a|,|b|)\ll p^e\log\log p\) after lifting. Indeed \(m\le p+1\), \(j<2p^{e-1}\), the two embeddings of \(\eta\) are \(O(\log p)\), and coefficient gcd removal only decreases height. The constants are independent of \(e\ge2\).

For any fixed family of primitive triples having \(\log c\ll 1+\log\max(|a|,|b|)\) and
\[
 \frac{c}{\operatorname{rad}(abc)}
       \gg\frac{|F(a,b)|}{\operatorname{rad}|F(a,b)|\,\operatorname{rad}|J(a,b)|},
\]
the Pell starting points therefore certify a gain \(\gg p^{e-1}/(\log p)^2\) for infinitely many moving primes. The determinant-\(p\) starting points certify \(\gg p^{e-2}/(\log p)^2\) for every sufficiently large compatible prime, in particular at depth \(e=3\). Both statements use exact exponent \(e\) in \(F\). All estimates here concern this specified gain; they do not imply a fixed power of the triple height.

## A check of both projective orbits

At \(p=683\), multiplication by \(1+\sqrt2\) sends \((b,a)\) to \((b+2a,b+a)\). Its projective order is \(228\), and the ratio of the \(\sqrt2\)-coefficient of \(\varepsilon^{228}\) to \(683\) times its scalar coefficient is \(624\bmod683\). Thus the first projective lift is nontrivial. The Pell index \(9\) is a root modulo \(683\). At indices \(9+228j\), the two values \(j=370,1053\) both lift that root to precision two; both have exactly that valuation. Evaluating the degree-50 abscissa factor \(G_{11}(-8H/S)\), the quotients by \(683^2\) are \(460,428\bmod683\), respectively.

The root \([a:b]=[-3:1]\) is outside the projective orbit of \(\varepsilon\bmod683\), as enumeration through the 228 residues verifies. It has \(J=-17\). Starting instead from \((1-3\sqrt2)\varepsilon^{228j}\), the root lifts to depth two at \(j=46\bmod683\), and to depth three at \(j=377745\bmod683^2\). At \(j=377745\) and \(377745+683^2\) the abscissa-factor quotients by \(683^3\) are \(354\) and \(307\bmod683\); both valuations are exactly three. These computations require only binary powering of coefficient pairs modulo \(683^4\) and evaluation of the existing division polynomial. Its homogeneous pullback differs by a unit at these roots.
