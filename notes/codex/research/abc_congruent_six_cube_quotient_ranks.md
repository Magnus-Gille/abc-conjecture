# Exact rank on the primitive surface for the cube condition

Put \(k=\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-3})\), \(\epsilon=(1+\sqrt{-3})/2\), and \(\gamma=s+t\sqrt{-3}\). The curve for the chosen unit branch is \(a^2=\operatorname{Tr}_{k/\mathbf Q}(\epsilon\gamma^{12})\). Its seed is \((s,t,a)=(1,0,1)\). Use \(z=\gamma/\bar\gamma\) and \(h=a/N(\gamma)^3\), which satisfy \(h^2=\epsilon z^6+\bar\epsilon z^{-6}\). Projective scaling allows rational \(s,t\) in these formulas.

## A rational genus-three quotient of rank three

Write \(u+t_1\sqrt{-3}=\gamma^2\) and \(H_1=aN(\gamma)\). There is a degree-two map to
\[
  \mathcal C:\quad H_1^2=(u^2+3t_1^2)\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(\epsilon(u+t_1\sqrt{-3})^6\bigr).
\]
The two factors have disjoint simple roots, so this is a smooth genus-three hyperelliptic curve after projective completion. Over \(k\), write \(v=(u+t_1\sqrt{-3})/(u-t_1\sqrt{-3})\). Then \(h^2=\epsilon v^3+\bar\epsilon v^{-3}\) and \(Y_1=h v^2\) gives \(Y_1^2=\epsilon v^7+\bar\epsilon v\). Here \(h=H_1/(u^2+3t_1^2)^2\) is a rational function over \(\mathbf Q\).

Let \(H=\mathbf Q(\zeta_9)^+\), \(\theta=\zeta_9^2\), so \(\theta^3=-\epsilon\) in the complex embedding with \(\sqrt{-3}=i\sqrt3\). The formulas
\[
 W=-(\theta v+\theta^{-1}v^{-1}),\qquad Z=h,\qquad
 E_*:\quad Z^2=W^3-3W
\]
give a map over \(H\): complex conjugation fixes \(W\), and \(W^3-3W=-(\theta^3v^3+\theta^{-3}v^{-3})=h^2\). The three conjugate points over the cyclic cubic lie on a horizontal line, so their elliptic sum is zero. Thus the restriction-of-scalars map lands in the trace-zero surface
\(A=\ker(\operatorname{Res}_{H/\mathbf Q}E_*\longrightarrow E_*)\).
Over the algebraic closure its three differentials are proportional to
\[
       (-\theta^\sigma v^2+(\theta^\sigma)^{-1})\,dv/Y_1.
\]
They span the two dimensions generated by \(dv/Y_1\) and \(v^2dv/Y_1\).

There is also a rational elliptic factor \(E_6:y^2=x^3-36x\). Set \(b=(\epsilon v^3-\bar\epsilon v^{-3})/\sqrt{-3}\). Then \(h^4+3b^2=4\), and \((x,y)=(-3h^2,9hb)\) lies on \(E_6\). Its differential is a nonzero constant times \(v\,dv/Y_1\). Independence proves
\[
          \operatorname{Jac}(\mathcal C)\sim_{\mathbf Q} E_6\times A.
\]

### Rank certificate for \(E_*(H)\)

Let \(T^3-3T-1=0\), with \(H=\mathbf Q(T)\), \(T'=2-T^2\) a conjugate, and \(\pi=T-1\). The ring of integers is \(\mathbf Z[T]\), via the standard real cyclotomic generator and discriminant 81. Minkowski's bound is 2, and 2 is inert, giving class number one. The prime above 3 is generated by \(\pi\) of norm 3. The units \(-1,T,T'\) have independent signatures on the three real embeddings, hence generate all unit squareclasses.

For the two-isogeny descent on \(Z^2=W^3+BW\) with \(B=-3,12\), the classes in the abscissa images are supported on primes dividing \(B\), by valuation parity. On \(B=-3\) there are at most 16, from the units and \(\pi\). On \(B=12\) all abscissas are totally positive, including the torsion squareclass of 12. Since signatures distinguish unit squareclasses, the four possible classes are exactly the candidates \(1,2,3,6\), distinguished by their dyadic and 3-adic parities. The classes 2 and 6 require
\[
  N^2=2(U^4+3V^4)\quad\hbox{or}\quad N^2=2(3U^4+V^4).
\]
Normalize \(U,V\) to be primitive locally at the inert dyadic place. Unless \(U/V\equiv1\bmod2\) at two units, the right side has valuation 1, since fourth powering is injective in the residue field \(\mathbf F_8\). Otherwise \((U/V)^4\equiv1\bmod8\) and the valuation is 3. Thus the dual image has size exactly two. The rank formula gives \(\operatorname{rank}E_*(H)\le3\).

Over \(\mathbf Q\), the same dual calculation gives image \(\{1,3\}\). In the original image \(-1\) is excluded by reducing \(N^2=-U^4+3V^4\) at 3, leaving \(\{1,-3\}\). Hence \(\operatorname{rank}E_*(\mathbf Q)=0\). The rational representation of the cubic Galois group on \(E_*(H)\otimes\mathbf Q\) has no trivial summand and therefore has even dimension. Consequently its rank is at most two. Conversely the seed \(P=(T,1)\) is nontorsion. At the split primes 17 and 19, direct finite field counts give 26 and 20 points, so torsion divides 2, and \(P\) has nonzero ordinate. Its conjugates span the nontrivial rational representation of dimension two. This proves
\[
      \operatorname{rank}A(\mathbf Q)=\operatorname{rank}E_*(H)=2,\qquad
      \operatorname{rank}\operatorname{Jac}(\mathcal C)(\mathbf Q)=3.
\]
The last equality uses the certified rational rank one of \(E_6\). [Rank of \(E_6\)](abc_full_quartic_signature.md)

The rational-ordinate test on \(E_*(H)\) loses one expected independent condition. A trace-zero point and its conjugates are collinear, so their line is rational and a horizontal slope is its sole additional line condition. In contrast, the rational genus-three model has Néron–Severi rank at least two from the two factors of distinct dimensions. It satisfies the quadratic Chabauty finiteness criterion \(3<3+\operatorname{rank}\mathrm{NS}-1\) at good odd primes. This note does not compute the rational points of this genus-three quotient. A different real-cubic quartic cover determines the original cube condition. [Cube classification](abc_full_quartic_cube_classification.md)

## Rank obstruction over \(\mathbf Q(\sqrt3)\)

Write \(\gamma^6=A_0+B_0\sqrt{-3}\). The rational branch polynomial factors as
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}(\epsilon\gamma^{12})
 =\big(A_0-(3+2\sqrt3)B_0\big)\big(A_0-(3-2\sqrt3)B_0\big).
\]
On the first factor's squareclass containing the seed, consider \(C_+:y^2=A_0-(3+2\sqrt3)B_0\) over \(K=\mathbf Q(\sqrt3)\). Let \(L=\mathbf Q(i,\sqrt3)\), \(e=2+\sqrt3\), \(\alpha=(1+ie)/2\). Then \(\alpha\bar\alpha=e\), and the sextic equals \(\alpha\gamma^6+\bar\alpha\bar\gamma^6\). Over \(L\) define
\[
 U=\alpha(\gamma/\bar\gamma)^2,\qquad V=\alpha y/\bar\gamma^3,\qquad
 E_D: V^2=U^3+D,\quad D=e\alpha .
\]
The conjugate map over \(K\) is independent: with \(x=\gamma/\bar\gamma\) and \(y_1=y/\bar\gamma^3\), its differential is proportional to \(dx/y_1\), whereas the displayed map has differential proportional to \(x\,dx/y_1\). Thus \(\operatorname{Jac}(C_+)\sim_K\operatorname{Res}_{L/K}E_D\).

Let \(\sigma\) fix \(\sqrt{-3}\) and negate \(i,\sqrt3\), and set \(\kappa=(1+\sqrt3)/(1+i)\). Then \(\kappa^2=-ie,\ \kappa\sigma(\kappa)=-1,\ D^\sigma/D=\kappa^{-6}\). Hence
\[
 J(P)=(\kappa^2\sigma(U),\kappa^3\sigma(V))
\]
is an endomorphism of the group \(E_D(L)\) with \(J^2=[-1]\). It commutes with the cubic-root CM endomorphism, since \(\sigma\) fixes \(\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-3})\). Consequently \(E_D(L)\otimes\mathbf Q\) is a module over the degree-four field \(\mathbf Q(i,\sqrt{-3})\) whenever its rank is positive. The seed \((U,V)=(\alpha,\alpha)\) is nontorsion. At the split primes 13 and 37 choose \((i,\sqrt3)=(5,4),(6,15)\), respectively. The two reductions have coefficients \(D=2,6\) and have orders 19 and 28, respectively. Their coprimality gives trivial torsion over \(L\).

Thus the rank of \(\operatorname{Jac}(C_+)(K)\) is a positive multiple of four. In particular the seed cover over this real quadratic field already attains the dimension of its full restriction of scalars. This is a rank obstruction to applying the standard rank-deficient method on that cover, even if all other squareclasses can be discarded locally.
