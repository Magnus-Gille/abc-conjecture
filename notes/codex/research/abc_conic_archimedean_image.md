# A small conic leg and the size of the congruence image

Use \(h=\log(1+\sqrt2)\), \(T(C)=\sqrt{\log C}/\log\log C\), and \(\kappa=\sqrt{e/\pi}\). For every fixed \(0<\beta<1\) and every \(0<c<1/\sqrt\kappa\), there are infinitely many primitive triples \(u^2+2v^2=w^2=C\) with
\[
 \log\frac{C}{\operatorname{rad}(2uvw)}\ge cT(C),\qquad
 \log\frac{w}{v}\ge
       \left(\frac{\beta}{2\sqrt\kappa}-o(1)\right)\sqrt{\log C}.
\]
Each odd prime \(p\le cT(C)\) divides \(v\) to depth at least \(((1-\beta)/2-o(1))\log\log C\). The same primes divide the small difference \(w-u\) to at least twice that depth. Every prime divisor of \(u\) is split in \(\mathbf Q(\sqrt2)\) and is at most \((1/2+o(1))\log C\).

## Combining a real linear form with the finite congruences

Set \(y=d\), take the first \(d\) odd split primes \(\ell_i>y\) and generators \(\eta_i\) of one ideal above each as in the [growing-prime construction](./abc_square_conic_growing_primes.md). Write \(W_i=\log\ell_i\), \(\theta_i=\tfrac12\log|\eta_i/\bar\eta_i|\), and let \(D\) be the product of the odd primes up to \(y\). Choose the generators once with positive norm and both real embeddings of size \(O(\sqrt{\ell_i})\). A unit of norm \(-1\) permits the sign choice, and powers of \(\epsilon^2\) then balance the embeddings. Use the totally positive unit \(\epsilon^2=(1+\sqrt2)^2\) as a further independent generator. For \(M=D^k3^t\) the kernel \(\Lambda\) of the map from \(\mathbf Z^{d+1}\) into the projective units modulo \(M\) has determinant at most \(ME\), where \(E=\prod_{p\mid D}(1+1/p)\). The first integer coordinate \(z_0\) is the exponent of \(\epsilon^2\).

Define the following parameters:
\[
 H=2\kappa d^2(\log d)^2,\quad R=H/\sqrt d,\quad
 S=\beta d\log d,\quad \tau=e^{-S},\quad
 \mathcal G_n=\frac2\pi\Gamma(2+n/2)^{2/n}.
\]
On \(\mathbf R^{d+1}\) apply the linear map
\[
 (z_0,z)\longmapsto
 \left((W_i z_i)_{i=1}^d,\quad
       \frac{R}{\tau}\bigl(2h z_0+\sum_i\theta_i z_i\bigr)\right).
\]
Its absolute determinant is \(2h(R/\tau)\prod_iW_i\).
Blichfeldt's Hermite bound in dimension \(d+1\) gives a nonzero image lattice vector of Euclidean norm at most \(R\), provided that
\[
 M\le V_d:=\frac{\tau R^d}{2h E\,\mathcal G_{d+1}^{(d+1)/2}\prod_i W_i}.
\]
For a short proof, scale the Euclidean minimum to 2. The sum over lattice translates of \(\max(1-\|x\|^2/2,0)\) is at most 1 by the centroid identity for points separated by distance 2. Integrating over a fundamental cell gives determinant at least \(2^{n/2}\operatorname{vol}(B_2^n)\,2/(n+2)\), which rearranges to the stated bound.

Choose \(k=\lfloor\log(V_d/2)/\log D\rfloor\), then use \(t\ge0\) to put \(M=D^k3^t\) within a factor of 3 below \(V_d/2\). For large \(d\), \(k\ge1\). The prime number theorem and Stirling's formula give
\[
\begin{split}
 \log D &=d+o(d),\\
 \sum_i\log\ell_i&=d(\log d+\log\log d+\log2-1)+o(d),\\
 \sum_i\log W_i&=d\log\log d+o(d),\\
 \log M+S&=d(\log d+\log\log d+\log2+1)+o(d).
\end{split}
\]
For the last line use \(\mathcal G_{d+1}=(1+o(1))d/(\pi e)\), \(\log E=o(d)\), and \(\log\kappa=(1-\log\pi)/2\). Thus \(k=(1-\beta+o(1))\log d\).

The short vector has
\[
  \sum_i W_i|z_i|\le H,\qquad \left|2h z_0+\sum_i\theta_i z_i\right|\le \tau .
\]
The second bound excludes \(z=0\) for a nonzero vector. Form \(\alpha=b+a\sqrt2\) using \(\epsilon^{2z_0}\), the indicated powers of \(\eta_i\) when \(z_i>0\), and of \(\bar\eta_i\) when \(z_i<0\). The element is projectively scalar modulo \(M\), hence \(M\mid a\). The ideal supports imply that \(a,b\) are nonzero and coprime, and that its positive odd norm \(u=\prod_i\ell_i^{|z_i|}=b^2-2a^2\) satisfies \(\log u\le H\). In particular \(b\) is odd. Put \(v=2|ab|\), \(w=b^2+2a^2\). With \(L=\tfrac12\log|\alpha/\bar\alpha|\), one has
\[
 |L|\le\tau,\qquad
 w=u\cosh(2L),\qquad
 \frac vw=\frac{|\tanh(2L)|}{\sqrt2},\qquad w-u=4a^2.
\]
These identities give \(v/w\le\sqrt2e^{-S}\) and \(\log C\le2H+O(1)\). Also
\[
 \frac{C}{\operatorname{rad}(2uvw)}
 \gg \frac{w}{|ab|}\,\frac{M}{D\prod_i\ell_i}
 \gg e^S\,\frac{M}{D\prod_i\ell_i}.
\]
The lower bound has logarithm at least \((1+o(1))d\). Since \(T(C)\le(\sqrt\kappa+o(1))d\) and \(\log\log C\le(2+o(1))\log d\), this proves the radical and prime-power claims. The bound on \(\log C\) also gives \(S\ge(\beta/(2\sqrt{\kappa})-o(1))\sqrt{\log C}\). Finally positivity of the norm gives \(\sqrt2|a/b|=|\tanh L|\le e^{-S}\). Together with \(M\mid a\ne0\) this yields \(\log C\ge 4(\log M+S)-O(1)=(4+o(1))d\log d\). Since \(\max_i\ell_i=(2+o(1))d\log d\), all primes in \(u\) satisfy the claimed smoothness bound.

## A lower bound for the actual projective image

For the same \(d\) write \(I_M\) for the subgroup generated by the projective classes of the \(d\) split generators modulo any odd \(M=\prod_{p\le d}p^{e_p}\). Put \(D_M=\operatorname{rad}(M)\). Uniformly in all choices of exponents \(e_p\ge0\),
\[
                 \log |I_M|\ \ge\ \log M-\log D_M-o(d).
\]
The same estimate holds after adjoining the unit. In particular an arbitrarily large extra power at any individual prime is covered.

For these fixed balanced generators, the \(2d\) elements \(\eta_i,\bar\eta_i\) give distinct lines over \(\mathbf Q\), since rational proportionality would require their split-prime ideals to have equal differences of conjugate valuations. Their coefficient sizes are \(O(\sqrt{d\log d})\). For each \(p\mid M\) the projective group modulo \(p\) has at most \(p+1<2d\) elements. Thus two representatives \(x,y\) coincide projectively. Their nonzero integer cross determinant \(\Delta\) is divisible by \(p\) and has absolute value at most \(K d\log d\) for one absolute \(K\). The class of \(x/y\) belongs to the subgroup, since \(\bar\eta_i\eta_i\) is scalar. Its exact depth of being scalar in the local ring is \(s_p=v_p(\Delta)\); the denominator in the coefficient of \(\sqrt2\) of \(x/y\) is the \(p\)-unit \(N(y)\).

For \(e_p>s_p\), this class has order \(p^{e_p-s_p}\) in the projective units modulo \(p^{e_p}\). To see this, write a scalar multiple as \(1+p^{s_p}t\sqrt2\) locally with \(t\) a unit. Raising to the \(p\)-th power increases its scalar congruence depth by exactly one by the binomial theorem at odd \(p\). Thus the local image, and hence the global image order, is divisible by \(p^{\max(0,e_p-s_p)}\). These divisibilities multiply over distinct \(p\). If \(p>\sqrt{Kd\log d}\), then \(s_p=1\). For the remaining primes use \(s_p\log p\le\log(Kd\log d)\); even counting all integers in this small range gives a total \(o(d)\). Summing proves the estimate. It also shows the logarithm of the index inside the whole projective group is at most \(\log D_M+o(d)\).

At the chosen height \(H\asymp d^2(\log d)^2\) the weighted volume permits lattice determinants of logarithm \(O(d\log d)\). For a modulus with \(\log M\asymp H\), the actual image has logarithm \(\log M-O(d)\), so using this determinant in the same volume argument cannot force such a modulus. The possibility of special short relations does not follow from the determinant estimate.

### A small set of generators accounts for all deep local lifting

Some \(O(\log d)\) of the original \(d\) generators already suffice for the same lower bound, simultaneously for every choice of \(M\). For each odd \(p\le d\) let \(j_p=\lfloor\log(Kd\log d)/\log p\rfloor+1\), increasing \(K\) if necessary to make \(j_p\ge2\). All \(2d\) representatives are projectively distinct modulo \(p^{j_p}\) by the determinant bound. In that finite abelian group the subgroup of elements of order coprime to \(p\) has cardinality \(p-(2/p)\). It is preserved under conjugation. Consequently at most \((p-(2/p))/2\) of the \(d\) generators have order coprime to \(p\) at this modulus.

Choose \(L=\lceil3\log_2(2d)\rceil\) generators independently and uniformly with replacement. The probability of missing every generator of order divisible by \(p\) at one given \(p\) is at most \(((d+1)/(2d))^L\). The union bound over \(p\le d\) tends to zero. Fix a successful selection and discard repetitions. At each \(p\) one selected generator raised to \(p-(2/p)\) is scalar modulo \(p\), with scalar depth strictly below \(j_p\). Its order modulo \(p^e\) is therefore divisible by \(p^{\max(0,e-j_p+1)}\). For \(p>\sqrt{Kd\log d}\) one has \(j_p=2\) and this is the full \(p^{e-1}\). Summing the losses exactly as above proves the image bound with the selected subgroup. In particular at every \(p\) in that upper interval at least \(d-(p-(2/p))/2\) individual split generators exhibit ordinary projective lifting.

## Counting distinct triples up to every sufficiently large height

Fix \(a_0,b_0>0\) with \(a_0+b_0<2/\sqrt\kappa\), and \(0<\beta<1\). For all sufficiently large \(X\) the number of distinct primitive triples from the construction with \(C\le X\) is at least
\[
 \exp\!\left[\left(\frac{2}{\sqrt\kappa}-a_0-b_0-o(1)\right)T(X)\right]
\]
when the triples must simultaneously satisfy
\[
\begin{split}
 \log(C/\operatorname{rad}(2uvw))&\ge b_0T(X),\\
 \log(w/v)&\ge(\beta/(2\sqrt\kappa)-o(1))\sqrt{\log X},\\
 v_p(v)&\ge((1-\beta)/(2a_0\sqrt\kappa)-o(1))\log\log X
       \qquad(2<p\le a_0T(X)).
\end{split}
\]
The prime-power depth doubles in \(w-u\). The count distinguishes triples; different triples may have the same hypotenuse.

For a Euclidean lattice \(L\subset\mathbf R^n\), let \(m=\#(L\cap B_2(R))\) include the origin and both signs. The following form of the Blichfeldt bound counts vectors:
\[
        m\ \ge\ \frac{R^n}{\mathcal G_n^{n/2}\det L}.
\]
Indeed, the graph on lattice points joining distinct points at distance at most \(R\) has maximum degree \(m-1\). Color any finite set of its vertices with at most \(m\) colors by the greedy algorithm. For a given \(x\), color the points with positive bump \(\rho(x-v)=\max(1-2\|x-v\|^2/R^2,0)\). In each color class the points are separated by at least \(R\). The centroid identity gives the sum of the bumps within one class at most one, so \(\sum_{v\in L}\rho(x-v)\le m\). Integration over a fundamental cell gives the inequality since \(\int\rho=R^n/\mathcal G_n^{n/2}\).

Now take \(d=\lfloor T(X)/\sqrt\kappa\rfloor\), \(y=a_0 T(X)\), \(H=(\log X)/2-1\) and \(S=\beta d\log d\). Thus \(H=2\kappa d^2(\log d)^2(1+o(1))\). Write \(D\) for the product of all odd primes through \(y\). Choose \(M=D^k3^t\) within a factor of three above
\[
     M_* = K_0 D\left(\prod_i\ell_i\right)\exp(b_0 T(X)-S)
\]
for a sufficiently large absolute constant \(K_0\). This ensures the required radical gain for every nonzero short vector. Since \(\log M_*=(1-\beta+o(1))d\log d\) and \(\log D=(a_0\sqrt\kappa+o(1))d\), one has \(k=((1-\beta)/(a_0\sqrt\kappa)+o(1))\log d\). All prior prime-support estimates still hold at this fixed multiple \(y/d\).

Apply the counting bound in dimension \(d+1\) to the transformed congruence lattice. Its count is at least \(V_d/M\) with \(V_d\) as above for the current \(d,H,D,S\). Since
\[
 \log V_d+S=d(\log d+\log\log d+\log2+1)+o(d)
\]
and \(\sum_i\log\ell_i=d(\log d+\log\log d+\log2-1)+o(d)\), this gives
\[
 \log(V_d/M)=2d-(a_0+b_0)T(X)+o(d).
\]
Removing the origin costs one vector. Each nonzero vector yields a triple with \(C\le X\), as \(2H+2\log\cosh(2e^{-S})\le\log X\). Its real and finite bounds follow from the same identities as before. Each triple has at most four preimages: \(u,w\) determine \(a^2,b^2\), and the prime ideal valuations of a given \(\alpha=b+a\sqrt2\) determine all \(z_i\). The unit exponent \(z_0\) is then uniquely determined. This proves the count.
