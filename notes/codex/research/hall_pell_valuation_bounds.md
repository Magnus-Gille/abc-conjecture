# Which primes can account for a counterexample in the Pell family?

Let $F_n,L_n$ be the Fibonacci and Lucas numbers. For $n=7+12j$, $j\ge0$, put $t=L_n-11$, $z=F_n$, $A=t^2+10t+5$, $B=t^2+4t-1$, and $M_n=tzAB$. The primitive triple $1728t+5(zB)^2=A^3$ has radical $\operatorname{rad}(30M_n)$. Both $\log M_n$ and $\log(A^3)$ equal $6n\log\alpha+O(1)$ for $\alpha=(1+\sqrt5)/2$. Thus a family of counterexamples to abc for some fixed $\varepsilon>0$ exists on this progression exactly if $\limsup_{n\to\infty} E(n)/n>0$, where

$$E(n)=\log\frac{M_n}{\operatorname{rad}M_n}=\sum_p(v_p(M_n)-1)_+\log p,\qquad x_+=\max(x,0).$$

The $O(1)$ and all subsequent implied constants may depend on the fixed family. [Construction and primitivity](hall_pell_direction.md)

## A bound at every index

For every $n$ on the progression,

$$\sum_{p\le n^{1/3}} v_p(M_n)\log p\ \ll\ \frac{n}{\log(2n)}.$$

This includes arbitrary powers of every prime in the range. Each fixed finite set $S$ satisfies the stronger bound $\sum_{p\in S}v_p(M_n)\log p\ll_S\log(2n)$. In particular, if $31^e\mid A$ and $59^h\mid B$, then $e\log31+h\log59\ll\log(2n)$. Every prescribed pair of depths on two previously computed branches can occur, but its logarithmic contribution divided by $n$ tends to zero as $n$ grows. [The simultaneous congruences](hall_pell_simultaneous.md)

### The unit polynomial

Put $X=\alpha^n$. Since $n$ is odd, $L_n=X-X^{-1}$ and $\sqrt5 F_n=X+X^{-1}$. Direct multiplication gives $P(X)=\sqrt5\,X^6 M_n$, where

$$\begin{aligned}
P(U)={}&(U^2-11U-1)(U^2+1)\\
&\cdot(U^4-12U^3+14U^2+12U+1)\\
&\cdot(U^4-18U^3+74U^2+18U+1).
\end{aligned}$$

The four factors correspond respectively to $t,z,A,B$. They are monic with constant term $\pm1$, so every root $\beta$ is an algebraic unit. The discriminant of the product is $-2^{54}3^{26}5^{23}$. Also $P(\alpha^n)\ne0$ since the four integer factors are positive for $n\ge7$ on this progression.

### Quantitative input and its dependence on the prime

We use Theorem 1 of Kunrui Yu, *p-adic logarithmic forms and group varieties. III*, Forum Math. **19** (2007), 187–280. The following specialization records the dependence on $p$ needed here. The constants need no numerical evaluation. The explicit formulas can also be checked in Alvarado, Koutsianas, Malmskog, Rasmussen, Vincent and West and in their SageMath implementation, functions `Yu_C1_star`, `Omega_prime` and `Yu_modified_height`. [Alvarado et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.00977) · [Explicit formulas in SageMath](https://github.com/sagemath/sage/blob/develop/src/sage/rings/number_field/S_unit_solver.py#L462-L607)

Let $u,v$ be fixed algebraic units with $u$ of infinite order, in a fixed field $K$ containing $i$. Suppose $p\ge7$ and the chosen completion of $K$ at $p$ is unramified of residue degree $f\le2$. Normalize $v_p(p)=1$. For $u^n/v\ne1$ and $n\ge1$, Yu gives

$$v_p(u^n/v-1)\ \ll_{K,u,v}\ \frac{p^f}{(\log p)^2}\log(2n).$$

Take a fixed free basis of the subgroup generated by $u,v$ modulo torsion. Its rank $r$ is at least one. Use those $r$ independent generators together with a generator of the roots of unity of $K$ as the $m=r+1$ inputs to Yu's theorem. The exponents for $u^n/v$ are $O(n+1)$, including a bounded representative for torsion. The presence of $i$ supplies Yu's auxiliary hypothesis (1.15) for odd $p$. In the bound $C_1^*\Omega\log B_0$, take $B_0\ge3$ bounding the exponents with $\log B_0\ll\log(2n)$. Formula (1.23) gives $C_1^*\ll_K p^f/(\log p)^{m+1}$ here. Indeed the factor $(f\log p)^{-(m+2)}$ is multiplied by a maximum of size $O_K(\log p)$ and the remaining factors besides $p^f$ are bounded for fixed degree and $m$. The $r$ independent generators contribute their fixed ordinary heights to an upper bound for $\Omega$, while the single remaining modified height is $O_K(\log p)$ by (1.21). Thus $C_1^*\Omega\ll_{K,u,v}p^f/(\log p)^m$, with $m\ge2$. For a fixed $p$ and arbitrary completion, the same theorem gives $O_{K,u,v,p}(\log(2n))$ after adjoining the required roots of unity if necessary. At $p=2$ one can adjoin a primitive cube root of unity. Conversion between integral-place valuation and $v_p$ involves a fixed ramification index.

### Applying the bound

For $p\ge7$ embed $\alpha$ in the unramified quadratic extension of $\mathbb Q_p$, which contains $\sqrt5$ and $i$. If $p\mid M_n$, then $\alpha^n$ reduces to a simple root of $P$ there. Hensel's lemma gives the unique lifted root $\beta$ in that same local field. Every other root of $P$ has different reduction since $p$ does not divide the discriminant. All factors other than $X-\beta$ in the root factorization are therefore units at $X=\alpha^n$. The denominator $\sqrt5 X^6$ is a unit as well, so

$$v_p(M_n)=v_p(\alpha^n-\beta)=v_p(\alpha^n/\beta-1).$$

Apply the specialization in $K=\mathbb Q(\alpha,\beta,i)$. The chosen completion has $f\le2$, and only finitely many global fields and pairs of units arise as $p$ varies. For $p=2,3,5$ factor $P$ in a fixed splitting field and apply the fixed-place bound to every root. The denominator has fixed valuation. This proves the fixed-$S$ assertion and, for $Y\ge2$, yields

$$\sum_{p\le Y}v_p(M_n)\log p\ll \log(2n)\left(1+\frac{Y^3}{\log^2(2Y)}\right).$$

Here $\sum_{7\le p\le Y}p^2/\log p\ll Y^3/\log^2(2Y)$ follows by partial summation from Chebyshev's bound for the number of primes. Substitution of $Y=n^{1/3}$ proves the claimed estimate.

There is a larger range for a subset of the primes in $AB$. If $p\ge7$ divides $AB$, then $5$ is a square modulo $p$ by the discriminants $80$ and $20$ of the two quadratics in $t$. If also $p\equiv1\pmod4$, both $\alpha$ and $i$ lie in $\mathbb Q_p$ and the same root lifts in $\mathbb Q_p$. Now $f=1$. Using $\sum_{7\le p\le Y}p/\log p\ll Y^2/\log^2(2Y)$ gives

$$\sum_{p\le\sqrt n,\ p\equiv1\ (4)}v_p(AB)\log p\ll n/\log(2n).$$

## An elementary reduction for the Fibonacci and Lucas factors

Let $m_+=(n+5)/2$ and $m_-=(n-5)/2$. Since $m_-$ is odd, the Lucas product identity gives $t=L_{m_+}L_{m_-}$. For $p\ge7$, define $d_p=p-(5/p)$ using the Legendre symbol, and $w_p=v_p(F_{d_p})$. The exceptional condition $w_p\ge2$ is the Fibonacci-Wieferich or Wall–Sun–Sun condition.

To verify the valuation formula, put $g=\alpha/\bar\alpha$ with $\bar\alpha=(1-\sqrt5)/2$ and take its order $r$ in the residue field at $p$. Then $r\mid d_p$. For a split prime this is Fermat's theorem, and for an inert prime Frobenius sends $g$ to $g^{-1}$. Hence $p\nmid d_p$. Binet's formula and the binomial lifting formula for an odd unramified prime give, whenever $r\mid k$,

$$v_p(F_k)=v_p(g^r-1)+v_p(k)=w_p+v_p(k).$$

If $p\mid L_m$ then $g^m\equiv-1\pmod p$, so $p\nmid F_m$ and $v_p(L_m)=v_p(F_{2m})$. A prime $p\ge7$ cannot divide both Lucas factors of $t$, since their indices differ by 5. If it did, then $g^5=1$ in the residue field and a power of this odd-order element would equal $-1$. Also $p\mid t,z$ implies $p\mid 125$ by the Pell equation. Thus each $p\ge7$ dividing $tz$ corresponds to exactly one factor and one index $k\in\{n,n+5,n-5\}$, with $v_p(tz)=w_p+v_p(k)$.

Consequently

$$0\ \le\ \sum_{p\ge7}(v_p(tz)-1)_+\log p-
\sum_{\substack{p\ge7\\p\mid tz}}(w_p-1)\log p\ \le\ \log\bigl(n(n+5)(n-5)\bigr).$$

This bounds the combined excess of **all** primes with $w_p=1$ by $O(\log(2n))$, irrespective of size. For the other primes, $w_p\log p\le\log F_{d_p}\ll p$. Since $\sum_{p\le Y}p\ll Y^2/\log(2Y)$, even the full contribution from $tz$ up to $\sqrt n$ is $O(n/\log(2n))$.

## How large can one square divisor be?

For each of the four positive integers $H\in\{t,z,A,B\}$, write $H=d_Hs_H^2$ with $d_H$ squarefree. Then $d_H\to\infty$ along the progression for each of the four choices. Equivalently, the largest square dividing $H$ is $o(H)$. This gives a second constraint on the large primes. For instance every $p$ with $p^2\mid AB$ and $p\ge7$ satisfies $p=o(t)$ uniformly as $n\to\infty$, since such a prime divides only one quadratic and each quadratic is asymptotic to $t^2$.

For a proof, fix a positive integer $d$ and put $Q(T)=T^2+22T+125$, so $Q(t)=5z^2$. Write $G(T)=T^2+10T+5$ or $T^2+4T-1$. If $G(t)=d s^2$, then $(t,5dzs)$ is an integral point on

$$V^2=5d\,Q(T)G(T).$$

The polynomial on the right has four distinct roots. Both quadratics are separable, and the two possible resultants $\operatorname{Res}(Q,G)$ are $720$ and $6480$. The smooth projective curve has genus one, so Siegel's theorem gives only finitely many integral points on this affine model for each fixed $d$. Likewise $z=ds^2$ gives $L_n^2=5d^2s^4-4$ and $t=ds^2$ gives $(5z)^2=5Q(ds^2)$. Each right-hand side is a separable quartic in $s$ for $d>0$, so Siegel applies again. Because $t$ and $z$ grow strictly with the index, each fixed cofactor occurs at only finitely many indices. Taking the finite union over $d\le D$ for each fixed $D$ proves the limit. This argument gives no growth rate for $d_H$.

## The remaining sum

For $AB$ keep the excess at primes $p>n^{1/3}$ with $p\equiv3\pmod4$, and at primes $p>\sqrt n$ with $p\equiv1\pmod4$. For $tz$ keep $(w_p-1)\log p$ only at $p\mid tz$ with $p>\sqrt n$ and $w_p\ge2$. The sum of these three nonnegative quantities differs from $E(n)$ by $O(n/\log(2n))$. This follows from the estimates above and the pairwise coprimality of the four factors away from $2,3,5$. Hence their sum has positive limsup after division by $n$ exactly if the Pell family disproves abc. The estimates here leave this limsup undetermined.
