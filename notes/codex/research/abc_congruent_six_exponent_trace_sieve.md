# The trace at a prime index excludes exponents through one billion

For odd \(n\), a nonunit denominator \(B_n\) on \(y^2=x^3-36x\) at \(G=(-3,9)\) is **never a \(p\)-th power for prime \(5\le p\le10^9\)**. Every possible larger exponent also satisfies \(p\equiv\pm1\pmod{24}\) and
\[
  (\ell/p)=1\qquad
  (\ell=5,7,11,13,23,37,47,73).
\]
Thus it splits completely in the degree-1024 multiquadratic field generated by the square roots of \(2,3,5,7,11,13,23,37,47,73\). The permitted primes have relative density at most \(1/1024\). The descent already excludes \(5\le p<27431\), and a perfect power anywhere forces \(B_q=z^p\) at a prime index \(q\). Use the Eisenstein integer \(\gamma\) from that descent, \(s=(-1)^{(p-1)/2}\), \(\gamma+\bar\gamma=spu^2\), and \(\theta=\gamma/z^2\). Choose an auxiliary \(\ell>3\) small enough that \(q>(\sqrt\ell+1)^2\), and \(p>\ell+1\). Neither \(a_q\) nor \(w_q=B_q\) is divisible by \(\ell\), since these are the denominator roots at index \(q\) for the points \(G+(0,0)\) and \(G\), both integral and affine modulo \(\ell\). A divisor would give one of these points order \(q\) against Hasse's inequality. Consequently \(q\) is also coprime to \(r=\operatorname{ord}_\ell(G)\).

The reduction of \(\theta\) is a unit of norm one, and its group has order \(h=\ell-(-3/\ell)\). Put \(j=p^{-1}\pmod h\). For \(L_j(t)\) the Lucas trace polynomial with zero-index term 2, first term \(t\), and recurrence \(L_{k+1}=tL_k-L_{k-1}\), the original elliptic abscissa determines the quadratic character:
\[
 t=\theta^p+\theta^{-p}=(a_q/w_q)^2=-x([q]G)/3,\qquad
 \left(\frac{L_j(t)}{\ell}\right)=\left(\frac{sp}{\ell}\right).
\]
In fact \(L_j(t)=\theta+\theta^{-1}=sp(u/z)^2\), and \(u\) is nonzero modulo \(\ell\) because \(U=spu^2\) divides \(a_q^2\). The verifier evaluates the trace over all \(t=-x([k]G)/3\) with \(k\) a unit modulo \(r\). If all values are nonzero squares and \((sp/\ell)=-1\), the prime \(\ell\) excludes \(p\).

The new universal symbols have a short direct verification. At \(\ell=37\) the reduction order of \(G\) is 3, and the trace coordinate is 1 at every unit index. At \(\ell=73\) the order is 10 and the two possible trace coordinates are \(1,-1\). A norm-one element of trace 1 or \(-1\) has order 6 or 3, respectively. Its trace is unchanged by an exponent coprime to 6, and \(-1\) is a square modulo 73. Thus \((sp/37)=(sp/73)=1\). Reciprocity gives the stated symbols at \(p\).

For the quantitative check at every auxiliary prime through 251, the needed size inequalities follow from \(z\ge13\) and
\[
       p\log13\le\log B_q < 0.52q^2+1.5.
\]
The upper bound follows from \(\widehat h(G)<0.52\), the duplication bound \(h(x)\le2\widehat h+\tfrac13\log5184\), and \(B_q^2\le\exp h(x(qG))\). Thus \(p\ge27431\) gives \(q>360\), while \((\sqrt{251}+1)^2<284\). The auxiliary primes through 251 leave the following ten exponents through \(10^9\). Every row is eliminated by its displayed further auxiliary prime. For these exponents the same height estimate gives \(q>22000\), and every additional Hasse bound is below 1322. The verifier enumerates signed residues \(sp\equiv1\pmod{24}\) that are nonzero squares modulo \(5,7,11,13,23\), then applies the trace tables. It proves primality of every remaining candidate by trial division and checks the ten final witnesses. The argument gives a finite lower bound on any possible prime exponent; an unconditional upper bound for that exponent remains open here.
