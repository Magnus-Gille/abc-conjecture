# Rational points on the fourth-power plane quartic through an explicit height

For \(D:w^4=m^4+4m^3n+10m^2n^2-4mn^3+n^4\), write \(B=\max(|m|,|n|)\) in primitive integer projective coordinates. The complete list of points with \(\log B\le10^{11}\) is
\[
 [m:n:w]=[1:0:\pm1],\ [0:1:\pm1],\ [2:1:\pm3],\ [-1:2:\pm3].
\]
The search is unconditional in this height range.

**Elliptic quotient.** Set \(x=m/n\), \(y=(w/n)^2\), and \(g=x^2+2x+3\). The quartic equation is \(y^2=g^2-16x-8\). Set
\[
 u=2(y+g)-8,\qquad v=2(u+8)x+2u.
\]
Then \(E_0:v^2=u^3+16u^2+32u\) and \(x=(v-2u)/(2(u+8))\). The elliptic curve has Cremona label 256a1 via \((u,v)=(4X-4,8Y)\). The listed full generators give \(E_0(\mathbf Q)=\mathbf ZG\oplus\langle T\rangle\), \(G=(-4,8), T=(0,0)\). For comparison the mixed curve \(v^2=u^3-8u^2+8u\) is 256a2 via \(u=X+3\), and its generator \((8,8)\) differs from \(-(1,1)\) by \((0,0)\). [Cremona generator data, lines 1001–1002](https://github.com/JohnCremona/ecdata/blob/master/allgens/allgens.00000-09999#L1001)

At every odd prime, reduce \(kG+eT\) for each residue of \(k\) modulo the order of \(G\), for \(e=0,1\). If \(u\ne-8\), a necessary local condition is that \(y=(u+8)/2-x^2-2x-3\) be a square in \(\mathbf F_p\), including zero. For \(u=-8,v=-16\), the finite value is \(x=-1/2,y=-9/4\); for \(u=-8,v=16\) the infinity value \(y/x^2\) is \(-1\). The elliptic identity corresponds to the other infinity with limit \(+1\). These also follow by taking the two branches at infinity of the monic quartic. Filtering every integer \(-10^6\le k\le10^6\) using primes through 163 leaves \(k=-2,0\) in each torsion coset. The four elliptic images are the identity, \(T\), \((1,-7)\), and \((32,224)\). Direct substitution gives precisely the eight displayed lifts. [Standard-library sieve](abc_octic_genus3_quartic_sieve.py)

**Height coverage.** For reduced \(u=U/V\), the duplication map is \(F/H\), where \(F=(U^2-32V^2)^2\), \(H=4UV(U^2+16UV+32V^2)\). Its gcd is at most \(2^{10}\). An odd common prime would force \(U^2=32V^2\), \(U=-4V\) modulo that prime, impossible. For \(U\) even, \(V\) is odd and \(v_2(U^2)\ne5\), so \(v_2(F)\le10\). Put \(H_0=\max(|U|,V)\). If \(|U|\le4V\) or \(|U|\ge8V\), then \(F\ge H_0^4/4\). In the intermediate interval \(|U^2+16UV+32V^2|\ge16V^2\), so \(|H|\ge H_0^4/16\). The upper bound is \(\max(F,|H|)\le1089H_0^4\). Therefore
\[
4h(u)-14\log2\le h(u(2Q))\le4h(u)+\log1089.
\]
Since \(u(4G)=961/196\), the doubling limit gives \(L=2\hat h(G)\ge(\log961-14\log2/3)/16>0.22\). Thus \(h(u(kG+eT))\ge 0.22 k^2-(\log1089)/3\). Conversely \(u=(2w^2+2m^2+4mn-2n^2)/n^2\) gives \(h(u)\le2\log B+\log17\), since \(w^4\le20B^4\). Every point with \(\log B\le10^{11}\) therefore has \(|k|<10^6\), with the projective infinities checked directly.

**Rank and quadratic Chabauty.** The conjugate quartic factors supply a complementary abelian surface isogenous to the restriction of scalars of \(E':y^2=x^3+(1+i)x\) over \(K=\mathbf Q(i)\). Its rank over \(K\) is exactly two. The usual two-isogeny descent maps on \(E'\) and \(y^2=x^3-4(1+i)x\) take values in the squareclasses generated by \(i,1+i\): away from \(1+i\), negative valuations of the elliptic abscissa are even, and a positive valuation forces twice the ordinate valuation to equal the abscissa valuation. The Gaussian ring is principal. Each descent image therefore has dimension at most two, yielding rank at most two. The independent points \(P'=(i,i)\) and \([i]P'\) give the matching lower bound. The plane quartic Jacobian has rank three. [Quotient maps and independence](abc_octic_mixed_fourth_power_quotients.md)

The rational endomorphism algebra is \(\mathbf Q\times\mathbf Q(i)\). The elliptic factors on the surface are not isogenous over \(K\): their quartic twisting ratio is \(-i\), which is not a fourth power in \(K\). All geometric endomorphisms of the source are defined over \(K\), so composing the isomorphism with an endomorphism cannot cancel this nontrivial twisting character. Rational endomorphisms of the restriction of scalars are conjugate pairs of elements of \(\mathbf Q(i)\), and the other elliptic factor has geometric CM by a different quadratic field. Its rational endomorphisms are the rationals. Rosati consequently has fixed subspace of dimension two, giving rational Néron–Severi rank two. Thus the quadratic Chabauty finiteness inequality \(3<3+2-1\) holds. The published QCMod implementation assumes real multiplication and a suitable Hecke generator. Its documented sample collection does not include an identified solution of this curve. A full quadratic Chabauty point computation has not been established here.
