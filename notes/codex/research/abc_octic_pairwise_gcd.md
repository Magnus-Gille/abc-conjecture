# An exact gcd law for the moving quadratic factor

At positive odd index \(N\ge3\) in the two-square orbit, let \(U_N=(s_N+t_N)^2+t_N^2\). For distinct admissible \(M,N\) write
\[
 g_- = \gcd(N^2+2,N-M),\qquad g_+ =\gcd(N^2+2,N+M).
\]
There is a fixed positive constant \(h=\widehat h(A)\) for which
\[
 \log\gcd(U_N,U_M)=h(g_-+g_+)+O(\log(2g_-g_+)).
\]
There is also an **exact integer formula** below. If \(M<N\), it follows that \(\log\gcd(U_N,U_M)=O(N)\), while \(\log U_N=hN^2+O(1)\). The linear upper bound is sharp in order. [Orbit and height](abc_octic_full_mixed_orbit.md)

## The integer whose denominator gives \(U_N\)

Work over \(K=\mathbb Q(\tau)\), \(\tau^2=-2\), with its principal ideal ring \(\mathcal O=\mathbb Z[\tau]\). On \(E':y^2=x^3+16x^2+32x\) use \(R=(-8,16)\) and \(S=R+(0,0)=(-4,-8)\). The curve has complex multiplication by \(\mathcal O\); choose the sign of the degree-two endomorphism so that \([\tau]S=(-2,2\tau)\). Indeed the isogeny with kernel generated by \((0,0)\), followed by the identification with the \(-2\) twist, gives the abscissa \(-(x+16+32/x)/2\). The two-isogeny from \(E\) sends \(A\) to \([2]R\), so \(2\widehat h(S)=\widehat h(A)=h\). [Isogeny and denominator coordinates](abc_octic_mixed_three_factors.md)

For a nonzero ideal \(I=(\alpha)\), define \(D(I)\) as the product over odd finite places \(\mathfrak p\) of \((\operatorname N\mathfrak p)^{\max(0,-v_{\mathfrak p}(x([\alpha]S))/2)}\). This is a positive integer by good reduction and the double pole of the abscissa. The two generator choices differ by sign and give the same value. For odd \(N\), translation by \((0,0)\) gives \(x([N]S)=32/x([N]R)=-4Y_N/V_N\), where \(Y_N=s_N^2+2t_N^2\) and \(V_N=(s_N-t_N)^2+t_N^2\) are coprime odd squares. Hence
\[
 x([N]S)-x([\tau]S)=-2U_N/V_N.
\]
Every odd \(p\mid U_N\) is prime to \(V_N\). At such a prime reduction of \([N]S\) equals \(\pm[\tau]S\); the ordinate \(\pm2\tau\) is a unit. The rationality of \([N]S\) forces \(p\) to split in \(K\), and the two places interchange the two signs. At the one with the plus sign, the local depth of \([N-\tau]S\) is exactly \(v_p(U_N)\), since the abscissa difference is a local parameter. At the other it is zero. Conversely positive depth at an odd place forces \(p\mid U_N\). Therefore, at every positive admissible index,
\[
 U_N=D((N-\tau)).
\]

## Taking two gcds in the quadratic ring

Put \(I_-=(N-\tau,M-\tau)\) and \(I_+=(N-\tau,M+\tau)\), meaning the ideals generated by the displayed pairs. Then exactly
\[
 \gcd(U_N,U_M)=D(I_-)D(I_+).
\]
To check multiplicities, for each good odd place and each depth \(j\ge1\) the multipliers sending \(S\) into the depth-\(j\) formal subgroup are an ideal in \(\mathcal O\). Endomorphisms preserve this subgroup by good reduction and integral formal power series. Thus two multipliers both reach depth \(j\) if and only if their ideal generator does. At a rational prime of the gcd there is a unique place with positive depth for \(N-\tau\). At this same place exactly one of \(M-\tau,M+\tau\) has positive depth, according to the sign at the second index. This proves the exact formula with the minimum of the two valuations.

The quotient \(\mathcal O/(N-\tau)\) identifies with \(\mathbb Z/(N^2+2)\) by evaluating \(\tau\) at \(N\). Consequently \(\operatorname NI_-=g_-\), \(\operatorname NI_+=g_+\). For every odd-norm \(I=(\gamma)\), the uniform height estimate gives
\[
 \log D(I)=2\widehat h(S)\operatorname NI+O(\log(2\operatorname NI)).
\]
Indeed \([\bar\gamma][\gamma]S=[\operatorname NI]S\) stays on the bounded real component for odd norm; the Lipschitz constant \(\sqrt{\operatorname NI}\) on the complex torus bounds the archimedean abscissa term by \(O(\log(2\operatorname NI))\). At the dyadic place all odd integer multiples of \(S\) have abscissa of valuation two, by the formula \(-4Y/V\), also at index one. A sufficiently small formal neighborhood is stable under \(\mathcal O\), so the dyadic denominator of \([\gamma]S\) is uniformly bounded. The absolute abscissa height is \(2\widehat h(S)\operatorname NI+O(1)\), giving the estimate. Applying this to the two gcd ideals proves the asymptotic formula. [Details of the height argument](abc_octic_mixed_cm_prime_mass.md)

For sharpness take any positive odd \(m\ge3\), let \(d=m^2+2\), and put \(M=m\), \(N=2d+m\). Since \(N\equiv m\bmod d\), the ideal \((m-\tau)\) divides \((N-\tau)\). Thus \(U_m\mid U_N\) by formal group divisibility. The gcd is exactly \(U_m\), with logarithm \(h m^2+O(1)\asymp N\). More generally \(U_m\mid U_N\) whenever \(N\equiv\pm m\bmod m^2+2\).

An independent computation of the slopes and of the quadratic denominator ideals checks the exact integer formula for all 465 pairs \(3\le M<N\le63\) of odd indices and several larger pairs. For example \((N,M)=(63,25)\) gives \((g_-,g_+)=(19,11)\), with respective denominator factors \(3761,137\); the integer gcd is \(515257\). [Exact arithmetic verifier](abc_octic_cm_gcd_check.py)

## Cumulative overlap

Even the full contribution to \(\log U_N\) from primes encountered at *any* earlier index is \(O(N^{1+o(1)})\), by the CM order argument in the [first-occurrence criterion](abc_octic_first_occurrence.md). Therefore
\[
 \log\operatorname{lcm}_{3\le N\le X,\ N\ {\rm odd}} U_N
 =\frac{h}{6}X^3+O(X^{2+o(1)}).
\]
Indeed the log lcm increases at index \(N\) by \(\log U_N-\log\gcd(U_N,\operatorname{lcm}_{M<N}U_M)\), where only admissible indices are used. The subtracted term is bounded by the total earlier prime contribution. Sum \(\log U_N=hN^2+O(1)\) over the odd indices to obtain the formula. Repetitions originating inside a new prime's very first term survive this lcm calculation.
