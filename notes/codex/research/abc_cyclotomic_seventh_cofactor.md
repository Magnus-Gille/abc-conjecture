# Seven times a square in the seventh and fourteenth cyclotomic forms

The only rational projective slope satisfying
\[
  \Phi_7(A,B)=A^6+A^5B+A^4B^2+A^3B^3+A^2B^4+AB^5+B^6=7z^2
\]
is \([1:1]\). For \(\Phi_{14}(A,B)=\Phi_7(A,-B)\) the only slope is
\([-1:1]\). In particular, neither equation has coprime integer solutions
with \(A>B>0\). The condition is well-defined on projective slopes because
scaling \((A,B)\) multiplies the form by a sixth power. The alternative
equation \(Z^2=7\Phi_7(A,B)\) gives the same slopes, with \(Z=7z\).

## Reduction to an elliptic curve

Suppose \(A+B\ne0\) and put \(s=(A-B)/(A+B)\) and \(v=8z/(A+B)^3\).
Expanding the difference of seventh powers gives
\[
          7v^2=s^6+21s^4+35s^2+7 .
\]
If \(s\ne0\), set \(t=1/s^2\) and \(w=v/s^3\). Then
\[
          w^2=t^3+5t^2+3t+1/7 .
\]
The coordinates \(x=49t,\ y=343w\) give the elliptic quotient
\[
          y^2=x^3+245x^2+7203x+16807 .
\]
Putting \(T=(x+83)/4,\ U=y/8\) yields the integral model
\[
          E\colon U^2=f(T)=T^3-T^2-800T+8359.
\]
We prove below that \(E(\mathbf Q)=\{O\}\), so no slope with
\((A+B)(A-B)\ne0\) can occur. At \(A=B\ne0\), the form is \(7A^6\) and
\(z=\pm A^3\). At \(A=-B\ne0\), its value is \(A^6\), which has even
7-adic valuation. This proves the classification, including the slope at
infinity, for which \(s=1\). Replacing \(B\) by \(-B\) proves the
assertion at index 14.

## A 2-descent on the quotient

Let \(g(t)=t^3+t^2-2t-1\), let \(\theta\) be a root, and put
\(K=\mathbf Q(\theta)\). The rational root test gives irreducibility.
The discriminant of \(g\) is \(49\). A proper index of
\(\mathbf Z[\theta]\) in the ring of integers would give field
discriminant 1, which Minkowski's bound excludes. Hence
\(\mathcal O_K=\mathbf Z[\theta]\). The field is totally real. Every
ideal class has an integral representative of norm at most
\((3!/3^3)\sqrt{49}=14/9\), so the class number is one. Modulo 7 one has
\(g(t)=(t-2)^3\). Thus there is a single prime above 7, of residue degree
one.

In this field put \(\lambda=-14\theta^2+7\theta+26\). Reduction by
\(g(\theta)=0\) gives \(f(\lambda)=0\), and \(\lambda\notin\mathbf Q\)
because \(1,\theta,\theta^2\) are independent. Consequently \(f\) is its
minimal polynomial. Its discriminant is \(7^{10}\), so
\([\mathcal O_K:\mathbf Z[\lambda]]=7^4\).

Write any finite rational point as \(T=a/d^2,\ U=b/d^3\) with
\(d>0\) and \(\gcd(a,d)=1\). At a prime where \(T\) has negative
valuation the leading term of \(f(T)\) gives \(2v(U)=3v(T)\), which
justifies the denominator exponents. The integral element
\(\alpha=a-\lambda d^2\) has norm \(b^2\ne0\). If a prime above
\(\ell\) divides \(\alpha\), then \(\ell\nmid d\), and its reduction of
\(\lambda\) must equal \(a/d^2\) in \(\mathbf F_\ell\). For
\(\ell\ne7\) the localization of \(\mathcal O_K\) at \(\ell\) is
generated by \(\lambda\), so this selects at most one prime above
\(\ell\), of residue degree one. The same uniqueness and residue degree
at 7 follow from the factorization of \(g\) above. At every prime
dividing \(\alpha\), its ideal valuation therefore equals the valuation
of its norm there and is even. The class number calculation gives
\(\alpha=\epsilon h^2\) for some unit \(\epsilon\) and
\(h\in\mathcal O_K\).

Order the real embeddings by the roots of \(g\) in
\((-19/10,-9/5),\ (-1/2,-2/5),\ (6/5,5/4)\). The units
\(\theta,\ \beta=\theta^2-2,\ -1\) have signatures \((--+),(+--),(---)\).
Here \(g(\beta)=0\), and the constant term of \(g\) gives norm one
for \(\theta\) and \(\beta\). The three signatures are independent, and
Dirichlet's unit theorem gives eight unit classes modulo squares.
Consequently signs determine the squareclass of any unit. In the first
embedding \(\lambda\) is negative, and in the other two it is positive.
Since \(f(T)>0\), the three signs of \(T-\lambda\) are either
\((+++)\) or \((+--)\). Thus only \(1\) and \(\beta\) can represent
\(\epsilon\) modulo squares.

To exclude \(\beta\), use coefficient triples in the basis
\(1,\theta,\theta^2\) modulo 4. The square of \(h\) depends only on its
residue modulo 2. Multiplying by \(\beta\) and reducing via
\(\theta^3=-\theta^2+2\theta+1\) gives the complete table.

| \(h\bmod 2\) | \(\beta h^2\bmod4\) |
|---|---|
| \((0,0,0)\) | \((0,0,0)\) |
| \((0,0,1)\) | \((2,1,3)\) |
| \((0,1,0)\) | \((3,3,1)\) |
| \((0,1,1)\) | \((3,2,0)\) |
| \((1,0,0)\) | \((2,0,1)\) |
| \((1,0,1)\) | \((2,3,2)\) |
| \((1,1,0)\) | \((3,3,0)\) |
| \((1,1,1)\) | \((1,0,1)\) |

For even \(d\), \(\alpha\) has triple \((a,0,0)\) with \(a\) odd.
For odd \(d\) its triple is \((a-2,1,2)\). The table excludes both
possibilities. Hence \(T-\lambda=\alpha/d^2\) is a square in \(K\).

## Halving and torsion

Choose \(r\in K\) with \(r^2=T-\lambda\) and \(N(r)=-U\). This sign
choice is possible since \([K:\mathbf Q]=3\). Let \(s_1,s_2\) be the
first two elementary symmetric functions of the conjugates of \(r\).
They are rational. Expanding \(f(t)=\prod_i(t-T+r_i^2)\) gives
\[
 f(t)-\bigl(-U-s_1(t-T)\bigr)^2=(t-T)(t-T-s_2)^2 .
\]
The displayed line goes through \((T,-U)\) and is tangent at a rational
point with abscissa \(T+s_2\). The chord and tangent law makes this point
a half of \((T,U)\), including the case of triple contact. Thus
\(E(\mathbf Q)=2E(\mathbf Q)\). The Mordell–Weil theorem gives rank zero.

The discriminant of this model is \(16\cdot7^{10}\), so reduction is good
at 3 and 13. Counting the possible ordinates at each residue \(T\) gives

| Prime | Counts at \(T=0,1,\ldots,p-1\) | \(\#E(\mathbf F_p)\) |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | \(2,0,2\) | 5 |
| 13 | \(1,0,2,2,0,1,0,0,0,1,0,0,0\) | 8 |

Prime-to-characteristic torsion injects under good reduction. The two
counts exclude every torsion prime, including 3 and 13 by using the other
reduction. This proves \(E(\mathbf Q)=\{O\}\).
