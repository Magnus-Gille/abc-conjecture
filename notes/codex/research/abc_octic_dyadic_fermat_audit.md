# A globally primitive factor at the two neighbours of a power of two

Use \(E:v^2=u^3-8u^2+8u\), \(A=(1,1)\), \(E':y^2=x^3+16x^2+32x\), \(R=(-8,16)\), and \(J,J'=(0,0)\) on the respective curves. For a power of two \(n\ge4\) write the rational point \([n/2]R\) as
\[
       x=u_n^2/v_n^2,\qquad |y|=u_n w_n/v_n^3,\qquad
       w_n^2=u_n^4+16u_n^2v_n^2+32v_n^4,
\]
where the three integers are positive. They are pairwise coprime, \(u_n,w_n\) are odd, and \(v_n\) is even for \(n\ge8\).

For every \(n\ge8\), every rational prime of \(w_n\) first appears anywhere in the full octic triple family at its odd index \(N=n-1\), in the signed slope numerator \(s_N\). It occurs there and at \(N=n+1\) with exactly its exponent in \(w_n\). All such primes split completely in the CM \(2n\)-torsion and have \(p\ge8n^2+1\). Moreover,
\[
 \log w_n=\tfrac12\widehat h(R)n^2+O(1),\qquad
 \log c_{n\pm1}=8\widehat h(R)n^2+O(n).
\]
If \(\liminf P^+(w_n)/n^4=0\), there is a common subsequence on which the qualities at *both* neighbours have lower limit at least \(16/15\). If \(P^+(w_n)\le cn^4\) infinitely often for a sufficiently small fixed \(c>0\), both qualities exceed one by a fixed amount infinitely often. The behaviour of the largest prime in these statements remains undetermined. [Full octic family](abc_octic_full_mixed_orbit.md)

## Explicit integer recursion and splitting

At \(n=4\) the triple is \((u,v,w)=(1,1,7)\), since \(2R=(1,-7)\). With \((u,v,w)=(u_n,v_n,w_n)\), the duplication formula gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 u_{2n}&=|u^4-32v^4|,\qquad v_{2n}=2uvw,\\
 w_{2n}&=u^8+32u^6v^2+192u^4v^4+1024u^2v^6+1024v^8 .
 \end{aligned}
\]
Indeed \(x(2P)=(x(P)^2-32)^2/(4y(P)^2)\), and the asserted \(w_{2n}\) can be checked by squaring it in the new quartic identity. There is no cancellation in the numerator-denominator pair for the square root of \(x(2P)\). An odd divisor common to \(u^4-32v^4\) and \(w\) would give simultaneously \(x^2=32\) and \(x^2+16x+32=0\) at \(x=u^2/v^2\). These imply \(x=-4\) and \(16=32\). At any odd prime of \(u\) or \(v\), coprimality with \(u^4-32v^4\) is immediate, and its parity is odd. The quartic then makes \(w_{2n}\) coprime to \(u_{2n}v_{2n}\) as well. The first two values for \(n\ge8\) are \(w_8=2273\), \(w_{16}=22151840117633\).

The two identities
\[
 w_{2n}=(u^4+16u^2v^2-32v^4)^2+2(32uv^3)^2
       =w_n^4-2(8u^2v^2)^2
\]
are primitive at every odd prime. Consequently every \(p\mid w_n\) for \(n\ge8\) splits in both \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)\) and \(K=\mathbb Q(\tau)\), \(\tau^2=-2\); in particular \(p\equiv1\bmod8\). Splitting in \(K\) is material in concluding that all the CM multipliers of a rational point remain rational over the residue field.

## Reduction orders and first appearances

For \(p\mid w_n\), \(P=[n/2]R\) reduces to one of the two nonidentity two-torsion points other than \(J'\). Its abscissa is a nonzero unit and its ordinate has valuation \(v_p(w_n)\). The ordinate is a local parameter at that two-torsion point at good reduction, since its abscissa is a simple root of the cubic. Doubling preserves this depth for odd \(p\), so \([n]R\) is formal of the same depth. The integer order of the reduction of \(R\) is exactly \(n\). Its annihilator in \(\mathcal O_K\) is exactly \((n)\). Indeed all ideal divisors of \((n)\) are powers of the unique prime \((\tau)\), and \((n/\tau)\) does not annihilate \(R\), since up to sign it sends \(R\) to \([\tau]P\) and the kernel of \([\tau]\) on \(E'\) is \(\{O,J'\}\). Thus the module generated by \(R\) has order \(n^2\), giving all \(E'[n]\). At the split prime \(p\) it lies in \(E'(\mathbb F_p)\). In particular the sets of prime divisors of \(w_n\) are pairwise disjoint as \(n\) varies over powers of two at least eight.

In fact all of \(E'[2n]\) is rational over \(\mathbb F_p\). Write the cubic roots as \(0,e_+,e_-\), where \(e_\pm=-8\pm4\sqrt2\). The usual full rational two-torsion halving criterion requires all three abscissa differences to be squares for a point to be twice another point. A rational half of the two-torsion point at \(e_+\) exists since \(E'[4]\) is rational. Thus \(e_+-e_-=8\sqrt2\) is a square. Since \(p\equiv1\bmod8\), both 8 and \(-1\) are squares, so \(x(R)=-8\) has square differences from all three roots. The halving criterion supplies \(R'\in E'(\mathbb F_p)\) with \(2R'=R\). Since \([n]R'=[n/2]R\) is non-J two-torsion, the same consecutive-powers-of-\(\tau\) argument gives \(\operatorname{ann}(R')=(2n)\). Its CM module is all of \(E'[2n]\). This extra factor of two is already sharp at \(p=2273,n=8\), where \(\#E'(\mathbb F_p)=2304\) is not divisible by \(32^2\).

The dual isogeny \(\psi:E'\to E\) has kernel generated by \(J'\), abscissa \((x+16+32/x)/4\), ordinate \((1-32/x^2)y/8\), and \(\psi(R)=A\). Thus \([n/2]A\) reduces to \(J\) at every \(p\mid w_n\). Its abscissa over the rationals is exactly \((w_n/(2u_nv_n))^2\). Equivalently by étaleness of \(\psi\) at odd primes, its distance to \(J\) has depth \(v_p(w_n)\). At full orbit index \(n-1\), the slope is evaluated at \(Q=[n/2]A\) and has a simple zero at \(J\); at \(n+1\) it is evaluated at \(Q=A+[n/2]A\) and has a simple zero at \(A+J\). Both simple-zero parameters have unit leading coefficients at every odd prime. Hence \(v_p(s_{n-1})=v_p(s_{n+1})=v_p(w_n)\). [Integral local slope parameters](abc_octic_mixed_skew_denominator.md)

The order of \(A\) is likewise \(n\). Its CM module has order \(n^2/2\), since it is the isogeny image of the full \(E'[n]\) and the kernel there has order two. The two CM actions are identified through the isogeny, up to conjugation. Thus this module is noncyclic. At any \(U_M\) prime, the known equality \([M]A=\pm[\tau]A\) would make the CM module cyclic. The small characteristics where that equality of affine coordinates needs separate care are excluded here by \(p\equiv1\bmod8\). At \(Y_M V_M\) one would have \(n\mid M\), incompatible with odd \(M\). At \(D_M\) one would have \([M]A=J\), requiring \(M\equiv n/2\bmod n\), also even. In the remaining \(s_Mt_M\) an occurrence requires \(M\equiv\pm1\bmod n\). The initial full index is three, proving the claim about first occurrence. ## Archimedean size and the prime bound

By positivity of all coefficients, \(\log w_n=2\log\max(u_n,v_n)+O(1)=h(x(P))+O(1)\). The canonical height gives the asserted leading term. Since \(\psi(R)=A\), one has \(\widehat h(A)=2\widehat h(R)\). With the octic height formula \(\log\max(|s_N|,t_N)=\widehat h(A)N^2/2+O(1)\) and \(c_N\asymp\max(|s_N|,t_N)^8\), the formula for \(\log c_{n\pm1}\) follows.

At a split good prime the Frobenius is a CM multiplier \(\pi\in\mathcal O_K\) of norm \(p\). Since it fixes \(R'\), its residue modulo the annihilator \((2n)\) is 1. Hence
\[
 p=(1+2na)^2+8n^2b^2,\qquad a,b\in\mathbb Z,\ b\ne0.
\]
The lower bound \(p\ge8n^2+1\) follows. For any fixed \(0<c\le1\) and every sufficiently large power of two \(n\), with an absolute implied constant independent of \(c\),
\[
       \sum_{p\mid w_n,\ p\le cn^4}\log p\ll c n^2.                              \tag{1}
\]
Indeed put \(X=cn^4\), \(B=2+\sqrt X/n\), and sieve pairs \((a,b)\) in the box \(|a|,|b|\le 2B\) for which the displayed norm is prime. For an odd prime \(\ell\), the number of zero classes modulo \(\ell\) is \(2\ell-1\) if \(\ell\) splits in \(K\), one if inert. This does not depend on \(n\), because \(n\) is a power of two. For odd squarefree \(d\), the multiplicative root count is at most \(2^{\omega(d)}d\), and counting classes in the box has error \(O(2^{\omega(d)}(B+d))\). The sum of these errors for \(d\le B^{1/2}\), even with any fixed divisor-power weight, is \(o(B^2/\log B)\). The local density product through \(z\) is \(\asymp1/\log z\) by Mertens in the fixed split residue classes, with the inert-prime correction absolutely convergent. An upper-bound sieve of dimension one, level \(B^{1/2}\) and cutoff \(B^{1/10}\), now gives \(O(B^2/\log B)\) surviving pairs. Every prime counted on the left exceeds \(2n^2\), so it survives. For fixed \(c\), \(B^2\asymp cn^2\) and \(\log B\sim\log n\), and each prime contributes at most \(\log(cn^4)\). This proves (1).

Let \(\mathcal R_N=\operatorname{rad}(a_Nb_Nc_N)\) and \(q_N=\log c_N/\log\mathcal R_N\). The integer \(T_N=2|s_Nt_ND_NU_N|\sqrt{Y_NV_N}\) has this radical and satisfies \(T_N\ll c_N\). Since \(w_n\mid s_{n\pm1}\),
\[
 \log(c_{n\pm1}/\mathcal R_{n\pm1})\ge\log(w_n/\operatorname{rad}(w_n))-O(1).
\]
If \(P^+(w_n)\le cn^4\), (1) bounds the radical term by \(Ccn^2\), which proves the fixed-exponent assertion on choosing \(Cc<\widehat h(R)/2\). If \(P^+(w_n)/n^4\to0\) on a subsequence, apply (1) for each fixed \(c>0\), then let \(c\downarrow0\). One obtains \(\log\operatorname{rad}(w_n)=o(n^2)\) and the ratio \(16/15\). Conversely \(q_{n-1}\le1+o(1)\) forces distinct primes exceeding \(cn^4\) in \(w_n\) to contribute at least \((\widehat h(R)/2-Cc-o(1))n^2\) in log mass.

For a fixed positive integer \(d\), both indices \(dn\pm1\) carry the same primes in \(s\) for odd \(d\), or in \(t\) for even \(d\). Multiplication by \(d\) from the disk at \(J\) sends it to the disk at \([d]J\) with depth increased by \(v_p(d)\), and the four zero/pole slope charts are simple. Thus the small-largest-prime subsequence yields the lower limit \(16d^2/(16d^2-1)\) there. If \(P^+(w_n)=o(n^4)\) holds along *all* dyadic indices, each fixed number of consecutive factors \(w_n,w_{n/2},\ldots\) has negligible radical and disjoint prime support. They all divide \(s_{n\pm1}t_{n\pm1}\). Summing their logarithmic masses and then increasing the number of factors gives \(\liminf_{n}q_{n\pm1}\ge12/11\).

## An explicit sieve constant

In (1), one can take the following limiting constant for every fixed \(c>0\):
\[
 \limsup_{n=2^k\to\infty}
 \frac{1}{cn^2}\sum_{p\mid w_n,\ p\le cn^4}\log p\ \le\ 4.                  \tag{2}
\]
Use the actual ellipse \((1+2na)^2+8n^2b^2\le X\), whose area is \(\pi B^2/\sqrt2\) with \(B=\sqrt X/(2n)\). Counting any fixed residue class modulo \(d\) gives area divided by \(d^2\) and error \(O(B/d+1)\), uniformly in its translate. This follows by surrounding each lattice point with a unit square after dividing through by \(d\), so only an absolute-width boundary band is in error. Writing \(g(\ell)=\rho(\ell)/\ell^2\) for the odd-prime density used above, the Selberg upper bound has main term \(\mathrm{area}/G(z)\), where
\[
 G(z)=\sum_{d<z,\ d\ {\rm odd\ squarefree}}\prod_{\ell\mid d}\frac{g(\ell)}{1-g(\ell)}.
\]
Its remainder is bounded by \(\sum_{d<z^2}3^{\omega(d)}|r_d|\), where \(|r_d|\ll2^{\omega(d)}(B+d)\) is the error in counting all forbidden classes modulo \(d\). In the usual minimizing Selberg weights \(|\lambda_d|\le1\); collecting pairs of squarefree moduli by their lcm gives the factor \(3^{\omega(d)}\). Thus \(z=B^{1/2-\epsilon}\) for any fixed \(0<\epsilon<1/2\) makes this remainder \(o(B^2/\log B)\).

Let \(\chi=\chi_{-8}\), \(h(\ell)=g(\ell)/(1-g(\ell))\). The Euler product \(\prod_{\ell>2}(1+h(\ell)\ell^{-s})\), for real \(s>0\), divided by \(\zeta(1+s)L(1+s,\chi)\) tends to \(1/2\) as \(s\downarrow0\). At split odd primes the quotient's local factor at zero is \((1-g(\ell))^{-1}(1-1/\ell)^2=1\); at inert primes it is \((1-g(\ell))^{-1}(1-1/\ell^2)=1\). The local quotients differ uniformly from 1 by \(O(\ell^{-2})\) for real \(s\ge0\), and the omitted dyadic Euler factor contributes \(1-1/2\). Since the coefficients are nonnegative, the Laplace–Stieltjes form of the Hardy–Littlewood–Karamata Tauberian theorem gives
\[
              G(z)\sim \tfrac12 L(1,\chi)\log z,\qquad L(1,\chi)=\pi/(2\sqrt2).
\]
The latter value is also the class number formula for \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-2})\), which has class number one and two units.

Every counted rational prime supplies at least the two lattice points with \(b\) and \(-b\), and exceeds the sieving threshold. Hence the prime count is at most half the bound on surviving pairs. Multiply it by \(\log X\); at \(X=cn^4\), \(\log X/\log B\to4\). On letting \(\epsilon\downarrow0\), the resulting coefficient of \(B^2=cn^2/4\) is
\[
 \frac12\frac\pi{\sqrt2}\frac{4}{L(1,\chi)}\cdot 4=16.
\]
This proves (2). More generally \(X=n^\beta\) with fixed \(\beta>2\) yields \((2\beta/(\beta-2)+o(1))X/n^2\) as the bound.

For any fixed \(c<\widehat h(R)/8\), smoothness \(P^+(w_n)\le cn^4\) along a subsequence therefore gives
\[
 \liminf q_{n\pm1}\ \ge\ \left(\frac{15}{16}+\frac{c}{2\widehat h(R)}\right)^{-1}>1 .
\]
Here the numerical choice \(c=2/75\) is rigorously admissible. Indeed \(u([4]A)=5166529/753424\); the global height bound \(2\widehat h(B)\ge h(u(B))-\log(128)/3\) gives \(\widehat h(R)/8=\widehat h(A)/16\ge (\log 5166529-(\log128)/3)/512>2/75\). In particular \(\widehat h(R)>1/5\), and \(P^+(w_n)\le n^4/250\) infinitely often would give \(q_{n\pm1}>21/20\) simultaneously infinitely often.
