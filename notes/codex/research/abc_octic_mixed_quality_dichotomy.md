# A quantitative alternative between simple elliptic primes and abc quality approaching two

Let \(E:v^2=u^3-8u^2+8u\), \(A=(1,1)\). For every odd good prime \(p\), let \(r_p\) be the reduction order of \(A\). Count \(p\) in \(W(X)\) if \(p\le X\) and \([r_p]A\) has formal depth exactly one, i.e. it is the identity modulo \(p\) and differs from the identity modulo \(p^2\). Up to finitely many primes, this is the usual elliptic non-Wieferich condition for the pair \((E,A)\). Indeed \(p\ge7\) cannot divide \(\#E(\mathbf F_p)\): the group has rational 2-torsion and Hasse's bound gives \(\#E(\mathbf F_p)<2p\).

At positive odd full indices \(N\ge3\) form the primitive octic triple
\[
 (a_N,b_N,c_N)=((2st)^4,(s^4-4t^4)^2,(s^4+4t^4)^2),\qquad q_N=\frac{\log c_N}{\log\operatorname{rad}(a_Nb_Nc_N)}.
\]
There is an absolute constant \(C\) for this fixed curve and point such that for each fixed \(0<\delta<1\) and every sufficiently large \(M\),
\[
 \#\{M\le N\le2M:\ N\text{ odd},\ q_N\le 2-\delta\}
 \ \ll_\delta\ W(\exp(CM^2)).
\]
In particular at least one of the following assertions holds:

* \(W(X)\gg\sqrt{\log X}\) for every sufficiently large \(X\).
* \(\limsup_{N\to\infty,\ N\text{ odd}} q_N\ge2\). This would disprove rational abc. For example there would be infinitely many primitive triples with \(c>\operatorname{rad}(abc)^{3/2}\).

The alternatives may both hold. The implication is unconditional. It does not decide the distribution of these primes or determine which qualities occur infinitely often.

## From multiplicities in the triple to formal depth

Write \(Y=s^2+2t^2,\ V=(s-t)^2+t^2,\ U=(s+t)^2+t^2,\ D=s^2-2t^2\), \(H=\max(|s|,t)\). On this orbit \(Y,V\) are squares, \(\log H=\widehat h(A)N^2/2+O(1)\), and \(c_N\asymp H^8\). Define
\[
 T_N=2|stDU|\sqrt{YV}.
\]
This integer satisfies \(T_N\ll H^8\) and its radical equals that of the whole triple. The six factors \(s,t,D,U,\sqrt Y,\sqrt V\) are pairwise coprime at odd primes. This follows by comparing the homogeneous forms at primitive slopes: the four quadratics have disjoint simple roots in odd characteristic, and they have no common odd prime divisor with \(s\) or \(t\). The exception for 2 is immaterial.

We show that an odd prime of exponent one in \(T_N\) is counted in \(W\), except possibly at \(p\le7\) or \(p\mid N^2+2\). The full slope formula gives \(u([N]A)=D^2/(YV)\). Put \(J=(0,0)\), and let \(\tau=\sqrt{-2}\) denote the complex multiplication endomorphism. [Full parametrization and reduction-order identities](abc_octic_full_mixed_orbit.md)

* If \(p\mid YV\), the formal depth of \([N]A\) is exactly \(v_p(\sqrt{YV})\), by the abscissa formula. If \(p\mid D\), \([N]A\) reduces to \(J\). The abscissa at \(J\) has a double zero with unit leading coefficient at odd primes. This gives depth \(v_p(D)\) for \([N]A-J\), and doubling preserves it, giving the same depth for \([2N]A\).
* If \(p\mid st\), \([N]A\) reduces to \(A\) or \(-A\), and \(u-1\) is a local parameter there. Since \(D^2-YV=2st(s^2-4st+2t^2)\), the depth of the corresponding \([N\pm1]A\) is exactly \(v_p(st)\).
* If \(p\mid U\), the equality \(2D^2+YV=U(3U-14st)\) gives \(v_p(u([N]A)+1/2)=v_p(U)\) for \(p>7\). The reduction of \([N]A\) is one of \(\pm\tau A\), whose abscissa is \(-1/2\) and whose ordinate squared is \(-49/8\). Since that ordinate is a unit, this abscissa difference measures the formal depth of \([N\mp\tau]A\). Work over a place of \(\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-2})\) above \(p\); it is unramified. If \(p\nmid N^2+2\), the complementary endomorphism \([N\pm\tau]\) has unit differential because its degree is \(N^2+2\). The depth stays the same on passing to \([N^2+2]A\).

In each case a positive integer multiple \([k]A\) has depth one when the prime occurs simply in \(T_N\). Its reduction order divides \(k\), and multiplying a formal point over \(\mathbf Q_p\) cannot lower its depth. Hence \([r_p]A\) already has depth one. The product of the possibly exceptional simple primes is \(O(N^2+2)\). Since every exponent at least two pays for its radical inside \(T_N^{1/2}\), this proves
\[
 \log\operatorname{rad}(T_N)\le4\log H+\tfrac12\sum_{p\mid T_N,\ p\text{ counted in }W}\log p+O(\log(2N)).
\]
For \(q_N\le2-\delta\) the left side is at least \((8\log H+O(1))/(2-\delta)\). Consequently the prime sum is \(\gg_\delta N^2\) at each such sufficiently large index.

## How often a prime can contribute

For a fixed \(p>7\) choose one place above it in the CM field. At every \(N\) with \(p\mid T_N\), the point \([N]A\) reduces into the fixed set \(\{O,J,A,-A,\tau A,-\tau A\}\). This statement also holds for primes dividing \(N^2+2\); only the formal-depth implication needed an exception there. Each target in the cyclic subgroup generated by \(A\) gives one congruence class modulo \(r_p\). Thus each prime can contribute at most \(6(1+M/r_p)\) times on \([M,2M]\). Finitely many primes \(p\le7\) contribute only \(O(M)\) to the sum across this interval.

For a contributing prime, \(\log p\ll M^2\) by the size of \(T_N\). Independently \(\log p\ll r_p^2\), by the denominator of the reduced abscissa of the nonzero point \([r_p]A\) and the quadratic height formula on a fixed elliptic curve. Therefore the total contribution of one prime is
\[
 O\big((1+M/r_p)\min(M^2,r_p^2)\big)=O(M^2).
\]
Summing the lower bound over the indices with \(q_N\le2-\delta\) now proves the cardinality estimate. If \(W(X)/\sqrt{\log X}\) has liminf zero, take \(M=\lfloor\sqrt{\log X/C}\rfloor\) along a sequence realizing that liminf. The proportion of odd indices in those intervals with \(q_N\le2-\delta\) tends to zero for every fixed \(\delta>0\). This proves the second alternative. Heights tend to infinity with these indices, so the resulting counterexamples, if this alternative occurs, would include infinitely many distinct triples.
