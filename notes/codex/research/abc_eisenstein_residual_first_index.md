# Prescribed first-index powers in the residual quartics on varying twists

Fix a nonempty finite set \(S\) of sufficiently good primes, and assign to each \(p\in S\) one of the two residual quartics \(F_\pm\). Require that its pullback \(H_\pm(s,t)=F_\pm(2s^4-t^4,t^4)\) have a projective root modulo \(p\). The same conclusion holds for any finite set of primes splitting completely in a fixed splitting field, apart from its finitely many exceptions. Put \(D=\prod_{p\in S}p\).

There are infinitely many common depths \(e\to\infty\) for which the prescribed quartics have **exact** valuation \(e\) at their assigned primes, already at index one on a rational quartic twist. The associated primitive triples satisfy
\[
 U^2+3V^2=C=W^{12},\qquad
 \frac{C}{\operatorname{rad}(3UVW)}\gg_S(\log C)^{2+\delta_S}
\]
for an effective \(\delta_S>0\). The marked quartic primes alone yield the displayed gain after paying a fixed norm radical. Every prime in \(W\) is \(1\bmod24\), every prime in \(U\) is \(\pm1\bmod24\), and \(24\mid V\). The twists escape every fixed finite set even when \(S\) is a single fixed prime. This construction addresses the residual quartics themselves; the corresponding first-index construction for the denominator at an arbitrary specified prime uses smooth integer endpoints. [Denominator construction](abc_eisenstein_first_index_varying_twists.md)

## The norm charge of degree two

Take a primitive parameter \((s,t)\), with \(s\) odd and \(t\) even, and put
\[
    x=s^4-t^4/2,\qquad y=t^4/2,\qquad
    \beta=x+y\sqrt{-3}=s^4+\omega t^4 .
\]
Here \(\omega=(-1+\sqrt{-3})/2\), \(W=N(\beta)=s^8-s^4t^4+t^8\), the two coordinates \(x,y\) are coprime and \(W\) is prime to 6. Primitivity, the prime congruences and \(24\mid V\) follow by taking the coefficients of \(\beta^{12}\). If \(H=\max(|s|,|t|)\), then \(C\asymp H^{96}\). [Eisenstein transfer and prime congruences](abc_eisenstein_power_conic.md)

The original reduced support \(G(x,y)\) has degree 26 and is separable on the projective line. Among its distinct factors are \(y\) and \(x+y\), and its quartic factors are \(F_0,F_+,F_-\). Pullback by \((s:t)\mapsto(2s^4-t^4:t^4)\) has degree four. It branches to order four only on the axes, whose images \((-1:1),(1:0)\) are the roots of \(x+y,y\), respectively. Consequently the squarefree homogeneous product of the pullback support has degree \(4\cdot26-2\cdot3=98\). Fixed normalization factors cost an absolute constant in the radical bound. This reduced product contains
\[
                 J_0(s,t)=s^2+t^2
\]
because \(x-y=s^4-t^4\). Each \(H_\pm\) is squarefree of degree 16, disjoint from \(J_0\) and from the other quartic layers; the corresponding original quartics have no zero at either branch value. [Factorization](abc_eisenstein_twelfth_large_squares.md)

Suppose \(p^e\mid H_{\pm(p)}(s,t)\) for all \(p\in S\). Since the primes are distinct, an upper bound for the radical of each marked form is its absolute size divided by the product of \(p^{e-1}\) assigned to that form. The two quartic values have size \(O(H^{16})\) each. All other support away from the norm has total degree \(98-2-32=64\). Thus for nondegenerate parameters
\[
                \frac C{\operatorname{rad}(3UVW)}
                          \gg \frac{D^{e-1}}{\operatorname{rad}(J_0(s,t))}.
                 \tag{1}
\]
The powers of two relating \(H_\pm\) to the actual values \(F_\pm(x,y)\) disappear at our good primes.

## Prescribing the roots with a fixed norm radical

Choose an integral Gaussian seed \(\alpha_0\) corresponding to the selected projective root at each \(p\mid D\), also \(\alpha_0\equiv1\bmod6\). This is possible by CRT. Its norm is a unit at each marked prime once primes dividing the fixed resultants \(\operatorname{Res}(J_0,H_\pm)\) are excluded. Exclude also the finitely many discriminant primes. Take three generators
\[
        \eta_i=1+6D m_i\, i_{\mathbb C}\in\mathbb Z[i_{\mathbb C}],
        \qquad i_{\mathbb C}^2=-1,
\]
where \(m_1=1\) and each subsequent \(m_i\) is a positive multiple of all earlier generator norms. These norms are odd, pairwise coprime, greater than one, and prime to \(6D\). A generator and its conjugate are coprime because their sum is 2. Hence each has a private split prime ideal establishing multiplicative independence of the \(\eta_i/\bar\eta_i\). Take \(s+ i_{\mathbb C}t\) to be the primitive projective representative of \(\alpha_0\prod_i\eta_i^{u_i}\), \(u_i\in\mathbb Z\). Negative powers can be represented with conjugates, at the cost of rational scalars. The radical of \(s^2+t^2\) is bounded in terms of the fixed seed and generator norms, and all local classes and parity requirements survive primitive reduction.

For clarity, let \(\Lambda_j\subset\mathbb Z^3\) be the exponent kernel modulo the projective Gaussian groups at all \(p^j,\ p\mid D\). The local principal group has order \(p^{j-1}\), generated by the first generator since its projective depth is exactly one. Their orders are coprime across primes. Hence \(\det\Lambda_j=D^{j-1}\) and \(D\Lambda_j\subset\Lambda_{j+1}\subset\Lambda_j\). A local unit-norm projective point is a freely transitive orbit of the group of projective units, so its lifts above the seed form a freely transitive orbit of the principal group. Each tuple of local lifts is therefore one coset of \(\Lambda_j\). At depth \(j=e+1\), pick the lift of a simple root modulo \(p^e\) that ceases to be a root modulo \(p^{e+1}\) at every marked prime. There are \(p-1\) choices at each.

The successive-minima argument for simultaneous principal lifts applies unchanged to this Gaussian group. Briefly, infinitely often \(\lambda_3(j)\le D\lambda_2(j)\), since a persistent larger gap would yield a fixed rational plane. Every nonzero integral direction in that plane has combined projective order \(\gg D^j\), by the fixed finite initial depths at every marked prime. Minkowski's second theorem in the plane and the ambient space would then bound \(\lambda_3(j)\), although even \(\lambda_1(j)\to\infty\). At one marked prime the fixed-place logarithm theorem of Yu gives \(\lambda_1(j)\gg D^{\kappa j}\) for some effective \(\kappa>0\), by multiplicative independence. Thus along those depths
\[
              \lambda_3(j)\ll D^{(1-\kappa)j/2}
\]
and every coset has a representative with \(\|u\|_\infty\le3\lambda_3(j)/2\). The logarithmic height of its primitive Gaussian coordinates is \(O_S(1+\|u\|_\infty)\). Combining with (1) gives \(\delta_S=2/(1-\kappa)-2>0\), reducing \(\kappa\) if necessary. [Full successive-minima proof and fixed-prime logarithm estimate](abc_cm_pell_higher_rank_crt.md)

For every prescribed exact common depth, even away from that subsequence, the first generator alone runs through the entire principal group. This gives \(\log C\ll_S D^e\) and \(C/\operatorname{rad}(3UVW)\gg_S\log C\). Along the improved subsequence one may also move the marked primes by a diagonal choice: given any sequence of nonempty finite admissible sets and assignments, take sufficiently large depths at stage \(j\) to achieve \(C/\operatorname{rad}(3UVW)\ge(\log C)^2(\log\log C)^j\).

## The twisting curve and the limit of a second fixed square layer

Define \(d,z\) by \(F_0(x,y)=d z^2\) with signed squarefree \(d\). At a marked prime \(F_0\) does not vanish, by disjointness of the good reduced forms. The point
\[
       P_d=\big(d(x/y)^2,\ d^2(x/y)z/y^2\big)
       \quad\text{on }Y^2=X^3-18dX^2+9d^2X
\]
produces this parameter at its first index, with the sign of the rational lift \(x/y\) as constructed; reversing that sign interchanges \(F_+,F_-\) but preserves their product. It is nontorsion once \(H\) is large because \(\hat h(P_d)=h(x/y)+O(1)=4\log H+O(1)\) uniformly via the fixed untwisted curve. The heights necessarily tend to infinity since the nonzero quartic values attain unbounded valuations.

For fixed \(d\), the parameters would give rational points on \(H_0(s,t)=d Z^2\). The homogeneous form has degree 16 with 16 simple geometric zeros by the same branching argument. After dividing by \(t^{16}\), its smooth projective model is hyperelliptic of genus seven. Faltings gives only finitely many rational directions, so these first-index witnesses escape every finite list of twists. This is stronger than merely choosing to change \(d\). It also allows distinct twists to be selected in the diagonal construction.

At fixed \(S\) and for the fixed Gaussian generators, the contribution from just the assigned primes has a converse upper bound \((1+\log C)^{O_S(1)}\). In the coordinate \((s+it)/(s-it)\) the marked quartic pullbacks have simple algebraic roots and no zero at zero or infinity. A large valuation is, up to a bounded constant, the proximity to one such root at a fixed place. Yu's theorem bounds it by \(O_S(\log(2+\|u\|_\infty))\); the private prime valuations give \(\|u\|_\infty\ll_S1+\log C\) by logarithmic height. [Detailed fixed-generator argument](abc_cm_pell_higher_rank_crt.md)

Thus the marked powers are genuinely first-index and pay for the complete radical bound, but this fixed-prime certificate grows as a power of \(\log C\). A fixed positive exponent in \(C\) is still unproved.

## Preserving each assigned sign with positive slice coordinates

The following real restriction ensures that \(x,y\) are both positive in the formula for the individual quartics, even when different primes have different assignments. It allows the same primes and the same originally chosen local roots. Write \(r_p=s/t\bmod p\) for each root. The excluded bad primes ensure that none of these is \(0,\infty,1,-1\): the quartics are nonzero at the four directions over \(\mathbb Q\). Choose by CRT a positive integer \(c\equiv1\bmod24\), prime to \(D\), such that \(c(r_p+1)/(r_p-1)\) is a nonzero square modulo every \(p\in S\). Set
\[
       s=(a^2+c b^2)/2,\qquad t=(a^2-c b^2)/2 .
\]
Choose primitive odd \(a,b\) with \((a,c)=1\). Then \(s\) is odd, \(t\) even and \((s,t)=1\); a common odd prime would divide \(a\) and \(cb\). Also \(s\ge|t|\), so \(x=s^4-t^4/2>0\) and \(y=t^4/2>0\) off the finitely many directions \(t=0\). The inverse root equation \((a/b)^2=c(r_p+1)/(r_p-1)\) is simple modulo each assigned prime.

The additional degree-two map is branched over the two values \(s/t=1,-1\), both already among the zeros of \(s^4-t^4\) in the previous reduced support. Hence its reduced pullback degree is \(2\cdot98-2=194\), with \(C\) comparable to \(\max(|a|,|b|)^{192}\) for fixed \(c\). The quadratically marked norm is now \(a^2+cb^2\), the inverse image of \(s=0\); it has two simple roots. The two marked quartic pullbacks now have degree 32 each and remain separable, since they are nonzero at both branch values. These three factors are disjoint.

Work in \(\mathbb Z[\sqrt{-c}]\). Choose \(a+b\sqrt{-c}\) initially in the desired root classes, with \(a,b\) both odd and \(a\equiv1\bmod c\). Use the independent generators \(\eta_j=1+2cD m_j\sqrt{-c}\) with \(m_1=1\) and subsequent \(m_j\) divisible by the previous positive norms. These norms are odd and prime to \(cD\); a generator and its conjugate are coprime as ideals of the maximal order, since their sum is 2. Coprimality of the rational norms again provides private split prime ideals and multiplicative independence. The seed norm has 2-adic valuation one and is coprime to \(c\). Multiplication by these generators or their conjugates preserves odd coordinates and \((a,c)=1\), also after division by the coordinate gcd. The local quadratic algebras at \(p\mid D\) are unramified and the first generator has projective depth one, exactly as before. The top-minima proof and whole-radical bound therefore apply with this norm. The pullback of \(F_0\) likewise has 32 simple zeros, giving genus 15 at fixed \(d\) and again forcing the twists to leave every finite list.

On either fixed norm subgroup the twist escape has an effective version. Write \(\gamma=a+\lambda b\) with \(\lambda^2=-c\), including \(c=1\) for the original Gaussian construction, and let \(\mathcal F(a,b)\) be the homogeneous degree \(m=16\) or \(32\) pullback used to define the squareclass of \(F_0\). Fixed square scalings can be absorbed in \(z\). There is a fixed finite set of places \(\mathcal T\) of \(K=\mathbb Q(\lambda)\) containing the archimedean places, coefficient denominators and all prime divisors of the seed and generator norms. Both \(\gamma,\bar\gamma\) are \(\mathcal T\)-units. Put \(u=\gamma/\bar\gamma\) and \(f(T)=\mathcal F((T+1)/2,(T-1)/(2\lambda))\). The polynomial is separable of degree \(m\); the norm-zero directions do not meet this quartic pullback. Now
\[
              f(u)=d\left(z/\bar\gamma^{m/2}\right)^2
\]
with both coordinates \(\mathcal T\)-integral. The explicit hyperelliptic height bounds of Bérczes, Evertse and Győry apply for every fixed nonzero \(d\). Taking a maximum over a finite list of twists gives an effective threshold for \(h(u)\), hence for the triple height by the fixed projective change of coordinate. In particular for each prescribed \(L\) there is an effective height beyond which \(d\) has a prime divisor larger than \(L\), since a squarefree integer supported on primes \(\le L\) belongs to a finite explicit list. [Effective hyperelliptic bounds](https://arxiv.org/abs/1301.7168)

## A prime excluded from the fixed orbit

Take \(s+it=(7+2i)^{2079}\) in the original degree-four transfer. Here \(x=s^4-t^4/2>0,\ y=t^4/2>0\), and the height \(C=W^{12}\) has 172,063 decimal digits. At \(p=239\) one has
\[
    F_+/p^2\equiv105\pmod p,\qquad
    F_-\equiv116\pmod p,\qquad F_0\equiv58\pmod p .
\]
Thus \(F_+\) has exact depth two at the first-index point of this twist. Its squareclass \(d\) is odd. The same prime cannot occur at all in the residual quartics of the original \(d=-2\) orbit: the two rational comparison points on \(v^2=u^3-576u\), \(v^2=u^3-192u\) reduce to \([80]P'=O\) and \([80]Q_*=(198,59)\), respectively, whereas a hit would force \(\operatorname{ord}(Q_*)\mid\operatorname{ord}(P')\). [Rational comparison points and order condition](abc_eisenstein_slice_marked_divisors.md)

For the complete radical write \((x+y\sqrt{-3})^{12}=P+Q\sqrt{-3}\) and
\[
       T=3|PQ|W/|st|^3 .
\]
This integer has exactly the prime support of \(3UVW\): the factor \(12y(x+y)\) in \(Q\) equals \(6s^4t^4\), leaving all divisors of \(st\) after division. Also \(53^{2079}=s^2+t^2\) divides \(x-y=s^4-t^4\), which is a factor of \(Q\) coprime to \(st\) at 53. The marked prime square divides \(P\) and is coprime to \(st\). Thus \(\operatorname{rad}(3UVW)\le T/(53^{2078}\cdot239)\). Integer division gives
\[
     \left\lfloor 10^8 C\,53^{2078}\,239/T\right\rfloor=333477463,
       \qquad C/\operatorname{rad}(3UVW)\ge3.33477463 .
\]
This certificate requires no factorization of the large entries. [Exact standard-library verifier](abc_eisenstein_first_index_239.py)
