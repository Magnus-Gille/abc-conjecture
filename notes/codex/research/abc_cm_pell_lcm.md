# Almost all of the logarithm of the product of the Pell terms survives in their lcm

Use the positive Pell indices and the primitive full-annihilator forms \(D_{q,n}=|F_q(a_n,b_n)|\), where \(b_n+a_n\sqrt2=(1+\sqrt2)^n\). Fix any finite set \(\mathcal Q\) of primes \(q>3\) splitting in \(\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-2})\), for example \(\mathcal Q=\{11\}\). Label every term by its pair \((q,n)\). Call an integer prime power \(p^j\), \(j\ge1\), shared up to \(N\) if it divides two differently labelled terms with \(1\le n\le N\). Then
\[
 \sum_{q\in\mathcal Q,\ n\le N}
       \ \sum_{\substack{p^j\mid D_{q,n}\\p^j\ {\rm shared}\ {\rm up\ to}\ N}}
                \log p=o_{\mathcal Q}(N^2).                                \tag{1}
\]
In particular, if \(L_N\) is the lcm of all these terms, then
\[
 \log L_N=\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q,\ n\le N}\log D_{q,n}+o_{\mathcal Q}(N^2)
   \sim \tfrac12\log(1+\sqrt2)\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q}(q-1)^2\, N^2.          \tag{2}
\]
Moreover \(\log\gcd(D_{q,n},L_{n-1})=o(n)\) along a density-one set of indices, simultaneously for the chosen ranks. The same holds for the entire contribution of previously occurring prime bases having exponent at most any fixed \(k\) in the current term. Indeed it is bounded by \(k\log\gcd(D_{q,n},L_{n-1})\). A density-one set follows from (1) by Markov on each dyadic interval and a decreasing threshold tending to zero. Formula (1) controls repeated occurrences across labels. A square supported at a previously unseen prime still occupies two layers in one label.

## Periods and common prime-power layers

Put \(g=(1+\sqrt2)^4\), \(K=\mathbf Q(\sqrt2)\), and let \(U_q\) be the finite set of multiplicative targets in the [Pell gcd formula](abc_cm_pell_target_gcd.md). Their heights are bounded, their elements are independent of \(g\) individually, and
\[
 D_{q,n}=\left|A_q(1-\sqrt2)^{(q-1)^2n}\prod_{u\in U_q}(g^n-u)\right|.
\]
The vertical bars here give the unsigned integer from the homogeneous form. Fix a common Galois splitting field \(E\) containing the targets and \(K\). Outside finitely many rational primes, it is unramified, all the normalizing constants, targets and differences of distinct targets within each rank are units. For each remaining rational \(p\) fix one place of \(E\) above it. A layer \(p^j\) divides \(D_{q,n}\) precisely when \(g^n\equiv u\bmod p^j\) at this place for one of its targets. The order \(r_{p,j}\) of \(g\) modulo \(p^j\) there is independent of the choice: the two possible embeddings of \(K\) send \(g\) to itself or its inverse, and unramified base extension preserves the congruence. There are \(O_{\mathcal Q}(N/r_{p,j}+1)\) incidences at a layer of this order.

Each omitted fixed \(p\) contributes \(O_p(N)\) up to \(N\), even counting all its incidences. Normalize \(v(p)=1\) at one place and let \(d=|U_q|\). A term of valuation at least \(j\) has \(v(g^n-u)\ge(j-v(A_q))/d\) for some \(u\). Two exponents at this same target differ by \(h\) with \(v(g^h-1)\ge(j-v(A_q))/d\). For this fixed \(p\), \(v(g^h-1)\le C_p+v_p(h)\): take a fixed power \(g^t\) sufficiently deep for the local logarithm to be isometric and apply it to \(g^{th}\), observing that \(v(g^h-1)\le v(g^{th}-1)\). Hence there are \(O_p(Np^{-j/d}+1)\) possible exponents. Since the height estimate gives \(j=O_p(N)\), summing proves the assertion.

For all layers visited somewhere on the orbit there is the weighted count
\[
                    \sum_{p,j:\ r_{p,j}\le R}\log p\ll_{\mathcal Q} R^{3/2}. \tag{3}
\]
Indeed at a hit \(u\) lies in the cyclic subgroup generated by \(g\) modulo \(p^j\). Among pairs with both coordinates between 0 and \(\lceil\sqrt R\rceil\), two monomials in \(u,g\) coincide there. Their quotient \(u^a g^b\) differs from 1 globally by individual independence. There are \(O(R)\) possible exponent pairs of size \(O(\sqrt R)\). Take for each difference the absolute norm of its numerator ideal in \(E\). Its log is \(O_{\mathcal Q}(\sqrt R)\), by the height bound. Every layer divides at least one resulting positive integer, since positive valuation at the chosen place to depth \(j\) implies this divisibility in the norm. Thus the total layer weight is bounded by the log of their product. [Earlier period and local depth estimates](abc_pell_full11_small_prime_mass.md)

By partial summation of (3), total incidences at orders \(r_{p,j}\le N\), including unshared layers, have weight \(O_{\mathcal Q}(N^{3/2})\). At every larger order, each layer has at most \(\sum_q|U_q|\) incidences up to \(N\), so it suffices to bound the weight of the distinct shared layers with these orders.

## An integer for each relation between two indices

Take any two differently labelled hits, at indices \(n\le m\) and respective targets \(u\in U_q,v\in U_r\). Given integers \(b\ge1,a\ge0\), let \(h=bn-am\). The common layer divides the norm of the numerator ideal of
\[
                              u^b-g^h v^a.
\]
The same fixed place over \(p\) can be used at both hits. Even for nonsplit real-quadratic reduction, the displayed element vanishes there to at least the specified precision. The expression is nonzero for every target pair, except possibly when \(q=r,a=b,h=0\). That exception would force \(n=m\), contrary to the distinct labels at a single rank, so it can be discarded entirely. For different ranks it is allowed and nonvanishing by independent Galois action. The case \(a=0\) is covered by the independence of \(u\) and \(g\). These nonvanishing assertions are proved by conjugation in the [common-factor argument](abc_cm_pell_target_gcd.md).

The product of these numerator norms over all pairs from the two ranks is a positive integer of log size
\[
                              O_{\mathcal Q}(1+a+b+|h|).                     \tag{4}
\]
All layers arising from this triple \((a,b,h)\) divide that same integer. Thus the sum of their distinct layer weights is bounded by (4), even when a given label pair realizes large multiplicities. Take finite unions over pairs of ranks in what follows.

## Covering the range of slopes

Write \(x=n/m\in(0,1]\). Fix \(0<\eta,\delta<1\). For almost every \(x\) are infinitely many positive denominators \(b\) and coprime positive numerators \(a\) with \(|x-a/b|<\eta/(2b^2)\), by the metric theorem on unbounded partial quotients or Khintchine's theorem. The open intervals \(I=(a/b-\eta/b^2,a/b+\eta/b^2)\) therefore cover this full-measure set in the Vitali sense. The intervals containing each such \(x\) shrink to zero; one may keep them inside \((0,1)\). The Vitali covering theorem gives a disjoint subfamily covering almost all of \((0,1)\). Keep finitely many whose complement \(E_\delta\subset[0,1]\) has length at most \(\delta\). The disjointness gives \(\sum_I b^{-2}\le 1/(2\eta)\).

For an interval in this fixed family the integer \(h=bn-am\) satisfies \(|h|\le\eta N/b\). By (4), the total possible layer weight summed over the integers \(h\) is at most
\[
                O_{\mathcal Q}(\eta^2N^2/b^2)+O_{\mathcal Q,\eta,a,b}(N+1).
\]
All intervals together cost \(O_{\mathcal Q}(\eta N^2)+o_{\mathcal Q,\eta,\delta}(N^2)\). When summing (4), omit the impossible case \(q=r,a=b,h=0\).

On \(E_\delta\) choose a Dirichlet approximation with \(1\le b\le Q=\lceil\sqrt N\rceil\), \(0\le a\le b+1\), \(|bx-a|\le1/Q\). Then \(|h|\le N/Q\) and \(a/b\) is in the \(1/Q\) enlargement of \(E_\delta\). This is a union of at most a fixed number \(T=T(\eta,\delta)\) of intervals of total length at most \(\delta+2T/Q\). For fixed \(b\), there are at most \(b(\delta+2T/Q)+2T\) such numerators. Summing over \(b\) gives \(O(\delta N)+O_T(\sqrt N)\) pairs \((a,b)\), without any restriction on the earlier rational direction. For each pair, (4) summed through \(|h|\le N/Q\) costs \(O_{\mathcal Q}(N)\). In particular a zero numerator near the endpoint 0 is included. The limsup of the long-period shared weight divided by \(N^2\) is consequently \(O_{\mathcal Q}(\eta+\delta)\). Both parameters are arbitrary, proving (1). Finally \(\log D_{q,n}=(q-1)^2 n\log\varepsilon+O_q(1)\) by the archimedean size of the definite full form, which proves (2).

## The private-square obstruction to a counting deduction

Pairwise coprimality, the correct exponential size, and the CM splitting restriction alone can hold together with a private exponential prime square at every index. For example, fix \(c>0\) and take the first rational prime \(P_n\) above \(e^{cn/2}\) which splits completely in the fixed CM torsion and real quadratic field. The prime ideal theorem or Chebotarev with fixed modulus gives \(P_n=e^{cn/2}(1+o(1))\); the primes are distinct for all sufficiently large \(n\). The integers \(P_n^2\) then satisfy \(\log P_n^2=cn+o(1)\) and their lcm equals their product after the finitely many initial indices. This example is an abstract test on what the size, splitting and collision conclusions imply. It supplies no Pell-orbit square. For the actual Pell values (1) gives a restriction on shared prime powers while leaving the frequency of private exponential squares unresolved.
