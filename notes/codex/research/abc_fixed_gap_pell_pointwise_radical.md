# A pointwise radical bound for consecutive integers on a Pell orbit

Put \(C=b+1\), \(D=\operatorname{sf}(b(b+1))\), the product of primes of odd valuation, and \(R=\operatorname{rad}(b(b+1))\), the product of all prime divisors. There exist absolute positive constants \(c_0,c_1\) such that for all sufficiently large \(C\),
\[
  D\le(\log C)^{c_0}
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \boxed{\displaystyle\log R\ge c_1\frac{(\log\log C)^2}{\log\log\log C}}.       \tag{1}
\]
All logarithms are natural. In particular, \(R\) exceeds every fixed power of \(\log C\) on each fixed squarefree-kernel orbit.

## The complete orbit

For \(b>0\), \(D>1\), since there are no positive consecutive squares. Write \(b(b+1)=Dz_0^2\). Then \((2b+1)^2-D(2z_0)^2=1\). Let \(\epsilon>1\) be the least positive norm-one unit \(X+Y\sqrt D\) with \(X\) odd and \(Y\) even, in the specified real embedding. The totally positive units satisfying this congruence condition form the subgroup generated by \(\epsilon\). Every positive solution appears once as
\[
  \epsilon^n=X_n+Y_n\sqrt D,\qquad C=(X_n+1)/2,\qquad
  R=\operatorname{rad}(D(Y_1/2) U_n),\qquad
  U_n=\frac{\epsilon^n-\epsilon^{-n}}{\epsilon-\epsilon^{-1}}.
\]
Here \(n\ge1\), \(U_1=1\), and \(h=\log\epsilon\ge\log(3+2\sqrt2)\). Uniformly
\[
     \log C=nh-\log 4+2\log(1+\epsilon^{-n}).
\]
For fixed \(D\), rational abc on this orbit is equivalent to \(\log\operatorname{rad}(U_n)=nh+o_D(n)\), since \(\log U_n=nh+O_D(1)\).

The pair \((\epsilon,\epsilon^{-1})\) is a Lucas pair with product one and nonzero integral sum. The primitive-divisor theorem gives a primitive prime of \(U_d\) for every \(d>30\). Such a prime avoids the discriminant and has rank \(d\). The quotient \(\epsilon^2\) has order \(d\) in \(\mathbf F_p^\times\) at a split prime or the norm-one subgroup of \(\mathbf F_{p^2}^\times\) at an inert prime. In particular \(p\ge d-1\). The chosen primes are distinct at different ranks and avoid \(DY_1/2\). Because \(U_d\mid U_n\) for \(d\mid n\),
\[
  R\ge\operatorname{rad}(D(Y_1/2))\prod_{\substack{d\mid n\\d>30}}(d-1).         \tag{2}
\]
For large \(n\), at least half the divisors of \(n\) are at least \(\sqrt n\), so \(\log\operatorname{rad}(U_n)\gg\tau(n)\log n\) uniformly. We use this if \(\tau(n)\ge\log n/\log\log n\). Otherwise \(k=\omega(n)\le\log(\tau(n))/\log2\ll\log\log n\). [Bilu, Hanrot and Voutier, *Existence of primitive divisors of Lucas and Lehmer numbers*](https://doi.org/10.1515/crll.2001.033)

## Cyclotomic factors with one or two index primes

Let \(d\mid n\) have one or two distinct prime divisors. Put \(F_d=\Phi_d(\epsilon,\epsilon^{-1})\), where \(\Phi_d\) is the homogeneous cyclotomic polynomial, and put \(z=\epsilon^{-2}\). The positive rational integer \(F_d\) divides \(U_n\). Indeed the homogeneous polynomial for \(d>1\) is symmetric in the conjugate units, and \(\prod_{d\mid n,d>1}F_d=U_n\).

At a prime over any rational prime \(\ell\nmid d\) dividing \(F_d\), reduction of \(\epsilon^2\) has exact order \(d\), since \(T^d-1\) has simple roots in characteristic \(\ell\). The two conjugate choices invert the element and preserve its order. At a ramified prime the two conjugates coincide and the quotient has order one. At split or inert primes the preceding finite-field argument gives \(\ell\ge d-1\). Thus the sets of primes in
\[
             W_d=\sum_{\ell\mid F_d,\,\ell\nmid d}\log\ell
\]
are disjoint over distinct \(d\), and their sum is at most \(\log\operatorname{rad}(U_n)\).

There are \(L\ge2\) and \(s\in\{-1,0,1\}\) such that
\[
      0<\left|F_d\epsilon^{-\varphi(d)}(1-z)^s-1\right|\le5z^L.                  \tag{3}
\]
For \(d=q^e\), put \(m=d/q\). Before subtracting one, the uncorrected expression is \((1-z^d)/(1-z^m)\). If \(e\ge2\), take \(s=0,L=m\). If \(e=1\), take \(s=1,L=d\). Thus \(\log L\ge\tfrac12\log d\).

For \(d=q^e r^f\), with \(q,r\) distinct primes, put \(m=d/(qr)\). The uncorrected expression before subtracting one is
\[
           \frac{(1-z^d)(1-z^m)}{(1-z^{mq})(1-z^{mr})}.
\]
If \(m>1\) take \(s=0,L=m\). The expression is strictly less than one since, writing \(u=z^m\), \(u^q+u^r<u\) and \(u^{qr}(1-u)>0\). Its distance to one is at most \(5u\). If \(m=1\), take \(s=-1,L=\min(q,r)\). The corrected expression exceeds one since \(1-z^{qr}\) exceeds each of the two denominator factors. Its distance to one is at most \(5z^L\). These bounds hold for every \(z\le( (3+\sqrt5)/2)^{-2}<0.15\). In both cases
\[
                  \log L\ge\tfrac12\min(\log q^e,\log r^f).                      \tag{4}
\]
When a component exponent is at least two, that component contributes at least half its logarithm to \(\log m\). In all cases the explicit formulas give \(\log F_d\le dh+1\).

## The logarithmic form

Use Matveev's real multiplicative bound. For positive real algebraic numbers \(\alpha_i\) in a real number field of degree \(D_0\), nonzero integer exponents \(b_i\), and nonzero \(\Lambda=\prod_{i=1}^t\alpha_i^{b_i}-1\), a standard explicit form gives
\[
 -\log|\Lambda|\le1.4\,30^{t+3}t^{4.5}D_0^2(1+\log D_0)
                   (1+\log(tB))\prod_{i=1}^t A_i,
\]
where \(B=\max(1,\max|b_i|)\) and \(A_i\ge\max(D_0 h_{\rm abs}(\alpha_i),|\log\alpha_i|,0.16)\). The factor \(1+\log(tB)\) weakens the commonly stated estimate. [Matveev, *An explicit lower bound for a homogeneous rational linear form in the logarithms of algebraic numbers II*](https://doi.org/10.1070/IM2000v064n06ABEH000314)

Apply this to (3) with \(\epsilon\), the rational primes dividing \(F_d\), and \(1-z\) if \(s\ne0\). Take \(D_0=2\). The first height factor is \(h\), the optional factor is \(O(h)\) by the height inequality for sums, and each rational prime contributes \(2\log\ell\). The exponents and \(t\) are \(O(dh)\). The height factor \(h\) for \(\epsilon\) cancels \(h\) in the bound \(-\log|\Lambda|\ge2hL-\log5\gg hL\). Absorb \(30^t t^{4.5}\) into an absolute exponential in \(t\). Taking logarithms yields the following inequality with absolute constants:
\[
     \log L\le O(1)+\log(2+h)+\log\log(3dh)
                          +\sum_{\ell\mid F_d}\log(C_2\log\ell).                 \tag{5}
\]
The terms for \(\ell\mid d\) cost \(O(1+\log\log(3d))\), since there are at most two. The remaining primes satisfy \(\ell\ge d-1\). The function \(\log(C_2 x)/x\) decreases for large \(x\), so their contribution is \(O(W_d\log\log(3d)/\log d)\), with small \(d\) absorbed into the absolute constant. Consequently, setting \(E_n=C_3(1+\log(2+h)+\log\log(3nh))\) for a suitable absolute \(C_3\), one has
\[
     W_d\gg\frac{\log d}{\log\log(3d)}\max(0,\log L-E_n).                        \tag{6}
\]

## Summing independent contributions

Write \(n=\prod_{i=1}^k q_i^{e_i}\), \(a_i=\log(q_i^{e_i})\), and \(M=\log n\). Suppose \(\log(2+h)\le c_2 M/\log M\), where \(c_2\) is a sufficiently small absolute constant. We may assume the small-divisor-count case \(k\ll\log M\) from (2). Discard each \(a_i<4E_n\). Their total weight is at most \(4kE_n\le M/2\) for all sufficiently large \(n\), since \(\log\log(3nh)=O(\log M)\). Therefore the retained weights sum to at least \(M/2\).

For a retained component take \(d=q_i^{e_i}\). Equations (3) and (6) yield \(W_d\gg a_i^2/\log M\). For two retained components take \(d=q_i^{e_i}q_j^{e_j}\). Equations (4) and (6) yield
\[
                W_d\gg\frac{(a_i+a_j)\min(a_i,a_j)}{\log M}
                        \ge\frac{a_i a_j}{\log M}.
\]
Each selected rank is distinct. Summing the single and double terms gives
\[
             \log\operatorname{rad}(U_n)\gg\frac{(\log n)^2}{\log\log n}.          \tag{7}
\]
The constants are absolute under the displayed restriction on \(h\).

## Enlarging the range of the Pell generator

In fact (7) holds whenever \(\log(2+h)\le c_4\log n\) for a sufficiently small absolute \(c_4>0\). Without the optional factor \(1-z\), inequality (5) holds with the whole term \(\log(2+h)\) removed. In the stated wider range, the error \(E_n\) in (6) for these forms may therefore be replaced by \(E_n'=C_4\log(3M)\), where \(M=\log n\). Again use (2) for \(\tau(n)\ge M/\log M\). In the remaining case \(k\ll\log M\), so discarding all \(a_i<4 E_n'\) loses \(O((\log M)^2)=o(M)\) mass. Increasing \(C_4\) by an absolute factor below has the same property.

If the retained \(a_i\) with \(e_i\ge2\) carry at least \(M/3\) mass, take all their singleton and pair ranks in the preceding proof. Their correction exponent \(s\) is zero, and the same sums with \(E_n'\) give (7). Otherwise the retained prime components with \(e_i=1\) carry weight \(S\ge M/2\) for all large \(n\). Arrange these as \(q_1>\cdots>q_j\), now putting \(a_i=\log q_i\) for this sublist. If \(a_1\ge M/16\), the singleton \(d=q_1\) already gives \(W_d\gg M^2/\log M\) by (6). Indeed \(\log L=a_1\) and the original \(E_n\) is at most \(M/32\) upon choosing \(c_4\) sufficiently small.

It remains to treat \(a_1<M/16\), so \(a_1\le S/8\). Fix \(i\). Pair the indices \(i+1,i+2\), then \(i+3,i+4\), and so on, omitting an unpaired last index. For \(q=q_i\) and the primes \(r,s\) of each pair, the uncorrected factor ratio is
\[
 \frac{F_{qr}}{F_{qs}}\epsilon^{\varphi(qs)-\varphi(qr)}
          =\frac{(1-z^{qr})(1-z^s)}{(1-z^{qs})(1-z^r)} .
\]
It differs from one by at most \(5z^{\min(r,s)}\), using \(z<0.15\). The difference is nonzero since \((1-z^{qr})/(1-z^r)=1+z^r+\cdots+z^{(q-1)r}\) is strictly decreasing in \(r\). This logarithmic form uses only \(\epsilon\) and the rational primes of \(F_{qr}F_{qs}\). Its exponents and number of bases are \(O(nh)\); as in (5) the \(h\) from \(\epsilon\) cancels. The at most three primes dividing \(qrs\) cost \(O(\log M)\) after taking logarithms. All other primes have rank \(qr\) or \(qs\) and are at least \(q\min(r,s)-1\). The argument of (5)–(6) gives
\[
 W_{qr}+W_{qs}\gg
 \frac{\log(q\min(r,s))}{\log M}
              (\log\min(r,s)-C_5\log(3M))
 \gg \frac{a_i\min(\log r,\log s)}{\log M},
\]
by taking the original cutoff \(4E_n'\) larger than \(2C_5\log(3M)\). Cancellation of any common rational-prime exponents in the logarithmic form can only decrease their contribution to its upper bound.

Because the weights decrease, the sum of the smaller weights of the chosen pairs after \(i\) is at least \((\sum_{j'>i}a_{j'}-a_{i+1})/2\), taking \(a_{j+1}=0\). Indeed the first weight plus twice the sum in even tail positions dominates the whole tail. All the used unordered index pairs are distinct across the row centers \(i\). Thus adding their \(W\)'s gives, up to absolute factors and the denominator \(\log M\), at least
\[
  \sum_{i<j'} a_i a_{j'}-\sum_i a_i a_{i+1}
      \ \ge\ \tfrac12(S^2-a_1 S)-a_1 S
      \ \ge\ \tfrac{5}{16}S^2 .
\]
This proves (7) for the wider generator range.

Let \(R_K\) be the real quadratic regulator of the maximal order. Its generator with positive first embedding has a totally positive power of exponent at most 32 in \(1+4\mathcal O_K\): first square to ensure total positivity and then use the size at most 16 of the unit group modulo \(4\mathcal O_K\). Every such unit has the prescribed odd and even integral coordinates, including when \(\mathcal O_K=\mathbf Z[(1+\sqrt D)/2]\). Hence \(h\le32R_K\). The real quadratic class-number formula and \(L(1,\chi)\ll\log(2D)\) give \(R_K\ll\sqrt D\log(2D)\). Thus \(\log(2+h)\ll\log(2D)\). As \(\log\log C=\log n+\log h+o(1)\), the range in (1) with \(c_0\) sufficiently small implies \(\log n\asymp\log\log C\) and \(\log(2+h)\le c_4\log n\). This proves (1).

## Other integral gaps and the distance to abc

The same conclusion holds for fixed gaps \(a=2,4\), with \(C=b+a\), \(D=\operatorname{sf}(b(b+a))\), and \((a,b)=1\). Write \(b(b+a)=Dz_0^2\). The totally positive norm-one element \(\lambda=(2b+a+2z_0\sqrt D)/a>1\) has integral trace \(2+4b/a\), so lies in the maximal order. The finitely many products with \(D=1\) come from a difference of squares equal to \(a\). Otherwise \(\lambda=\eta^n\) for the least totally positive maximal-order norm-one unit \(\eta>1\). Its trace is at least three, hence \(\eta\ge(3+\sqrt5)/2\), sufficient for (3). Write \(\eta-\eta^{-1}=t\sqrt D\) with \(t\) a positive integer. Then \(4z_0=atU_n\); in particular \(\operatorname{rad}(U_n)\le2\operatorname{rad}(z_0)\). Also \(C=a(\lambda+1)^2/(4\lambda)\), and \(\log\eta\le2R_K\). The proof of (7) and the conversion to (1) therefore apply. For a general fixed primitive gap the trace need not be integral; the corresponding points can lie on translates of a unit orbit without the divisibility property used here.

For fixed \(D\), the desired abc scale in \(\log R\) is \(nh+o_D(n)\); (7) only reaches a scale quadratic in \(\log n\). The argument bounds the collective prime support of each selected part and leaves open high multiplicities at first occurrence.
