# Rational abc triples from short elliptic congruences

Let \(E:v^2=u^3-8u^2+8u\), \(A=(1,1)\), and \(\tau=\sqrt{-2}\) acting by complex multiplication. The abscissa of \([\tau]A\) is \(-1/2\). There is an explicit family of primitive rational abc triples indexed by positive integers \(n\), with finitely many degenerate indices, satisfying
\[
 \log c_n=32\widehat h(A)n^2+O(1).
\]
Outside a fixed finite set of primes, every contact \([n]A\equiv\pm[\tau]A\bmod p\) contributes at least \((d_p-1)\log p\) to the lower bound for \(\log(c_n/\operatorname{rad}(a_nb_nc_n))\); all these contributions may be added simultaneously with one \(O(1)\) error. Here \(d_p\) is the depth of \([r_p]A\), and \(r_p\) is the order of \(A\bmod p\). Both quadratic factors arising from such a contact on the previous parametrization are included. ## The degree and the radical

Write \(z=8((u-3)/(u+4))^2\) and
\[
 G(u)=\frac{7^7z^2(6z+1)}{(z-1)^3(5z+9)(50z^2-345z-48)^2}.
\]
On the \(u\)-line this is a degree-16 Belyi map, ramified only over \(0,1,\infty\). Its special fibers include \(u=0,\infty,4\pm2\sqrt2,-1/2\). For \(P=[n]A\), write \(G(u(P))=j/k\) in lowest terms, omitting special values themselves. The three positive integers \(|j|, |k-j|, |k|\), reordered with the largest labeled \(c_n\), are pairwise coprime and the smaller two add to the largest. If \(u(P)=X/Z\) is reduced with \(Z>0\) and \(H=\max(|X|,Z)\), then \(c_n\asymp H^{16}\) by the usual height law for a fixed rational map. More explicitly the two homogeneous output forms have fixed nonzero resultant, bounding the canceled gcd, and the maximum of their real absolute values on the unit box boundary is bounded above and bounded away from zero. Also \(\log H=2\widehat h(A)n^2+O(1)\).

The union of the three special fibers on the \(u\)-line has exactly 18 geometric points by Riemann–Hurwitz. Its four points \(0,\infty,4\pm2\sqrt2\) have primitive product of linear factors \(ZX(X^2-8XZ+8Z^2)\). Let \(H_{14}(X,Z)\) be a squarefree integral homogeneous form for the remaining 14 points, obtained by clearing the fixed denominators. It has \(2X+Z\) as a factor. If \(Z=B^2\) and \(v(P)=Y/B^3\), the curve equation gives \(X(X^2-8XZ+8Z^2)=Y^2\). Thus the integer
\[
 T=|BY H_{14}(X,Z)|\ll H^{16}
\]
contains every prime of \(a_nb_nc_n\) outside a fixed finite set. This follows by factoring the numerator, denominator and their difference as fixed homogeneous forms. Accordingly \(\operatorname{rad}(a_nb_nc_n)\le C\operatorname{rad}(T)\) with a fixed constant. Every good \(p>7\) with \(e=v_p(2X+Z)>0\) supplies at least \((e-1)\log p\) to \(\log(T/\operatorname{rad}(T))\) outside the finite set. Since \(T\ll c_n\), the same sum is a lower bound for the triple's log radical loss up to a constant.

If \([n]A\equiv\pm[\tau]A\) at an odd place over \(p>7\), the abscissa difference is a local parameter since \(v([\tau]A)^2=-49/8\) is a unit. Also \(p\nmid Z\). Hence \(v_p(2X+Z)\) is the formal depth of \([n\mp\tau]A\). The reduction group has even order strictly below \(2p\), so \(p\nmid r_p\). Applying \([r_p]\) preserves this formal depth; commuting the endomorphisms shows that it is at least \(d_p\). This proves the assertion about contacts.

## How much an individual prime can cost

Suppose \([\tau]A\) in the residue group belongs to the cyclic subgroup generated by \(A\). Define \(h_p\) as the least absolute value of an integer \(n\) such that \([n]A\equiv [\tau]A\) at a place above \(p\). The sign of the place or endomorphism has no effect on this least absolute value. Applying the conjugate endomorphism gives \(r_p\mid h_p^2+2\), so \(\sqrt{r_p-2}\le h_p\le r_p/2\). For composite \(r_p\) it matters which square root of \(-2\) modulo \(r_p\) actually acts on the given point.

An infinite sequence with \((d_p-1)\log p\ge\delta h_p^2\), for fixed \(\delta>0\), would yield a rational abc disproof. The indices \(h_p\) must be unbounded, since each fixed abscissa difference is nonzero and has finitely many numerator primes. In particular, for primes with \(h_p=O(\sqrt{r_p})\), it suffices that the excess be a fixed positive multiple of \(r_p\). This improves the height cost from \(O(r_p^2)\) on such a sequence. Infinitely many qualifying primes have not been established.

At \(p=205883\), the ordinary reduction order is \(34387=137\cdot251\). The two possible residues \(n\) for the abscissa \(-1/2\) are \(8443\) and \(-8443\bmod34387\), and \((8443^2+2)/34387=2073\). Thus the least positive index for this contact is **8443**. At that index the valuations of \(2u+1\), \(z-8\), and \(1-G(u)\) are all exactly two. In the original octic parametrization the companion \(3s^2-8st+6t^2\) has valuation two and \((s+t)^2+t^2\) has valuation zero. The new primitive triple has one entry exactly divisible by \(205883^2\). [Local arithmetic verifier](abc_short_cm_contact_verify.py)
