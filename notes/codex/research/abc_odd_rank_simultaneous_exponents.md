# Valuations escaping every exponent simultaneously on the odd scalar orbit

Take the positive square root \(B_n\) of the reduced abscissa denominator of \([n]R\) for \(R=(-8,16)\) on \(y^2=x^3+16x^2+32x\). Fix \(m\ge1\) and \(T\ge1\). Except at a set of odd indices of upper Banach density zero, the following statement holds **simultaneously for every integer \(k\ge2\)**. There are at least \(m\) distinct rational primes \(p>T\) for which \(k\nmid v_p(B_n)\). Each witness can be required to have exact CM annihilator \((t)\) for some inert prime divisor \(t\) of \(n\).

Here upper Banach density zero means that the fraction of exceptional indices in intervals of \(L\) consecutive odd integers tends uniformly to zero as \(L\to\infty\). In particular, the odd indices at which \(B_n\) is any nontrivial perfect power have upper Banach density zero. Also, on integers supported on any fixed finite set of odd primes, the same simultaneous assertion holds for every sufficiently large \(n\), if the witnesses may have full annihilator a prime power dividing \(n\). The witnesses in these statements occur in \(B_n\) by divisibility from lower ranks. At a varying prime index the only nontrivial scalar divisor is the index itself, and this density argument gives no restriction on the exceptional set within those indices.

## Density proof

The fixed-exponent covering lemma for the origin states that for every fixed \(k\ge2\), all sufficiently large scalar indices \(t\) have a prime \(p\) of positive valuation in \(B_t\) not divisible by \(k\). It follows from Faltings on finitely many fixed twists for this \(k\). In particular \(B_t>1\) for all sufficiently large \(t\). For an inert prime index \(t\) (the classes 5 and 7 modulo 8), each such prime \(p\) has exact scalar order \(t\) and full CM annihilator \((t)\), since \((t)\) is prime in the CM ring and \(R\) is integral. Moreover \(p\ge2t^2+1\) by Frobenius. At multiples of \(t\) prime to \(p\) the formal depth of \([t]R\) persists unchanged. [Covering lemma and formal depth](abc_primitive_denominator_twists.md) · [Frobenius and annihilator](abc_odd_rank_quadratic_prime_barrier.md)

Given \(\eta>0\), take a large finite list \(J\) of inert primes \(t\), all above any prescribed starting threshold. The relative density among odd integers divisible by fewer than \(m\) primes in \(J\) can be made smaller than \(\eta\). Indeed these divisibility events are independent on a complete period, and their means \(1/t\) sum without bound by Dirichlet's theorem on reciprocal sums in progressions. The variance is at most the mean, so Chebyshev gives the conclusion. For one chosen witness \(p_t\mid B_t\) per \(t\), the proportion of integers with both \(p_t\mid n\) and \(t\mid n\) for at least one \(t\in J\) is at most \(\sum_{t\in J}1/(t p_t)\). This is below \(\eta\) on taking the starting threshold large enough. On the complement of these two periodic exceptional sets, the \(m\) witnesses have distinct scalar orders and retain their initial exponents at \(n\). The starting threshold can ensure \(p_t>T\).

First make these choices without an exponent restriction, and put \(K=\max_{t\in J}v_{p_t}(B_t)\). The resulting periodic good set works for every \(k>K\), since the surviving positive depths are all at most \(K\). For each of the finitely many \(2\le k\le K\), make new choices using the fixed-exponent lemma, with errors smaller than \(\eta/(1+K)\) in each of the two preceding estimates. Intersecting the finite collection of good sets yields a periodic set of relative density at least \(1-4\eta\) on which the assertion holds for every \(k\). The proportion occupied by a fixed periodic set in a length-\(L\) interval converges uniformly to its period average. Letting \(\eta\) tend to zero proves the stated upper Banach density.

## All sufficiently large indices on fixed finite prime support

Fix the support \(S\). At every sufficiently large \(t=s^j\) for \(s\in S\), the fixed-support primitive covering theorem supplies a prime \(p\) of full CM annihilator \((s^j)\) with valuation not divisible by any *one prescribed* \(k\). Taking \(k=2\) first, choose \(m\) such indices for each \(s\), with all witnesses outside \(S\) and larger than \(T\). They are distinct at different orders. Let \(K\) be the largest of their finitely many positive valuations. For each fixed \(2\le k\le K\) choose \(m\) more such prime powers for each \(s\), with witnesses of valuations not divisible by this \(k\), again outside \(S\) and above \(T\). There is a common maximum exponent \(J\) among all selected powers. Every sufficiently large \(S\)-supported \(n\) is divisible by \(s^J\) for some \(s\in S\). The selected witnesses at those powers of \(s\) retain their valuations since none divides \(n\). The first set handles all \(k>K\) at once, and the other finite sets handle all smaller \(k\). [Fixed-support primitive covering theorem](abc_primitive_denominator_twists.md)

The full-annihilator part at \(n\) itself admits a related dichotomy for fixed \(S\). Let \(C_n\) be this part and let \(g_n\) be the gcd of its exponents. For each fixed \(J\), at sufficiently large \(n\) supported on \(S\), either \(g_n=1\) or every prime factor of \(g_n\) exceeds \(J\), by applying the primitive covering theorem at each prime exponent at most \(J\). Thus along any sequence of actual powers \(C_n\) on this support, \(g_n\to\infty\) and \(\log\operatorname{rad}(C_n)=o(n^2)\). The exact mass formula gives \(\log C_n\ge (\kappa_S-o(1))hn^2\), where \(h=\widehat h(R)\) and \(\kappa_S=\prod_{\lambda\mid\prod_{s\in S}(s)}(1-1/N\lambda)>0\), with prime ideals \(\lambda\) in the CM ring. In the octic construction, \(\log c_n=8hn^2+O(1)\) and the radical loses at least the repeated part in \(C_n\) from a product of size \(O(c_n)\). Hence these powers would give a lower limiting abc quality of at least \(8/(8-\kappa_S)\). This last consequence treats an assumed infinite sequence.
