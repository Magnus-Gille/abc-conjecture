# Counting smooth fractions and the size of a forced abc gain

Curtis Bright proved that infinitely many primitive triples with \(a+b=c\) and \(R=\operatorname{rad}(abc)\) satisfy
\[
\log(c/R)>6.563\,\frac{\sqrt{\log c}}{\log\log c}.
\]
This exceeds the logarithmic lower bound on \(c/R\) from the 631 family. A disproof would require \(\log(c/R)\ge\eta\log c\) infinitely often for a fixed \(\eta>0\). The displayed inequality is a lower bound. I checked the published statement against the repository’s earlier full-text audit. Direct access to the paper returned HTTP 403 in this session. The argument below treats a specific counting construction independently. [Bright, *A new lower bound in the abc conjecture*](https://doi.org/10.4153/S0008439523000784) · [Earlier source check](../../../notes/claude/reference-audit.md#L526) · [631 family](abc_lifting_631.md)

## An optimized count

For a finite set \(S\) of odd primes, put \(P_S=\prod_{p\in S}p\). Let \(N_S(H)\) count the reduced positive fractions \(u/v\) formed from these primes with \(u,v\le e^H\). The empty support is allowed. Then
\[
\log\sup_S\frac{N_S(H)}{P_S}
=(8+o(1))\frac{\sqrt H}{\log H}\qquad(H\longrightarrow\infty).
\tag{1}
\]
The division by \(P_S\) charges for all primes allowed in the numerator and denominator. An individual fraction can have smaller support.

Write \(u/v=\prod p^{z_p}\). For \(s>0\) put \(Z_p=\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}p^{-s|j|}=(1+p^{-s})/(1-p^{-s})\). Since \(\log(uv)\le2H\),
\[
\frac{N_S(H)}{P_S}\le e^{2sH}K(s),\qquad
K(s)=\prod_{p\text{ odd}}\max(1,Z_p/p).
\]
For \(Y>3\) choose \(s=\log((Y+1)/(Y-1))/\log Y\), so \(s\sim2/(Y\log Y)\). The function \(Z_t/t\) decreases in the real variable \(t>1\) and equals 1 at \(Y\). Uniformly for odd primes \(p\le Y\), expansion of \(\coth(s\log p/2)\) at zero gives
\[
\log(Z_p/p)=\log(Y/p)+\log(\log Y/\log p)+O(Y^{-2}).
\]
The prime number theorem and partial summation give
\(\sum_{p\le Y}\log(Y/p)\sim Y/\log Y\) and
\(\sum_{p\le Y}\log(\log Y/\log p)=O(Y/\log^2Y)\).
Indeed the two sums, with all primes included, equal the integrals of \(\pi(t)/t\) and \(\pi(t)/(t\log t)\) from 2 to \(Y\). Omitting 2 changes neither leading estimate. Hence \(\log K(s)\sim Y/\log Y\). Taking \(Y=2\sqrt H\) yields the upper bound in (1).

For the lower bound fix \(0<\delta<1\), take \(Y=2(1-\delta)\sqrt H\), and let \(S\) contain every odd prime at most \(Y\). Independently give each \(z_p\) the probabilities \(p^{-s|j|}/Z_p\). The means of \(\log u\) and \(\log v\) are equal to
\[
\mu=\sum_{p\in S}\frac{\log p}{2\sinh(s\log p)}
\sim\frac{|S|}{2s}\sim Y^2/4 .
\]
The variance of each sum is \(O(|S|/s^2)\): summing \(j^2p^{-s j}\) shows that the second moment of each individual positive or negative contribution is \(O(s^{-2})\). Chebyshev's inequality puts both sums in \((1\pm |S|^{-1/4})\mu\) with probability \(1-o(1)\). For large \(H\) this event implies both height constraints. Each vector in the event has probability at most \(\exp(-(2-o(1))s\mu)/\prod Z_p\). Therefore
\[
\log(N_S(H)/P_S)\ \ge\ \log K(s)+(2-o(1))s\mu+o(1)
\sim 2Y/\log Y
\]
where the asymptotic equality applies to the right-hand side. Letting \(\delta\) decrease to zero proves (1).

## What this count guarantees for actual integers

Reduce these odd fractions modulo \(2^n\). There are \(2^{n-1}\) odd residue classes. Choose \(n\) with \(2^{n-1}<N_S(H)\le2^n\). Two distinct fractions agree, and dividing one by the other gives a reduced ratio \(U/V\ne1\) with \(2^n\mid U-V\). Both \(U,V\le e^{2H}\). The primitive triple formed from \(\min(U,V), |U-V|, C=\max(U,V)\) has radical
\[
R\le P_S\,|U-V|/2^{n-1},\qquad C/R\ge N_S(H)/(2P_S).
\]
Using the lower bound in (1), \(C\) tends to infinity along the constructed triples and \(\log C\le2H\). Since \(\sqrt t/\log t\) eventually increases, this proves that for every fixed \(\kappa<4\sqrt2\) infinitely many triples satisfy
\[
\log(C/R)>\kappa\,\frac{\sqrt{\log C}}{\log\log C}.
\]
Bright's published constant \(6.563\) is larger. Formula (1) bounds the count relative to the chosen height budget. A congruence can yield an unusually short relation or leave many primes unused, and either event can improve the actual triple.

There is also a limit on what the cyclic group structure and even the individual orders of its generators can imply on their own. Fix \(S,H,n\). For each \(p\in S\), choose the sign \(\epsilon_p\) making \(\epsilon_p p\equiv1\bmod4\), and put \(b_p=v_2(\epsilon_p p-1)-2\). Modulo \(2^{n+2}\), the principal units \(1\bmod4\) form the cyclic group of order \(2^n\) generated by 5. Replace \(p\) by \(\epsilon_p5^{w_p}\), where the exponents \(w_p\) are independent and \(w_p=2^{b_p}\) times a uniformly random odd residue modulo \(2^n\). This preserves the sign and the actual order of the principal component at every precision through \(2^{n+2}\), by \(v_2(5^k-1)=2+v_2(k)\) for positive \(k\).

For any nonzero admissible vector \(z\), let \(r\) be its smallest supporting prime. Conditional on the other choices, the probability of \(\sum z_pw_p=0\bmod 2^n\) is at most \(2^{1+b_r}|z_r|/2^n\) by counting solutions of a linear congruence with an odd unknown. The full signed relation implies this principal relation. Since \(2^{b_r}\le r\), \(|z_r|\le H/\log3\), and the remaining nonzero coordinates use only \(S_{>r}=\{p\in S:p>r\}\), summing these probabilities gives
\[
\Pr(\text{some nonzero relation of height at most }e^H)
\ \ll\ \frac{(1+H)^2}{2^n}\sum_{r\in S}rN_{S_{>r}}(H)
\ \ll\ \frac{(1+H)^2P_S}{2^n}\sup_T N_T(H)/P_T.
\]
The second inequality uses \(\sum_{r\in S}1/\prod_{p\in S,\ p<r}p\le\sum_{j\ge0}3^{-j}\). Consequently, whenever \(2^n/P_S\ge e^{\eta H}\) with fixed \(\eta>0\) and sufficiently large \(H\), some assignments with the prescribed orders have no such relation. An argument forcing that gain for the actual primes needs to use information about their joint residues.

## Allowing the prime support to vary with every fraction

There is a direct statement using the actual rational numbers while varying their \(2\)-adic target. Fix \(\eta>0\) and let \(E_\eta\) consist of units \(\alpha\in\mathbb Z_2^\times\) for which infinitely many distinct reduced ratios of positive odd \(u,v\), of height \(h=\max(u,v)\), satisfy
\[
|u/v-\alpha|_2\le\frac{1}{\operatorname{rad}(uv)h^\eta}.
\tag{2}
\]
Then \(E_\eta\) has Haar measure zero. More precisely its Hausdorff dimension in the usual \(2\)-adic metric is \(2/(2+\eta)\).

For the upper bound, the sum of the \(d\)-th powers of the covering radii is at most
\[
\left(\sum_{m\ge1}\frac{1}{\operatorname{rad}(m)^d m^{\eta d/2}}\right)^2
=\prod_p\left(1+\frac1{p^d(p^{\eta d/2}-1)}\right)^2<\infty
\]
for \(d>2/(2+\eta)\). Here \(h\ge\sqrt{uv}\) and coprimality makes the radical multiplicative. Tails cover the limsup set. Taking \(d=1\) gives the Haar measure assertion.

For completeness, the lower bound follows already from the smaller balls \( |u/v-\alpha|_2\le h^{-\tau}\) with \(\tau=2+\eta\), since \(\operatorname{rad}(uv)\le h^2\). Here are details of the counting required for this bound. In any fixed unit ball \(B\) specified by one odd residue modulo \(2^k\), there are \(\gg 2^{-k}X^2\) coprime odd pairs \(u,v\in[X/2,X]\) with \(u/v\in B\), for sufficiently large \(X\) depending on \(k\). The implied lower constant is absolute. Möbius inversion over odd common divisors gives main term \(2^{-k}X^2\prod_{p>2}(1-p^{-2})/8\), with error \(O(X\log X)\): for each odd divisor \(d\), count odd \(v/d\) in the scaled interval and one residue class for \(u/d\) per such \(v/d\). Distinct reduced ratios at this scale are at \(2\)-adic distance at least \(X^{-2}\), by the nonzero integer determinant \(uv'-u'v\).

Fix \(0<d<2/\tau\). Give the starting unit ball mass equal to its radius to the power \(d\), and choose successively large scales \(X\). Within each previous ball of radius \(r_0\), put child balls of dyadic radius \(r_1\asymp X^{-\tau}\), \(r_1\le X^{-\tau}\), around the above ratios. Split the parent's mass equally among the children. There are finitely many parents at a given stage. One can choose \(X\) so large that each child mass is at most \(r_1^d\), because it is \(O(1/(r_0X^2))\) and \(\tau d<2\). Choose the height intervals at successive stages disjoint. For a ball of radius \(r_1\le r\le r_0\) meeting the construction, at most one parent meets it. If \(r<X^{-2}\) it meets at most one child. Otherwise separation bounds the number of children it meets by \(O(rX^2)\). In the latter case its mass is \(O(r\,r_0^{d-1})=O(r^d)\), using the parent mass bound. These uniform mass estimates give Hausdorff dimension at least \(d\) for the nested intersection. Let \(d\) increase to \(2/\tau\).

For the particular target \(\alpha=1\), infinitely many pairs satisfying (2) would disprove abc, since \(2^{v_2(u-v)}\ge\operatorname{rad}(uv)h^\eta\) gives \(h/\operatorname{rad}(uv(u-v))\ge h^\eta/2\). The measure and dimension results do not decide membership of this individual rational target. I obtained no fixed-exponent sequence at \(\alpha=1\) and no uniform radical lower bound for general primitive triples.
