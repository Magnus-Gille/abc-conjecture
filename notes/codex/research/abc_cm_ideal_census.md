# Exact first CM denominator depths on the curve of conductor a power of two

Let \(E:v^2=u^3-8u^2+8u\), \(A=(1,1)\), \(K=\mathbb Q(\tau)\), and \(\tau^2=-2\). For an odd finite place \(\mathfrak p\) of \(K\), let \(I_{\mathfrak p}\) be the annihilator in \(\mathcal O_K=\mathbb Z[\tau]\) of the reduction of \(A\). The norm-Euclidean ring \(\mathcal O_K\) is principal, so write \(I_{\mathfrak p}=(\gamma)\). Its first depth is the positive depth of \([\gamma]A\) in the elliptic formal group at \(\mathfrak p\). Changing the sign of \(\gamma\) preserves depth. A primitive generator \(a+b\tau\) satisfies \(\gcd(a,b)=1\). This is equivalent to cyclicity of \(\mathcal O_K/(a+b\tau)\) as an additive group, by the Smith form of the matrix with columns \((a,b),(-2b,a)\).

**Every odd place with primitive annihilator of norm at most 280 has first depth one.** This certificate covers all residue primes of arbitrary size. Its 96 classes up to conjugation contain 403 distinct underlying rational primes. For comparison, at the odd split prime \(p=205883\), the primitive ideal \((167-57\tau)\) of norm 34387 has exact first depth two at one of the places. Thus the smallest primitive annihilator norm with first depth at least two lies in \([281,34387]\). The prime in this upper bound is \(3\bmod8\), and occurs in the \(Y\) factor of the octic orbit. All primes of the other factor \(U\) are \(1\bmod8\). [Octic factor and splitting condition](abc_octic_mixed_skew_denominator.md)

The CSV includes all 136 conjugacy classes through norm 400. Of these, 119 have complete factorizations with 545 distinct proven prime bases, all to first depth one. A question mark records one of the 17 incomplete factorizations. The first is the ideal generated by \(9+10\tau\) of norm 281. Every row with a listed factorization can be verified separately after its exact full denominator and all earlier prime supports have been recomputed, even when a smaller row has an incomplete factorization. The verifier uses Python's exact rational arithmetic and PARI's rigorous primality test. It needs no prime database. Run it using Python with either cypari or cypari2 installed. ## From a complex abscissa to all prime factors at first depth

Choose the complex multiplication map for which
\[
 u([\tau]P)=-\tfrac12\left(u(P)-8+8/u(P)\right),\qquad
 v([\tau]P)=\tfrac\tau4\left(1-8/u(P)^2\right)v(P).
\]
These are the rational two-isogeny with kernel \((0,0)\) followed by the scaling from its target to \(E\) over \(K\). In particular \([\tau]A=(-1/2,-7\tau/4)\) has affine reduction at every odd place. All odd places have good reduction. The point \(A\) has infinite order, for instance \([3]A\) has abscissa \(289/2025\) and lies nontrivially in the torsion-free formal group at 3. [Full orbit](abc_octic_full_mixed_orbit.md)

Compute \(u([a+b\tau]A)=(m+n\tau)/d\) by adding the exact rational point \([a]A\) to \([\tau]([b]A)\), clearing the two rational coefficient denominators with a common positive denominator \(d\). Its denominator ideal is \((d)/((d)+(m+n\tau))\). The norm of the ideal in the divisor equals the gcd of the rank-two minors of the four lattice generators \((d,0),(0,d),(m,n),(-2n,m)\). Therefore the norm of the denominator ideal is exactly
\[
             \frac{d^2}{\gcd(d^2,dm,dn,m^2+2n^2)}.
\]
At any odd denominator place the negative abscissa valuation is twice the formal depth. Define \(B_{a,b}\) as the positive square root of the odd part of this integer.

For primitive \(\alpha=a+b\tau\), the ideal gcd of \((\alpha),(\bar\alpha)\) divides \((\tau)\). Any common odd prime ideal would divide both \(a\) and \(b\). At the ramified prime \((\tau)\), only even \(a\) can contribute, and primitive even \(a\) forces odd \(b\) and \(\operatorname N\alpha\equiv2\bmod4\). If both places above a split odd prime gave positive depth, conjugation and the rationality of \(A\) would place both \(\alpha,\bar\alpha\) in the annihilator at the same place. The same conclusion holds at an inert prime, whose unique place is fixed by conjugation. Bézout then forces \([\tau]A\) to reduce to the identity, contrary to its displayed coordinates. Thus every rational prime dividing \(B_{a,b}\) splits in \(K\), contributes at exactly one place, and has its ordinary integer valuation equal to the full local depth of \([\alpha]A\).

Enumerate \(a,b\ge0\), \(\gcd(a,b)=1\), by increasing \(a^2+2b^2\). This visits each pair of conjugate principal ideals once, up to units. At each row remove from \(B_{a,b}\) all powers of every prime appearing in any earlier \(B\). This requires only gcd and exact division by already known integers, independently of whether those integers were factored. What remains is precisely the product of \(p^{e_{\mathfrak p}}\) over the primes with this exact annihilator up to conjugation. Indeed any place with positive depth has \(I_{\mathfrak p}\mid(\alpha)\). Every divisor ideal is primitive as well, since an integer dividing its generator's two coefficients would also divide those of \(\alpha\). A proper divisor has strictly smaller norm and has therefore been visited. A new prime identifies the exact annihilator, and a rational prime's conjugate place gives just its conjugate annihilator. Consequently distinct rows of equal norm cannot share a new rational prime. Exact prime powers in the retained integer certify the first, unlifted depths. The verifier checks both reconstruction of every factored integer and primality of every proposed base.

## Distinction from first depth at an integer index

If \(I_{\mathfrak p}=(\gamma)\) is primitive of norm \(r\), then \(r\) is the order of the reduction of \(A\) since \(\mathcal O_K/(\gamma)\) is additively cyclic and generated by the class of 1. For \(p\ge7\), good reduction over \(\mathbb F_p\), rational two-torsion, and Hasse's inequality give \(p\nmid \#E(\mathbb F_p)\): the group order is even and less than \(2p\). Thus \(p\nmid r\), and the complementary complex multiplier \(r/\gamma\) has unit differential at \(\mathfrak p\). The depths of \([\gamma]A\) and \([r]A\) agree. At 3 the distinction matters. Although \([3]A\) has formal depth two, the abscissa of \([1+\tau]A\) is \((47+14\tau)/9\), giving \(B_{1,1}=3\) and initial CM depth one.

## An exact exceptional ideal

At \(p=205883\), choose the place \(\tau\equiv 49823\pmod p\). Then \([\tau]A=(102941,67222)\) in \(E(\mathbb F_p)\). Exact finite-field point multiplication gives
\[
 \operatorname{ord}_p(A)=34387,\qquad [167]A-[57][\tau]A=O.
\]
The second relation shows that \((167-57\tau)\) is contained in the annihilator. Its norm, \(167^2+2\cdot57^2=34387\), equals the size of the group generated by \(A\) over the integers. The quotient by the actual annihilator must contain that group, proving equality of ideals. The normalized division polynomials at \(A\) have initial values \(W_0,W_1,W_2,W_3,W_4=0,1,2,-45,868\). Their integer recurrence modulo \(p^3\) gives \(W_{34387}\equiv34977 p^2\pmod {p^3}\). Since \(A\) is integral at a good prime, the abscissa denominator at this index has depth \(v_p(W_{34387})=2\). The complementary multiplier is a unit on the formal group since \(p\nmid34387\). This proves the asserted depth at the primitive ideal.
