# No quartic rational transfer to the conic divisor

Put \(D=\{0,\infty,\pm\sqrt2,\pm\sqrt{-2}\}\). No degree-four rational function over \(\mathbb Q\), branched only above \(D\), has at least three rational points in \(f^{-1}(D)\). The proof uses the cubic resolvent of a quartic cover and explicit functions for the remaining ramification types. This is a finite-degree obstruction. [Cubic covers, discriminant Jacobians and quadratic normalization](abc_conic_cubic_transfer_maps.md)

## Decomposable covers

A geometrically imprimitive transitive quartic monodromy group is cyclic of order four, dihedral of order eight, or the regular Klein four group. In the first two cases there is a unique system of two blocks of size two; the arithmetic monodromy preserves it. In the Klein case every branch cycle is a double transposition, hence there are three branch values and at least one is rational. The inertia involution there is unique because the geometric group is abelian; arithmetic monodromy preserves the block system given by its orbits. Thus in every case a proper intermediate function field exists over \(\mathbb Q\). Lüroth's theorem gives a composition of two rational quadratic functions.

As proved in the cubic note, a viable outer quadratic normalizes to \(cz^2\) or \(z\pm1/(2z)\), with rational cusps \(0,\infty\). An inner quadratic branched at the rational pair gives two rational points. If it branches at a quadratic conjugate pair \(\alpha,\bar\alpha\), to supply three rational points it must split over both rational cusps. In branch coordinates this requires \(\alpha/\bar\alpha\) to be a square in the norm-one group of \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\). For a pure pair in either \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)\) or \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-2})\), the ratio is \(-1\), which is not a square there. The only remaining quadratic pairs in the outer cusp lists are \(h\pm1/\sqrt2\) for \(h=\pm1\). Their ratio is \((1\pm\sqrt2)^2\) up to inversion. Both its square roots have norm \(-1\). This excludes every decomposition.

## Primitive covers and their branch signatures

Write \(S,H,T,Q\) for cycle types \((2,1,1),(2,2),(3,1),(4)\), and use a dash for unramified. A primitive quartic group is \(A_4\) or \(S_4\), and it acts transitively on the three partitions of four letters into pairs. On these partitions \(S,Q\) are transpositions, \(H\) is trivial, and \(T\) is a 3-cycle. Thus there is a geometrically connected cubic resolvent, defined over \(\mathbb Q\) by the arithmetic action on the same partitions. If \(n_H,n_Q\) count the quartic branch values of those types, the genus of the cubic resolvent is \(1-n_H-n_Q\), by comparison of the two Riemann–Hurwitz formulas. In particular \(n_H+n_Q\le1\).

The following list is exhaustive. Each entry in the second column stands for the same type at both elements of one conjugate pair; the two pairs may interchange. One obtains the list by giving \(S,H,T,Q\) weights \(1,2,2,3\), requiring total weight six, with the last column of branch types counted twice, and requiring \(n_H+n_Q\le1\).

| At \(0,\infty\) | At the two conjugate pairs | Exclusion below |
|---|---|---|
| \(-,-\) | \(S,T\) | Two triple branches and two simple branches |
| \(-,H\) | \(-,T\) | Two double poles |
| \(-,H\) | \(S,S\) | Four-simple-branch cubic resolvent |
| \(-,T\) | \(-,T\) | Three triple branches |
| \(-,T\) | \(S,S\) | One triple pole and four simple branches |
| \(S,S\) | \(-,T\) | Two triple branches and two simple branches |
| \(S,S\) | \(S,S\) | Six simple branches |
| \(S,Q\) | \(-,S\) | Four-simple-branch cubic resolvent |
| \(H,T\) | \(-,S\) | Rational double and triple fibers |
| \(T,T\) | \(-,S\) | Two triple branches and two simple branches |

**Four-simple-branch cubic resolvent.** The cubic has precisely four simple branches, at either all four nonrational values or \(0,\infty\) and one conjugate pair. The discriminant Jacobian has \(j=1728\), whereas the Galois closure would induce a rational 3-isogeny of this Jacobian, impossible by the cubic argument.

**Six simple branches.** The geometric group is \(S_4\), since it is transitive and generated by transpositions. The \(S_3\) quotient of its Galois closure has simple inertia at all six points. Above the discriminant double cover \(Y:y^2=cx(x^4-4)\), its cyclic cubic cover is étale. A geometrically connected cyclic étale cubic cover defines a Galois-stable line in \(\operatorname{Jac}(Y)[3]\): over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\) its eigensheaves are the powers of a line bundle of exact order three, and the subgroup they generate is fixed by Galois. Quadratic twisting preserves existence of such a line, because it multiplies the Jacobian representation by a scalar character. At 7 the curve with \(c=1\) has 8 points over \(\mathbb F_7\) and 78 over \(\mathbb F_{49}\), including infinity. Its Jacobian Frobenius polynomial is \(X^4+14X^2+49\), congruent modulo 3 to \((X^2+1)^2\). Its lack of a linear factor excludes the line.

**Two triple branches and two simple branches.** Over the field of the triple values, send them to \(0,\infty\). Label their triple preimages and unique companions. In a source coordinate putting the first triple point at 0, its companion at 1, and the second triple point at infinity, the function is proportional to \(x^3(x-1)/(x-u)\). Here \(u\) is rational: the four distinguished source points are individually rational over the field of the two triple values, and the only possible additional Galois action interchanges the two triples and the two companions simultaneously. This double transposition preserves their cross-ratio. The two remaining branch values are opposite in the target branch coordinate. Indeed if the triple values are rational this is immediate; if they are \(\pm\alpha\), then \((t-\alpha)/(t+\alpha)\) takes \(0,\infty\) to \(-1,1\), or takes \(\pm i\alpha\) to \(\pm i\). For the normalized function the free critical points are roots of \(3x^2-(4u+2)x+3u\). Eliminating \(x\) gives their value polynomial
\[
 27t^2+(-256u^3+192u^2+6u+4)t+27u^2 .
\]
Trace zero would give \(128u^3-96u^2-3u-2=0\), with no rational root by the rational root test.

**Rational double and triple fibers.** The triple fiber contributes exactly two rational points. The double fiber must split into two rational points to reach three. Put the double fiber over zero and the triple pole at infinity, using \(t\mapsto2/t\) if necessary. Up to scaling the function is \(x^2(x-1)^2/(x-u)\) with \(u\in\mathbb Q\setminus\{0,1\}\). The free critical polynomial is \(3x^2-(4u+1)x+2u\). The coefficient of the linear term in the quadratic for its critical values is proportional to \((2u-1)(32u^2-32u-1)\). It must vanish, so \(u=1/2\); the other factor has nonsquare discriminant. The critical points then lie in \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})\), which cannot supply the required nonrational critical values in \(D\).

**Two double poles.** The \(H\)-type value is rational, so make it infinity. If its poles do not split, at least three rational zeros are needed; hence all four zeros split, as this fiber is unramified. The cubic resolvent then has a completely split fiber over zero. But it is genus zero, branched at just the two conjugate triple values; the completely split fiber gives rational points, contradicting the two-branch cubic argument in the companion note. If the poles split, put them at \(0,\infty\) to write \(f=az^2+bz+c+d/z+e/z^2\). Its other two branch points have index three; hence \(z^3f'=2a(z^2+u z+v)^2\). The missing quadratic term forces \(v=-u^2/2\), with \(u\ne0\). Its two critical points lie in \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)\), incompatible with the branch values.

**Three triple branches.** The rational triple branch has rational preimages of orders three and one; put them at infinity and zero as poles. Write \(f=az^3+bz^2+cz+d+e/z\). The other two critical points have index three, so \(z^2f'=3a(z^2+u z+v)^2\). Coefficient comparison gives \(u=b=0,\ c=6av,\ e=-3av^2\). At the critical points \(z^2=-v\) the values are \(d+8avz\), so \(d=0\). A finite zero would require \((z^2/v)^2+6(z^2/v)-3=0\), impossible rationally.

**One triple pole and four simple branches.** Use the same Laurent expression \(f=az^3+bz^2+cz+d+e/z\) with \(ae\ne0\). The finite critical values are a translate and sign change of the roots of the monic-in-\(t\) normalization of \(\operatorname{disc}_z(az^4+bz^3+cz^2+tz+e)\). Thus depressing this last quartic in \(t\) must give a quartic with both quadratic and linear coefficients zero. Its centering translation is \(b(9ac-2b^2)/(54a^2)\).

If \(b\ne0\), substitute \(z=(b/a)w\) and scale the polynomial by \(a^3/b^4\), giving leading coefficients 1,1 and quadratic and constant coefficients \(r=ac/b^2,v=a^3e/b^4\). Taking the resultant in \(v\) of the two suppressed coefficients gives a nonzero constant times
\[
 995328r^6-1492992r^5+839808r^4-237600r^3+38115r^2-3696r+188 .
\]
This polynomial has no root modulo 5 and its leading coefficient is a 5-adic unit. Thus \(r\) cannot be rational. If \(b=0\), the center is zero so \(d=0\). Vanishing of the quadratic coefficient requires \(c=0\) or \(c^2=36ae\). In the former case the fourth powers of the critical values equal \(256ae^3/27=4\), so \(e/a>0\) and \(z^4=-e/a\) has no real solution. In the latter case \(c^2-4ae=(8/9)c^2\) is nonsquare, and the quadratic \(a(z^2)^2+c z^2+e\) has no rational root in \(z^2\). Only the two rational poles remain.
