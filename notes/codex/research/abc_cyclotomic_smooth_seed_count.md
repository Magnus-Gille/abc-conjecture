# Counting smooth cyclotomic values over all coprime bases

Let \(\Phi_n(A,B)=B^{\varphi(n)}\Phi_n(A/B)\) be the homogeneous cyclotomic polynomial. For \(n\ge7\) and \(X\ge2\), let \(N_n(X)\) count all coprime positive integers \(A>B\) for which every prime factor of \(\Phi_n(A,B)\) is at most \(X\). There is no upper bound on \(A\) or \(B\). Put \(D=\varphi(n)\) and \(k=\#\{p\le X:p\text{ prime},\ p\equiv1\pmod n\}\). Then
\[
                  N_n(X)\le (D+1)^k\,2^{16n+48k}.                       \tag{1}
\]
In particular, for every fixed \(C>0\),
\[
     N_n(Cn^2)\le \exp\!\left(O_C\!\left(\frac{n^2}{\varphi(n)}\right)\right).
                                                                            \tag{2}
\]
When \(\omega(n)\le s\), the right side is \(\exp(O_{C,s}(n))\). The same bounds apply to the number of coprime bases for which the entire difference \(A^n-B^n\) has largest prime factor at most \(X\), since \(\Phi_n(A,B)\mid A^n-B^n\). [The radical bound for varying bases](abc_few_index_primes.md)

## The finite places for three factors

Work in \(K=\mathbf Q(\zeta_n)\), where \(\zeta_n\) is a fixed primitive \(n\)-th root of unity. Choose three distinct primitive roots \(\alpha_i=\zeta_n^{j_i}\), \(1\le i\le3\). There are \(D\ge4\) available. For a pair counted by \(N_n(X)\), write \(\gamma_i=A-\alpha_i B\). Each \(\gamma_i\) is a nonzero algebraic integer and one of the factors of \(\Phi_n(A,B)\) over \(K\).

If \(p\nmid n\) divides \(\Phi_n(A,B)\), then \(p\nmid AB\) by coprimality and the leading and constant coefficients of \(\Phi_n\). The reduction of \(A/B\) has order exactly \(n\) in \(\mathbf F_p^\times\). Indeed \(T^n-1\) is squarefree modulo \(p\), so its cyclotomic factors for the divisors of \(n\) have disjoint roots. It follows that \(p\equiv1\pmod n\).

For each of the \(k\) eligible primes choose either that it is absent or one primitive \(n\)-th root \(\lambda_p\in\mathbf F_p\) for the value \(A/B\bmod p\). These choices give at most \((D+1)^k\) assignments. At such a prime, the \(D\) places of \(K\) over \(p\) are identified by the reductions \(\zeta_n\mapsto\eta\), as \(\eta\) ranges over the primitive roots in \(\mathbf F_p\). For each fixed \(i\) and present \(p\), the congruence \(\eta^{j_i}=\lambda_p\) selects exactly one place since \(\gcd(j_i,n)=1\). Thus the three chosen factors use at most three places over \(p\).

For each assignment let \(S\) consist of these selected places, every place over a prime dividing \(n\), and every archimedean place. If \(p^v\parallel n\), the ramification index at \(p\) is \(\varphi(p^v)\). Indeed the extension generated locally by the \(p^v\)-th roots of unity is totally ramified and that generated by the roots of order \(n/p^v\) is unramified. Hence at most \(D/\varphi(p^v)\) places lie above \(p\). The total over \(p\mid n\) is at most
\[
 D\sum_{p\mid n}\frac1{p-1}
 \le D\left(\prod_{p\mid n}\left(1+\frac1{p-1}\right)-1\right)
 =n-D.
\]
There are \(D/2\) archimedean places since \(K\) is totally imaginary. We obtain \(|S|\le n-D/2+3k\). All three \(\gamma_i\) are \(S\)-units. Every difference \(\alpha_i-\alpha_j\) for \(i\ne j\) is also an \(S\)-unit, since distinct \(n\)-th roots of unity stay distinct at primes of residue characteristic prime to \(n\).

## A unit equation that recovers the bases

Put
\[
     u=\frac{\alpha_2-\alpha_3}{\alpha_2-\alpha_1}\frac{\gamma_1}{\gamma_3},
 \qquad
     v=\frac{\alpha_3-\alpha_1}{\alpha_2-\alpha_1}\frac{\gamma_2}{\gamma_3}.
\]
Then \(u+v=1\). The coefficients in the two numerators sum to one after division by \(\alpha_2-\alpha_1\), and their weighted sum with \(\alpha_1,\alpha_2\) is \(\alpha_3\). This proves the identity for \(\gamma_i=A-\alpha_iB\). From \(u\) one recovers \(w=\gamma_1/\gamma_3\), and
\[
                     \frac AB=\frac{w\alpha_3-\alpha_1}{w-1}.
\]
Here \(w\ne1\) since \(B>0\) and \(\alpha_1\ne\alpha_3\). The reduced fraction with positive denominator determines the original ordered pair.

For the fixed assignment, \((u,v)\in(\mathcal O_{K,S}^{\times})^2\). Dirichlet's \(S\)-unit theorem gives this group rank \(2(|S|-1)\). By Theorem 1.1 of Beukers and Schlickewei, a finite-rank subgroup of \((\mathbf C^\times)^2\) of rank \(r\) contains at most \(2^{8(r+1)}\) solutions of \(u+v=1\). Applying it to the product group bounds the number of pairs for this assignment by \(2^{16|S|-8}\). Summing over assignments proves (1). [F. Beukers and H. P. Schlickewei, *The equation x + y = 1 in finitely generated groups*, Acta Arith. 78 (1996), 189–199](https://doi.org/10.4064/aa-78-2-189-199)

## The quadratic cutoff

For \(X>n\), Brun–Titchmarsh gives \(k\le 2X/(D\log(X/n))\). At \(X=Cn^2\) this yields \(k\log(D+1)=O_C(n^2/D)\) as \(n\to\infty\). Taking logarithms in (1) and using \(D\le n\) proves (2). Finally \(n/D=\prod_{p\mid n}(1-1/p)^{-1}\) is bounded in terms of \(s\) when \(\omega(n)\le s\). More generally, for fixed \(b>1\), the same calculation at \(X=n^b\) yields \(N_n(n^b)\le\exp(O_b(n+n^b/\varphi(n)))\). The sieve input is H. L. Montgomery and R. C. Vaughan, *The large sieve*, Mathematika **20** (1973), 119–134.

## Why smoothness of one factor can suffice for a disproof

Suppose $n\to\infty$, $A\to\infty$, $n/\varphi(n)\le K$ for a fixed $K$, and $P^+(\Phi_n(A,B))\le Cn^2$ for a fixed $C>0$. Write $h=\log A$. The logarithm of the product of the prime powers of exact order $n$ in $A^n-B^n$ is $(1+o(1))\varphi(n)h$ uniformly over the coprime pairs. This follows from the uniform Möbius estimate and the bound on imprimitive factors. Brun–Titchmarsh in the class $1\bmod n$ shows that the log sum of these primes counted once is at most
$$
 \frac{2Cn^2\log(Cn^2)}{\varphi(n)\log(Cn)}\ll_{C,K}n=o(\varphi(n)h).
$$
Subtracting, the logarithm of $(A^n-B^n)/\operatorname{rad}(A^n-B^n)$ is at least $(1-o(1))\varphi(n)h$. Consequently
$$
 \frac{\log\operatorname{rad}(AB(A^n-B^n))}{\log A^n}
                    \le 1-\frac{\varphi(n)}n+o(1).
$$
This gives a fixed-exponent disproof if such a sequence exists. If $\varphi(n)/n\to1$, in particular along prime indices, the quality itself tends to infinity. The argument permits arbitrarily large prime factors in earlier orders. [Uniform cyclotomic mass estimate](abc_variable_seed_prime_mass.md)

## Exact finite search over rational bases

With $1\le B<A\le40$ and $2\le n\le300$ there are 489 coprime pairs and 146,211 triples of parameters. Exactly 253 triples have $P^+(\Phi_n(A,B))\le n^2$. The largest index is $156$ at $(A,B)=(2,1)$. Among $A\ge3$ there are 230 such triples and the maximum index is $48$ at $(3,1)$.

Requiring $P^+(A^n-B^n)\le n^2$ for the whole difference leaves 88 triples on 42 coprime pairs. The largest index is $60$ at $(2,1)$, or $36$ at $(3,2)$ when $A\ge3$. Among these 88 the unique maximum of the abc quality is $4\log7/\log210=1.455673100177\ldots$, from $1+2400=2401=7^4$. It is the only one above $4/3$, as can also be checked by comparing $A^{3n}$ and $\operatorname{rad}(AB(A^n-B^n))^4$ with exact integers. A separate enumeration taking a gcd of every full difference against the product of all primes up to $n^2$ reproduced the 88 cases. [Reproduce the search](abc_variable_base_smooth_scan.py)
