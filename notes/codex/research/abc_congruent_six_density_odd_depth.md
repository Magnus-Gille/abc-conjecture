# Many large primes of odd valuation at almost every odd index

On \(E:y^2=x^3-36x\) let \(G=(-3,9)\) and let \(B_n\) be its positive abscissa denominator square root at \([n]G\). Define \(\operatorname{sf}(B_n)=\prod_{v_p(B_n)\ {\rm odd}}p\). Put
\[
 \kappa=\frac32-\frac1{15813}-\frac1{502},\qquad
 \kappa\log2=1.03829616555\ldots .
\]
For every nondecreasing \(L(x)\to\infty\), on a set of relative density one among odd positive \(n\), there are at least \((\log n)^{\kappa\log2-o(1)}\) distinct primes \(p>n^{1/L(n)}\) dividing \(B_n\) to odd valuation. In particular
\[
                   \log\operatorname{sf}(B_n)
                \ge (\log n)^{1+\kappa\log2-o(1)}
\]
on a set of relative density one, by taking \(L(n)=\log\log n\). These valuations concern the actual denominator at \(n\).

## The character

Use \(K=\mathbf Q(i)\), \(\mathcal O=\mathbf Z[i]\), and the [exact Gaussian denominator identity](abc_congruent_six_cubic_packet_character.md). For an odd Gaussian ideal \(I=(a+bi)\) choose a generator with \(a\) odd and \(b\) even, unique up to sign. Set \(D_I=N\mathfrak B((a+bi)G)\). With rational abscissas \(A_j/B_j^2\) in lowest terms one has \(D_I=A_aB_b^2+A_bB_a^2\), including \((A_0,B_0)=(1,0)\). The integers \(A_b\) are squares for even \(b\).

Use the rational prime \(h=2011\), where \(G\) has order \(1006=2v\), \(v=503\). Since \(v\equiv3\bmod4\), the quotient \((\mathcal O/v)^\times/\{\pm1\}\) is cyclic of order \(C=(v^2-1)/2=126504\), generated by \(g=1+6i\). Its odd part is \(M=15813=3^2\cdot7\cdot251\); \(C=8M\). For ideals prime to \(2v\), let \(u(I)\in\mathbf Z/C\mathbf Z\) be the exponent class of the chosen generator modulo \(v\). Choosing parity modulo 2 makes \(u\) additive under multiplication of ideals.

The bit \(f(u(I))\) equals 0 or 1 according as \((D_I/h)\) is \(+1\) or \(-1\). In fact \([a]G\) always has finite reduction because \(a\) is odd. For \([b]G\) finite the character is that of \(x([a]G)+x([b]G)\), since clearing denominators multiplies by a unit square. For \([b]G=O\) modulo \(h\) the bit is zero by the square numerator \(A_b\). The character cannot vanish since \(h\mid D_I\) would imply \([NI]G=O\) modulo \(h\), which is impossible for odd \(NI\). These rules depend only on \(a,b\bmod 2v\) up to common sign, establishing the claimed finite function.

Let \(T\) act on binary functions on \(\mathbf Z/C\mathbf Z\) by \((Tf)(j)=f(j+1)\). The single finite certificate is
\[
  f_* = (1+T^{C/3})(1+T^{C/7})(1+T^{C/251})f,\qquad f_*(1)=1.             \tag{1}
\]
In increasing binary-mask order, with bit positions indexing \(3,7,251\), the eight Legendre symbols are \(-,-,+,+,+,+,+,-\). The [standard-library verifier](abc_congruent_six_density_odd_depth.py) proves the order and generator assertions by finite arithmetic and checks these eight evaluations.

In characteristic two \(X^C-1=(X^M-1)^8\). Decompose the finite function space by the irreducible factors of \(X^M-1\), including their multiplicity eight. Every root of order a proper divisor of \(M\) has order dividing \(M/\ell\) for some \(\ell=3,7,251\). The factor \(1+X^{C/\ell}=(1+X^{M/\ell})^8\) annihilates its entire primary subspace. Thus \(f_*\) is nonzero and supported on the primitive \(M\)-th-root subspaces. Whenever \(M\nmid u\), the operator \(1+T^u\) is invertible on their direct sum, since its defining polynomial is coprime to every corresponding irreducible factor.

## The supply of prime ideals dividing the index

For odd \(n\) let \(m\) be the product of its distinct rational prime divisors outside \(2,3,v,h\). Call a prime ideal \(\lambda\mid(m)\) available when \(u(\lambda)\ne0\bmod M\), and let \(K(n)\) count such prime ideals. For split rational primes the degree-one prime ideals are uniform across the \(C\) exponent classes by the prime ideal theorem in fixed Gaussian ray classes. The parity choice chooses 1 modulo 2, and the remaining sign is quotiented out. The two places over a split rational prime contribute with their multiplicities to the count. Their contribution to its harmonic mean is \((1-1/M)\log\log x+O(1)\). For an inert rational prime, its Gaussian generator is the integer prime itself. The scalar group \(\mathbf F_v^\times/\{\pm1\}\) has odd order \((v-1)/2=251\), so its projection onto the order-\(M\) quotient is injective. Rational primes \(3\bmod4\) are equidistributed modulo \(v\), and the inert contribution is \(\tfrac12(1-1/251)\log\log x+O(1)\). Here one can use Mertens estimates in the indicated fixed progressions and ray classes. The bounded strongly additive function \(K(n)\) therefore satisfies
\[
                 K(n)=(\kappa+o(1))\log\log n
\]
on a density-one set by the Turán–Kubilius inequality. Restricting a density-one estimate to odd integers preserves relative density one.

For each of the three classes \(C/3,C/7,C/251\), select one degree-one prime ideal dividing \((m)\). Also reserve one degree-one prime ideal of each exponent \(2^j\), \(0\le j\le16\). One can take these with all underlying rational primes distinct. Indeed, each fixed ray class occurs among the places above a set of rational primes of positive density, and almost every integer has arbitrarily many prime divisors in each of these finitely many sets, again by Turán–Kubilius. All the selected and reserved ideals are available.

Select one more available ideal, an inert rational prime \(b>n^{1/L(n)}\). For completeness, among \(n\le x\) discard \(n<\sqrt x\) and set \(H_x=\min(L(\sqrt x),\sqrt{\log x})\). The available inert primes in \((x^{1/H_x},x^{1/4}]\) have a reciprocal sum tending to infinity by Mertens in fixed progressions. The corresponding divisor count has variance \(O(1+\text{mean})\) by the elementary second moment, since each pair of primes is at most \(\sqrt x\) in product. Thus almost every remaining \(n\) has a prime in this interval, and it exceeds \(n^{1/L(n)}\).

## From finite differences to actual prime valuations

For a squarefree Gaussian ideal \(I\mid(m)\), the rational Möbius quotient \(\prod_{J\mid I}D_J^{\mu(I/J)}\) has character bit
\[
                 \left(\prod_{\lambda\mid I}(1+T^{u(\lambda)}) f\right)(0).
\]
The signs of the Möbius coefficients agree modulo two. Include in \(I\) the three mandatory degree-one ideals from (1) and the selected large inert prime \(b\). Choose an arbitrary subset of all the other available ideals except the 17 reserved ones. The resulting function before evaluation is a product of invertible operators acting on \(f_*\), hence is a nonzero function \(F\). For some \(0\le k<C\) one has \(((1+T)^kF)(0)=1\). Otherwise all \((T^jF)(0)\), \(0\le j<C\), would vanish, since \((1+X)^k\) form a triangular polynomial basis. There are 17 binary places because \(C<2^{17}\). Include precisely the reserved ideals indexed by the bits of \(k\). In characteristic two, \(1+T^{2^j}=(1+T)^{2^j}\), so their product realizes \((1+T)^k\). Distinct free subsets give distinct \(I\), providing at least \(2^{K(n)-21}\) ideals of negative character.

At a good place \(\mathfrak p\), let \(A\) be the Gaussian annihilator of the reduction of \(G\). Its local denominator at \(J=(\beta)\) is zero unless \(A\mid J\); when \(A\mid J\) it equals the first formal depth plus \(v_{\mathfrak p}(J/A)\). This is the formal logarithm on the unramified Gaussian local field at every \(p\ge5\). At the two bad primes every odd-norm denominator is a unit. Consequently for squarefree \(I\), a nonprimitive local contribution to the Möbius quotient requires exactly \(I=A\mathfrak p\) and \(\mathfrak p\mid(m)\). Discard these at most \(2\omega(n)\) ideals. Also discard the annihilator itself at each of the at most \(2\omega(n)\) good places over primes dividing \(n\). The normal order \(\omega(n)\sim\log\log n\) makes these deletions negligible.

Each remaining negative quotient gives some exact-annihilator place of odd first depth above a rational prime \(p\nmid n\). Since \(I\mid(m)\), the order of \(G\bmod p\) is odd. Rational two-descent at \(G=(-3,9)\) gives \(p\equiv1\bmod12\), hence the place has residue degree one and the Gaussian multiplication is defined over \(\mathbf F_p\). The norm quotient therefore records the same odd valuation. Moreover \(p\nmid n\) makes the multiplier \((n)/I\) a local unit, so the valuation in \(B_n\) equals that first depth. A rational prime accounts for at most two conjugate annihilator ideals. Finally \(\mathcal O/I\) injects into \(E(\mathbf F_p)\), and \(NI\ge b^2\). Hasse's bound gives \(p\ge(b-1)^2>b\) for \(b\ge3\). This supplies the asserted number and size of distinct primes.
