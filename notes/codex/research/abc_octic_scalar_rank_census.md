# Exact scalar reduction orders through 36 on the j=8000 orbit

For \(A=(1,1)\) on \(E:y^2=x^3-8x^2+8x\), exactly 273 odd primes have reduction order \(r_p\le36\). Every one has \(v_p(B_{r_p})=1\) except \(p=3\), of order three and initial depth two. Here \(B_n^2\) is the reduced denominator of \(x(nA)\). The computation exhausts primes of arbitrary size at these ranks. The largest certified factor has 112 digits and order 35. In particular the excess sum over \(p>7\) at each rank through 36 vanishes. [Factorizations and primality certificates](abc_octic_scalar_rank_certificates.json) · [Standard-library verifier](abc_octic_scalar_rank_verify.py)

At every odd prime the model has good reduction. Its projective point reduces to infinity exactly when the rational abscissa has negative valuation. Thus an odd prime in \(B_n\) has order dividing \(n\). Remove from \(B_n\) all powers of two and all prime powers supported on any \(B_d\) at a proper divisor \(d\mid n\), by repeated integer gcd. The resulting positive integer contains exactly the odd primes of first rank \(n\), each at its original exponent. This avoids counting subsequent formal lifting. For example \(B_3=45\) has \(3^2\) at first entry and \(v_3(B_9)=3\), \(v_3(B_{27})=4\). The higher valuations are inherited. The odd first-rank parts at ranks 4, 5 and 6 are respectively \(7\cdot31\), \(281\cdot349\), and \(17\cdot47\cdot113\).

The verifier computes the \(B_n\) directly by exact rational group addition, removes inherited factors, and checks the full product at every rank. Small prime certificates use trial division, then the Lucas criterion with a fully factored \(p-1\). For large primes the JSON supplies the parameters of elliptic-curve primality certificates generated with PARI. Each step proves a smaller prime \(q\), constructs a point of exact order \(q\) modulo any prime dividing the candidate \(p\), and checks \(q>(p^{1/4}+1)^2\) using an integer upper bound for the right side. Every affine denominator used in the computation must be invertible modulo \(p\), and the curve discriminant must be coprime to \(p\). Hasse's inequality rules out a prime divisor at most \(\sqrt p\). The independent verifier uses only Python's standard library.

The first open scalar rank is 37. Its 286-digit primitive denominator has the three certified simple factors 418027, 1205387, and 46902031372350629. The bounded computation left two coprime composite cofactors of 126 and 131 digits. The verifier checks the product, coprimality and compositeness. Whether either cofactor has a repeated prime divisor remains open.
