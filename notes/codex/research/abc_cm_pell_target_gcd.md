# Common prime powers at different Pell indices and torsion ranks

Put \(\varepsilon=1+\sqrt2\), \(g=\varepsilon^4\), and write \(b_n+a_n\sqrt2=\varepsilon^n\). For a split prime \(q>3\) of \(K_0=\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-2})\), set \(D_{q,n}=|F_q(a_n,b_n)|\), using the primitive full-annihilator form of degree \((q-1)^2\). Then for positive \(m,n\), split primes \(q,r>3\), and \((q,n)\ne(r,m)\),
\[
 \log\gcd(D_{q,n},D_{r,m})\ll q^2r^2
  \left(1+\min\{\sqrt{\max(n,m)},|n-m|\}\right).                       \tag{1}
\]
The implied constant is absolute for the fixed curve. More generally one may use \(1+|a|+|b|+|an-bm|\) in place of the parenthesized bound for any nonnegative integers \(a,b\) with \(a+b>0\). Consequently the gcd along two fixed distinct positive affine index progressions with a fixed rational ratio of slopes is bounded, including all prime-power valuations. For every fixed pair of split ranks one moreover has
\[
               \log\gcd(D_{q,n},D_{r,m})
                   =o_{q,r}(\sqrt{\max(n,m)})\quad((q,n)\ne(r,m)).                    \tag{2}
\]
If \(p^e\mid D_{11,n}\) with \(e\log p\ge\delta n\) for fixed \(\delta>0\), then the next occurrence of that *same power* must have index \(m/n^2\to\infty\) as \(n\to\infty\). In particular this conclusion applies to each exponential-size prime, using \(e=1\). It leaves open an isolated exponential-size square divisor of one term, which would suffice for the proposed abc application. [Triple construction and sufficient criterion](abc_cm_pell_whole_triple.md)

## The targets and their conjugates

On \(E:y^2=x^3+16x^2+32x\) define \(\theta_\pm=-8\pm4\sqrt2\). With \(H=b^2+2a^2+2ab\), \(S=b^2+2a^2\), the abscissa used in the twist is \(-8H/S\). At the Pell parameters it equals
\[
 t_n=\frac{\theta_-g^n+\theta_+}{g^n+1}.
\]
For each full \(q\)-torsion abscissa \(x=x(T)\) take the target \(u=(\theta_+-x)/(x-\theta_-)\). These are finite and nonzero, since \(\theta_\pm\) are two-torsion abscissas. Denote their set by \(U_q\), of size \(t_q=(q-1)^2/2\). Their absolute logarithmic heights are bounded by an absolute constant for the fixed curve since the naïve abscissa height differs from twice canonical height by a bounded amount, and the Möbius transformation is fixed. There is a constant \(A_q\ne0\) with
\[
 F_q(a_n,b_n)=A_q\bar\varepsilon^{2t_qn}\prod_{u\in U_q}(g^n-u),
 \qquad h(A_q)\ll q^2.                                                   \tag{3}
\]
Indeed homogenizing the abscissa division factor in \((-8H,S)\) gives (3) since \(S=\bar\varepsilon^{2n}(g^n+1)/2\). The roots of the primitive binary form have uniformly bounded height as well, by the height formula for the fixed degree-two parameter map. Thus its Mahler measure has logarithm \(O(q^2)\), using the sum of heights of all its roots, with no root at infinity. Its integer coefficients are \(\exp(O(q^2))\). At \(n=0\) the left side is a nonzero such coefficient. Every factor \(1-u\) is nonzero, since \(t_0=-8\) lies on the bounded real component and is not an odd-order torsion abscissa. Evaluating (3) there gives the bound on \(h(A_q)\). [The full factor and its real locus](abc_cm_twist_sharp_depth.md)

Set \(L=\mathbf Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt{-2})\). The absolute Galois group of \(L\) is transitive on \(U_q\), and for distinct \(q,r\) it can move an arbitrary member of \(U_q\) to any other while fixing \(E[r]\). Indeed CM over \(K_0\) acts on \(E[qr]\) by scalars. For arbitrary unit scalar classes at \(q,r\), choose a degree-one prime ideal generated by \(\beta\) with those classes and \(\beta\equiv1\bmod8\mathcal O_{K_0}\). Such primes exist by the prime ideal theorem in ray classes, since \(K_0\) has class number one. The signed CM Frobenius is \(\beta\) because its finite sign character is trivial on this congruence class. Its norm is 1 modulo 8, so Frobenius fixes \(L\). All scalar units at both levels can thus be realized independently. The primitive full-annihilator points are precisely the generators of the free rank-one \(\mathcal O/(q)\)-module. [Sign and conductor of this CM character](abc_odd_rank_signed_frobenius.md)

At least two targets in \(U_q\) have distinct absolute values. The unbounded real component has \(q\) points of order dividing \(q\). A nonidentity one must have full annihilator, since either proper prime-ideal annihilator and its conjugate would both kill the real point, forcing it to vanish. Their \((q-1)/2\) distinct abscissas are positive. The transformation to \(u\) sends distinct nonnegative abscissas to distinct negative real numbers. It follows by conjugation over \(L\) that every individual target is multiplicatively independent of \(g\).

If \(u,v\in U_q\) and \(u^a=g^\ell v^b\), with \(a,b\ge0\), take a Galois element \(\sigma\) sending \(u\) to \(v\) and write \(d\) for its order on the finite splitting field. Iterating gives \(u^{a^d-b^d}=g^{\ell(a^{d-1}+a^{d-2}b+\cdots+b^{d-1})}\). Thus \(a=b\), and \(\ell=0\) if this common value is positive. For distinct ranks conjugate \(u\) to targets of different magnitudes while fixing \(v\); this rules out any relation with \(a>0\). With \(a=0,b>0\), independence of \(v\) and \(g\) suffices.

## Bounding common valuations

Choose \(a,b\ge0\), \(a+b>0\), \(\ell=an-bm\). The preceding argument gives
\[
                      u^a-g^\ell v^b\ne0
               \quad(u\in U_q,\ v\in U_r).
\]
At a nonarchimedean place where \(g^n-u\) and \(g^m-v\) both have positive valuation, \(u,v,g\) are units. The smaller of those valuations is bounded by the valuation of the displayed difference by reducing at their common precision. The same argument works with arbitrary signed powers whenever the resulting difference is nonzero.

Take a number field containing the targets, use the standard normalized local weights for absolute height, and sum. For nonnegative finite sequences \((A_i),(B_j)\) one has \(\min(\sum A_i,\sum B_j)\le\sum_{i,j}\min(A_i,B_j)\). Applying this to the positive parts of local valuations in (3), the normalized log gcd is bounded by
\[
 h(A_q)+h(A_r)+
 \sum_{u\in U_q,v\in U_r} h(u^a-g^\ell v^b)
 \ll q^2r^2(1+a+b+|\ell|).
\]
Here the product formula bounds total positive valuations by height and \(\varepsilon\) is a global unit. This treats all rational primes. Taking \(a=b=1\) gives the shift bound. If \(n\le m\), Dirichlet approximation to \(n/m\) with denominator \(a\le\lceil\sqrt m\rceil\) gives a nonnegative numerator \(b\le a+1\) and \(|an-bm|\le\sqrt m\). Interchange the indices otherwise. This proves (1).

## A sub-square-root bound at fixed ranks

All 50 targets at rank eleven are multiplicatively independent modulo torsion, and their product is \(g^{-12}\). Hence any two distinct targets there together with \(g\) are multiplicatively independent. [Interval log-matrix certificate](abc_cm_pell_target_rank.md)

We use the following consequence of Corvaja and Zannier's gcd theorem. In a fixed number field and a fixed finitely generated group of units away from a finite set of places, for **multiplicatively independent pairs** \(A,B\) in the group, the sum over finite places of the common positive logarithmic valuations of \(A-1,B-1\) is \(o(\max(h(A),h(B)))\) as this maximum grows. For any fixed positive error fraction the theorem puts exceptions in finitely many translates of proper subtori. On a translate \(A^iB^j=c\ne1\), the common valuations are bounded by those of \(c-1\) wherever both differences have positive valuation, since \(A,B\) are local units there. On a subtorus through the identity the pair is dependent. More explicitly, apply their rational-function height theorem to the pair of polynomials \(X-1,Y-1\). The difference \(h(1:A-1:B-1)-h(A-1:B-1)\) is the sum of the common positive logarithmic proximities at *all* places, by the projective height formula. Laurent’s theorem on finitely generated subgroups of a torus gives the stated finite-union description of the exceptional closed set on that subgroup. [Corvaja and Zannier, “A lower bound for the height of a rational function at S-unit points”](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0311030)

For \(N=\max(n,m)\) consider the rank-two lattice
\[
                   \Lambda=\{(a,b,c)\in\mathbf Z^3:an+bm+c=0\}.
\]
Its covolume in its plane is \(\sqrt{n^2+m^2+1}\asymp N\), by taking the cross product of \((1,0,-n),(0,1,-m)\). Its Euclidean successive minima satisfy \(\lambda_1\lambda_2\asymp N\) by Minkowski's second theorem. Fix targets \(u,v\). The map \((a,b,c)\mapsto u^a v^b g^c\) has zero kernel modulo torsion on \(\Lambda\) for all sufficiently large \(\min(m,n)\), with the ranks fixed. One may raise any putative root-of-unity relation to its order. For mixed ranks, the independent Galois actions exclude it. For equal ranks the preceding nonnegative-power lemma handles coefficients \(a,b\) of opposite signs or a zero coefficient, since \(n\ne m\). For coefficients of the same sign, take absolute values and logs: once both indices exceed \(\max_{w\in U_q}|\log|w||/\log g\), the two terms \(a(\log|u|-n\log g)\) and \(b(\log|v|-m\log g)\) have the same strict sign. Their sum cannot vanish. If \(\min(m,n)\) remains bounded, the smaller term already bounds the gcd by a constant for fixed ranks. Thus in the remaining case independent lattice vectors give independent units.

For an explicit uniformity argument, fix a target pair and assign a desired error \(\epsilon>0\). For any vector of length \(T\), the height of its unit \(A\), and that of \(A-1\) if nonzero, is at most \(C(1+T)\) for a fixed \(C\). At any finite place where the bases approximate the two targets, the valuation of \(A-1\) is at least the shared depth, since signed powers in the unit group preserve congruences. If \(\lambda_1\le\eta\sqrt N\), a shortest vector therefore gives total shared logarithmic depth at most \(C(1+\eta\sqrt N)\); take \(\eta<\epsilon/(4C)\). Otherwise Minkowski gives two independent vectors of lengths at most \(C_1\sqrt N/\eta\). Their units \(A,B\) are independent by the preceding kernel test and both differences from 1 retain the shared depth. In the fixed group apply the gcd theorem with an error fraction small enough in terms of \(\epsilon,\eta,C,C_1\). Above its fixed height threshold this gives total shared logarithmic depth at most \(\epsilon\sqrt N/2\). Below the threshold Northcott gives a bounded contribution independent of the indices. Thus the threshold in \(N\) works for *every* lattice direction, including those coming from rational slopes that vary with \(N\). The finitely many target pairs can each receive an appropriately smaller error before summation in (3). Mixed ranks at the same index are already bounded by the zero-shift case of (1). For a fixed positive rational affine relation the first minimum is bounded, which gives a constant bound directly. This proves (2).

## Several distinct target hits

At rank 11, suppose one prime place simultaneously satisfies \(g^{n_i}\equiv u_i\) to common precision \(p^k\) for \(s\) distinct targets, with \(0\le n_i\le N\), \(2\le s\le 50\). Then, for fixed \(s\),
\[
                              k\log p=o_s(N^{1/s}).
\]
Indeed the lattice \(\sum_i a_i n_i+c=0\) has rank \(s\), covolume \(\sqrt{1+\sum n_i^2}\), and maps to the group of monomials in the targets and \(g\). This map is injective modulo torsion on the lattice by independence when \(s<50\). For \(s=50\), the sole relation generated by \((1,\ldots,1,12)\) lies outside the lattice since \(\sum_i n_i+12>0\). The product of successive minima is \(O_s(N)\). A first minimum of size \(o(N^{1/s})\) again gives the bound using one nonzero difference; otherwise there are two independent vectors of size \(O_s(N^{1/s})\) and the S-unit gcd theorem applies. The fixed splitting field only introduces a constant when passing between one local valuation and absolute height. This assertion concerns distinct targets. Repeated indices separated by a multiplicative period can meet the same target.
