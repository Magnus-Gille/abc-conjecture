# The rational points of \(6y^2=1-x^5\)

The only affine rational points are
\[
 (x,y)=(1,0),\qquad (1/3,11/27),\qquad (1/3,-11/27).
\]
In particular, the open range \(0<x<1\) leaves precisely \(x=1/3\). If the arithmetic application supplies \(x=c/u^4\) with \(3\nmid u\), or with \(\gcd(c,u)=1\), this last value is excluded too. This note proves only the classification and the stated conditional implication.

Use \(X=-6x,\ Y=216y\). The resulting curve is
\[
 H:Y^2=f(X)=X^5+6^5.
\]
Its projective completion has one rational point \(\infty\) at infinity. Write \(J=\operatorname{Jac}(H)\) and embed \(H\) in \(J\) by \(D\mapsto[D-\infty]\). Put \(T=[(-6,0)-\infty]\), \(P=[(-2,88)-\infty]\).

## Two-descent

Use the odd-degree hyperelliptic two-descent map into the squareclasses of the étale algebra of \(f\), with components \(X-\theta\) evaluated on divisors. The algebra is \(\mathbb Q\times K\), where \(K=\mathbb Q(\zeta)\), \(\zeta\) is a primitive fifth root and the selected roots in the two components are \(-6,-6\zeta\). The map on \(J(\mathbb Q)/2J(\mathbb Q)\) is injective and its image has square norm. Thus the \(K\)-component determines the rational component. The same holds after completion because the linear factor stays rational. The element associated to \(T\) in the \(K\)-component is \(-6+6\zeta=-6\pi\), with \(\pi=1-\zeta\). The two rational factors of \(f\) give exactly two rational points in \(J[2]\), namely zero and \(T\). See E. F. Schaefer, *2-descent on the Jacobians of hyperelliptic curves*, Journal of Number Theory 51 (1995), 219–232.

At primes outside \(2,3,5\) the integral monic quintic has separable reduction. Descent classes have even valuations there. For example, moving a divisor away from the roots, at an integral slope at most one root can be met, giving even valuation by the curve equation. At a nonintegral slope all the valuations are equal to its even valuation, since the quintic has odd degree. The argument applies to the residue fields of the points making up the divisor and then to their norms.

The field \(K\) has discriminant \(125\) in absolute value and class number one, by the Minkowski bound \( (4/\pi_{\rm real})^2(4!/4^4)\sqrt{125}<2\), where \(\pi_{\rm real}\) denotes the circle constant. Both 2 and 3 are inert, while 5 is totally ramified. The units are generated by the roots of unity and \(\epsilon=(1+\sqrt5)/2=1+\zeta+\zeta^{-1}=-\zeta^2-\zeta^3\), for the indicated choice of \(\sqrt5\). Indeed, for any unit \(u\) the quotient \(u/\bar u\) is a root of unity by the absolute values of its conjugates. It is one modulo \(\pi\) and therefore lies in \(\mu_5=\mu_{10}^2\). Dividing \(u\) by a suitable root of unity makes it real, and the real quadratic units are generated by \(-1,\epsilon\). Thus the possible \(K\)-components have bit vectors \((a,b,c,d,e)\) representing
\[
 (-1)^a\epsilon^b2^c3^d\pi^e.
\]

For \(p=3,5,2\), the number of irreducible local factors of \(f\) is two, so the local rational two-torsion has order two. At the odd places the finite quotient \(J(\mathbb Q_p)/2J(\mathbb Q_p)\) has that same order, since multiplication by 2 is invertible on an open pro-\(p\) subgroup. At 2 the quotient has order eight, since the open formal group has dimension two. These local sizes apply also at primes of bad reduction, by using a sufficiently deep formal subgroup.

* At 3 the nonzero local image is represented by \(-6\pi\), of odd valuation. The rational units and \(\epsilon\) are squares in the residue field \(\mathbb F_{81}\): the latter reduces into \(\mathbb F_9^\times\). The unit \(\pi\) is nonsquare, since its norm to \(\mathbb F_3\) is \(5\bmod3\). This imposes \(e=d\).
* At 5 the class of \(-6\pi\) again has odd valuation. The unit \(-6\) is square modulo 5, as is \(-1\), while \(\epsilon,2,3\) reduce to the nonsquares \(3,2,3\). This imposes \(b=c+d\).
* At 2, \(K_2/\mathbb Q_2\) is unramified of degree four. A unit is a square if and only if it is a square modulo 8, by Hensel's lemma. The local image is generated by \(T,P\) and a point above \(X=1\), since \(1+6^5\equiv1\bmod8\). To check independence, write the \(K_2\)-components as \(-6+6\zeta,-2+6\zeta,1+6\zeta\). Record the valuation modulo two and the lexicographically least coefficient vector modulo 8 in the unit's square coset, in the ascending basis \(1,\zeta,\zeta^2,\zeta^3\). In the order \(T,(1,\sqrt{1+6^5}),P\) these representatives are \((1;0,0,1,3),(0;0,0,1,6),(1;0,0,3,3)\). They generate eight distinct classes, exhausting the local image. ## Rank, torsion and reduction at 19

Direct point counts give the following table. The last column is \((\#H(\mathbb F_p)^2+\#H(\mathbb F_{p^2}))/2-p\) by the genus-two symmetric square.

| \(p\) | \(\#H(\mathbb F_p)\) | \(\#H(\mathbb F_{p^2})\) | \(\#J(\mathbb F_p)\) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 7 | 8 | 50 | 50 |
| 11 | 16 | 118 | 176 |
| 13 | 14 | 170 | 170 |
| 19 | 20 | 438 | 400 |

All four places are good. The first two orders have gcd two, with \(7\nmid176\) and \(11\nmid50\). Injectivity on prime-to-\(p\) torsion gives \(J(\mathbb Q)_{\rm tors}=\{0,T\}\). The point \(P\) is a nonbranch point and its embedded class differs from both torsion points. Thus the rank is exactly one.

Write \(P=jB+t\) for a generator \(B\) of the free quotient and rational torsion \(t\). The 2-adic descent above shows \(2\nmid j\). Modulo 13, \(P\) has order 85 by Cantor arithmetic, whereas \(v_5(\#J(\mathbb F_{13}))=1\), so \(5\nmid j\). Consequently the entire reduction of \(J(\mathbb Q)\) modulo 19 is generated by \(P,T\), since \(j\) is invertible modulo 400. Here \(P\) has order ten, and the generated subgroup has order twenty. Its intersection with the embedded curve consists of
\[
 \infty,\qquad (13,0),\qquad (17,7),\qquad (17,12)\quad\bmod19.
\]
The last three were checked by testing every affine point in \(\mathbb F_{19}^2\). They are precisely the reductions of the three known affine rational points.

## One rational point per surviving disk

Use the integral differential basis \(\omega_0=dX/(2Y),\ \omega_1=X\,dX/(2Y)\). The divisor \(10P\) lies in the formal kernel at 19. To obtain its first logarithm digit, translate by \(P+T\), where the two support points have distinct abscissas. Exact rational Cantor addition gives the Mumford pair of \(11P+T\) modulo \(19^2=361\):
\[
 U=X^2+46X+240,\qquad V=212X+18
\]
with the convention \(Y=V(X)\) on the support. The coefficients of the exact pair are integral at 19. The simple roots modulo 19 lift to two points, one near \((-2,88)\) with abscissa \(X'_P\equiv321\bmod361\), the other near \((-6,0)\) with ordinate \(Y'_T\equiv190\bmod361\). For the latter use \(X'_T=-6\bmod361\), since \(f'(-6)\) is a unit and \(Y'_T\) is divisible by 19.

Integrating to first order on these two disks gives the vector of \(\log_J(10P)/19\) modulo 19. Near \(P\) the coefficient for the displacement in \(X\) is \(1/176\), while near \(T\), \(dX/(2Y)=dY/f'(X)\). This gives
\[
 \frac{X'_P+2}{19\cdot176}(1,-2)
 +\frac{Y'_T}{19 f'(-6)}(1,-6)
 \equiv 11(1,-2)+10(1,-6)=(2,13)\pmod{19}.
\]
Higher terms of the integral have valuation at least two before division by 19. This computation uses ordinary local parameters on smooth disks and the power series of an integral regular differential.

The rational group has rank one, so its entire logarithm lies on the line generated by \(\log_J(10P)\). The vector divided by 19 is primitive. Therefore its exact annihilator has an integral generator reducing to \(6\omega_0+2\omega_1\). At finite points this differential vanishes only over \(X=-3\bmod19\); neither \(13\) nor \(17\) has this value. At the Weierstrass point the local parameter is \(Y\), and at infinity \(\omega_1\) is a unit differential while \(\omega_0\) vanishes. Hence the reduction of the annihilator is nonzero on all four surviving disks.

On a disk with integral parameter \(z\in19\mathbb Z_{19}\), the differential is a unit times \(dz\). Its integral between different parameters has valuation exactly \(v_{19}(z_1-z_2)\), by expansion about \(z_2\) with integral coefficients and the first term a unit. But the integral between two rational points is the annihilator on their logarithm difference and must vanish. Thus each disk contains at most one rational point.
