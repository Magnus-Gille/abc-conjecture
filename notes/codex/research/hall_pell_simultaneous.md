# Simultaneous square divisors in the Pell family

For $n=7+12j$, $j\ge0$, set $t=L_n-11$, $A=t^2+10t+5$ and $B=t^2+4t-1$. The primitive triple is $1728t+5(F_nB)^2=A^3$. In this report $D$ is the product of selected **distinct** primes $p\ge7$ for which $p^2$ divides $A$ or $B$. This certifies a factor $D$ in $M/\operatorname{rad}(M)$ for $M=tF_nAB$. The full logarithmic saving includes higher powers and other prime divisors. A positive limsup of $\log D/n$ would suffice for a counterexample because $\log M\sim\log(A^3)\sim6n\log((1+\sqrt5)/2)$. [Family identity and coprimality](hall_pell_direction.md)

## Computing the classes

The discriminants of $A$ and $B$ as polynomials in $t$ are 80 and 20, so any $p\ge7$ contributing to either must split 5. Let $T$ be the Fibonacci matrix and let $r$ be the order of $T^{12}$ modulo $p$. Diagonalizing $T$ over $\mathbb F_p$ gives $r\mid(p-1)/\gcd(12,p-1)$. The subsequence $x_j=L_{7+12j}$ starts at 29 and 9349 and obeys $x_{j+2}=322x_{j+1}-x_j$. One period modulo $p$ finds all candidate residues $j_0$. For $G(j)=A$ or $B$ evaluated at that index and $p\mid G(j_0)$, write

$$v=G(j_0)/p\pmod p,\qquad s=(G(j_0+r)-G(j_0))/p\pmod p.$$

As $T^{12r}=I+pH$ for an integer matrix $H$, expansion modulo $p^2$ gives $G(j_0+kr)/p\equiv v+ks\pmod p$. For $s\ne0$ the unique square class has $k\equiv-v/s\pmod p$. If $s=0$, a zero $v$ admits the whole class modulo $r$, and a nonzero $v$ admits none. Every square class found here had nonzero slope. For two prescribed classes $a\bmod m$ and $b\bmod h$, compatibility is exactly $a\equiv b\pmod{\gcd(m,h)}$.

An independent method worked directly modulo $p^2$. For a lift of $\sqrt5$, put $\alpha=(1+\sqrt5)/2$ and $w=\alpha^n$. Since $n$ is odd, $L_n=w-w^{-1}$. The targets for $L_n$ are $6\pm2\sqrt5$ for $A$ and $9\pm\sqrt5$ for $B$. For each target $x$ solve $w^2-xw-1=0$, then solve $(\alpha^{12})^j=w\alpha^{-7}$ in the cyclic subgroup generated by $\alpha^{12}$. This matched all 530 classes. Direct enumeration of every Lucas index in a full joint period modulo $p^2$ and 12 also checked all primes from 7 through 200.

## Forcing arbitrary powers of 31 and 59 together

For $31^2\mid A$, the classes are $j\equiv13,61\pmod{155}$. For $59^2\mid B$, they are $j\equiv43,121\pmod{1711}$. The earliest pair uses 61 and 43, giving $j\equiv5176\pmod{265205}$. The Fibonacci index is $7+12j$.

Take these two particular branches. Each lifts uniquely at every successive power. For 31 the residue modulo the initial period $r=5$ is 1 and the slope above is 28 modulo 31. For 59 the corresponding data are $r=29$, residue 14 and slope 19 modulo 59. For odd $p$, $(I+pH)^{p^a}\equiv I+p^{a+1}H\pmod{p^{a+2}}$, so the same nonzero slope controls each successive digit of the index. For $31^e\mid A$ and $59^f\mid B$, the periods in $j$ are $5\cdot31^{e-1}$ and $29\cdot59^{f-1}$ on these branches. They are coprime for every $e,f\ge2$. This proves existence of infinitely many indices for every prescribed pair of depths.

For the two branches the period in $j$ is exactly 145 times the certified factor. More generally every square class in the scanned pool has period divisible by its prime, so the joint period of any compatible tuple is at least $D$. This bounds the period, while the least nonnegative solution can be much smaller. Obtaining $n=O(\log D)$ for an unbounded sequence would require unusually small simultaneous solutions. CRT existence alone supplies no such upper bound.

## How common are these gains?

Over a full joint period of the scanned classes the mean number of contributing primes is 0.02692047 and the mean of the sum of their $\log p$ is 0.10145615. Each class contributes its reciprocal period to frequency. Distinct classes of one prime are disjoint, and linearity of the mean requires no independence across primes.

There is also a bound with a growing prime cutoff. Consider only primes with $T^{12r}\not\equiv I\pmod{p^2}$ and $5$ a square modulo $p$. For these primes there are at most eight square classes of period $pr$. Indeed $w=\alpha^n$ has at most eight possible values modulo $p$ from the four targets above, and takes distinct values as $j$ runs through one period. The derivative of $w-w^{-1}$ can vanish at a target only if $x^2+4=0$. The products of $x^2+4$ over the conjugate targets are 720 for $A$ and 6480 for $B$, excluding every $p\ge7$ from this degeneracy. The target polynomials also have simple roots. The split eigenvalues of $T^{12r}$ are inverse to one another, so a nonidentity lift at $p^2$ changes $w$ in a nonzero first-order direction. Thus each mod-$p$ hit lifts uniquely.

The series $\sum_p \log p/(pr)$ over these primes converges. To see this, group $X<p\le2X$ by $r$. For a fixed $r$, all such primes divide $F_{12r}$, whose logarithm is $O(r)$. Orders $r\le\sqrt X$ contribute $O(X^{-1/2})$ altogether, and larger orders contribute $O(X^{-1/2}\log X)$ even by counting all the integers in the interval. Summing over dyadic $X$ proves convergence.

It follows that for $N\le j<2N$, summing the single-square logarithmic savings from these primes up to $P$ over all the indices gives $O(N+P)$. A class can occur at most $N/(pr)+1$ times, and $\sum_{p\le P}\log p=O(P)$ by Chebyshev's estimate. In particular for $P=N$ the mean saving stays bounded as $N$ grows. This bound leaves open exceptional indices, larger primes, higher valuations and primes with $T^{12r}\equiv I\pmod{p^2}$.

## Reproduction

Run from the directory containing this note. The first command uses SymPy for the independent discrete-log check. Omitting that flag needs only Python's standard library.

```sh
python3 -B hall_pell_crt.py --prime-limit 10000 --tuple-size 3 --algebraic-check --csv hall_pell_classes.csv
python3 -B hall_pell_verify_records.py
```

The record verifier checks the proposed minima and maxima using the full class table. For $k\ge2$ it fixes the two largest selected primes $p<q$, intersects their classes, and at each relevant index checks the $k-2$ largest matching smaller primes. For this pair $D\le q p^{k-1}$. Above the resulting explicit ceiling on $n$, even that upper bound cannot beat the displayed score. Integer bit-length bounds certify the comparisons without depending on floating-point rankings.
