# Selecting only primes that already divide the elliptic norms

On the [fixed elliptic orbit](abc_octic_two_squareful_norms.md), the two norms \(U_\pm=(s\pm t)^2+t^2\) can simultaneously have all their prime exponents at least two, and all their exponents at primes \(p\le Y\) at least any fixed \(k\ge3\), while
\[
 Y/\log\log H\longrightarrow\infty,\qquad
 \operatorname{rad}(|st(s^8-16t^8)|)\ll H^8/\sqrt{\log H}.
\]
Here \(H=\max(s,t)\); the implied constant can depend on the fixed seed. This gives an unbounded improvement in the cutoff and retains the full logarithmic saving. A logarithm in the denominator still falls short of a fixed power of \(H\).

## The increasing-prime construction

Write \(M=\operatorname{rad}(U_+(s_0,t_0)U_-(s_0,t_0))\). Start at the odd index \(n=M^{\lceil\sqrt Y\rceil}\). In increasing order of primes \(p\le Y\), if \(p\) occurs in either norm at the current index with positive valuation below \(k\), multiply \(n\) by \(p^{\lceil k/2\rceil}\). The seed's primes already exceed the threshold in multiplicity for large \(Y\). Use the signed rational slope to keep track of the two factors; taking its absolute value finally can swap their names. At odd indices the replacement \((s,t)\mapsto(t,s/2)\) when \(s\) is even, described in the [denominator calculation](abc_octic_elliptic_denominator.md), divides each norm by two and preserves its odd valuations.

For a good odd prime at which one of the norms is met, the first odd index \(r_p\) is the order modulo \(p\) of \(T=[P-\iota P]\) on \(E_d\). Indeed \(X(nT)=-4s^2t^2/z^2,\ s^4+4t^4=d z^2\). For \(p\nmid2d\) the numerator is a unit whenever \(p\mid z\); reduction to the identity is precisely \(r_p\mid n\). On odd multiples of \(r_p\) the same branch point is met, since odd scalar multiplication preserves the branch label. Equivalently one can use \(P_n-W=[n/r_p](P_{r_p}-W)\) with the unnormalized slope and \(W\) the branch at the first encounter. Its local projection has order two. Thus the valuation increases by \(2v_p(n/r_p)\). The normalized slopes may interchange signs if made positive, which does not affect a test of the two norms together.

Every inserted prime is \(1\bmod4\). For a good one, \(4r_p\mid\#E_d(\mathbb F_p)\), since \(r_p\) is odd and the curve has full rational 2-torsion. Hasse gives \(r_p\le(\sqrt p+1)^2/4<p\). Once \(p\) has been checked, multiplying the index by a larger prime therefore cannot create a first occurrence at \(p\), and it cannot change the depth at \(p\) if the prime already occurred. At \(p\mid d\), the seed valuation formula applies directly on the untwisted curve with good reduction. All inserted primes hence meet the target depth, and all primes outside the seed support still have even valuations whenever they occur.

Let \(n\) now denote the final index. For a prime inserted after the seed stage, its initial valuation was even and at least two, and its final valuation in the product is at least \(2+2v_p(n)\). At seed primes it is the seed valuation plus \(2v_p(n)\). Consequently
\[
 \operatorname{rad}(U_+U_-)\ll_{\rm seed} \sqrt{U_+U_-}/n .
\]
Since \(\log H\asymp n^2\), this gives the asserted radical bound after accounting for \(s,t,s^2-2t^2,s^2+2t^2\). It remains to estimate the size of \(n\) relative to \(Y\).

## The possible inserted primes have density zero

The following argument uses the main theorem of complex multiplication, Chebotarev's density theorem and Mordell–Weil. No rate of convergence in Chebotarev is needed. Over \(K=\mathbb Q(i)\) the endomorphism ring of \(E_d\) is \(O=\mathbb Z[i]\). The image of the action on all torsion has finite index in \(\widehat O^\times\) and is abelian. In particular for any finite list of distinct sufficiently large rational primes the image on their torsion contains the full product of the corresponding groups of \(O\)-units modulo those primes. A fixed finite set of exceptions suffices for all lists. These are the standard open-image conclusions for elliptic curves with complex multiplication defined over the CM field. [Silverman, *Advanced Topics in the Arithmetic of Elliptic Curves*, Chapter II](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4612-0851-8) · [Lombardo and Tronto on elliptic Kummer theory](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.05376)

Here are details of the required independence of the division points of \(T\). For every sufficiently large Gaussian prime \(\lambda\), the class of \(T\) in \(E_d(K)/\lambda E_d(K)\) is nonzero: \(T\) is non-torsion and the torsion-free quotient of this finitely generated \(O\)-module is free. Let \(H_{\rm tor}\) be the Galois group of the full torsion field over \(K\). Its action on \(E_d[\lambda]\) contains a scalar different from one. Since \(H_{\rm tor}\) is abelian, \(H^1(H_{\rm tor},E_d[\lambda])=0\): comparing the cocycle evaluated on \(gh=hg\) with a fixed such scalar \(h\) expresses the cocycle as a coboundary. Inflation-restriction now shows that the Kummer class of \(T\) remains nonzero on the absolute Galois group of the torsion field. Its image there is a nonzero additive subgroup of \(O/\lambda\), stable under all scalar units, and therefore all of \(O/\lambda\). For primes \(\lambda\) of distinct residue characteristics these additive groups have pairwise coprime orders. A subgroup surjecting to every one surjects onto their product. With the independent scalar actions, the finite joint affine image on the \(\lambda\)-division torsors of \(T\) is therefore the product of the full affine groups. We use just one \(\lambda\) above each rational prime.

Fix one sufficiently large prime \(\ell_0\equiv3\bmod4\). There is a positive-density set \(\mathcal B\) of rational split primes \(q\) where the order of \(T\bmod q\) is divisible by \(\ell_0\). To see this, use \(\lambda=\ell_0\) and select Frobenius elements with scalar one and nonzero translation. They fix no point of the torsor \([\ell_0]^{-1}T\), so \(T\bmod q\) is not divisible by \(\ell_0\) over the residue field. In a finite group this forces its order to be divisible by \(\ell_0\). Chebotarev over \(K\) gives positive density among prime ideals, and those of residue degree greater than one have zero density there. One can take \(\mathcal B\) to be the primes satisfying the specified Frobenius condition: the condition is invariant on the two conjugate degree-one places, since \(T\) is rational and \(\ell_0\) inert. In particular \(\sum_{q\in\mathcal B}1/q\) diverges. Omit any fixed finite collection, including the seed primes and the exceptional primes for independence.

No member of \(\mathcal B\) can be inserted, since the current index has only primes \(1\bmod4\) and therefore cannot be divisible by \(\ell_0\). Consequently, at any prime \(p\) that can be inserted, the order \(r_p\) avoids **all** \(q\in\mathcal B\). This last restriction defines a density-zero subset of the rational split primes. In detail, for each of finitely many \(q\in\mathcal B\) choose one Gaussian prime \(\lambda\mid q\). In its affine group over \(\mathbb F_q\), the proportion of elements with scalar one and nonzero translation is \(1/q\). At a degree-one place of \(K\) with that Frobenius, \(T\) has no rational \(\lambda\)-division in the residue field, so its order is divisible by \(q\): an order prime to \(q\) would allow division by \(q=\lambda\bar\lambda\) within the generated cyclic subgroup. Independence and Chebotarev bound the upper density of eligible places by \(\prod_q(1-1/q)\) for every finite list. This product tends to zero. Each eligible rational split prime contributes degree-one places in \(K\), giving the rational density assertion.

The sum of \(\log p\) over all eligible primes up to \(Y\) is therefore \(o(Y)\), by the prime number theorem or its upper bound and partial summation. Each newly inserted prime receives only the fixed exponent \(\lceil k/2\rceil\), and the initial index costs \(O(\sqrt Y)\) in its logarithm. Thus \(\log n=o(Y)\). Since the initial index also forces \(n\to\infty\), this proves \(Y/\log\log H\to\infty\). The density argument does not imply that only finitely many primes can be inserted, or that an index meeting the unrestricted cubeful condition exists.
