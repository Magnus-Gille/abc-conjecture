# Pointwise bounds from odd support and primitive divisors

Write \(D_j=\operatorname{sf}(B_j)\) for odd positive \(j\), on \(G=(-3,9)\), \(E:y^2=x^3-36x\). At every odd prime \(q\) choose a prime \(f(q)\mid D_q\). The nontrivial square classification gives existence. The order of \(G\bmod f(q)\) is \(q\), so the map is injective and \(f(q)>2q\). ## A single prime-power cofactor never exceeds the final cofactor

For every odd index \(n\) and every \(q^a\mid n\) with \(q\) prime and \(a\ge1\),
\[
                       D_{q^a}\le D_n .
\]
For each \(p\mid D_{q^a}\), stop at \(p\) if \(p\mid D_n\). Otherwise formal lifting shows that \(p\mid n\), since \(p\) originally has odd valuation at \(q^a\). Continue along \(p\mapsto f(p)\) until reaching \(D_n\). At every subsequent step, a missing odd witness again divides \(n\). Strict increase and finiteness of \(n\)'s prime support ensure termination. Distinct starting primes have disjoint paths: by injectivity a collision between two paths forces some start \(p'\) to be strictly downstream of another. The predecessor would be a prime equal to the order of \(G\bmod p'\), a positive power \(q^j\). It must thus be \(q\), which lies strictly below every start. Distinct terminals, each at least its start, prove the product inequality. Divisibility of the two kernels is not required.

For \(D_n\le Y\), this gives \(D_q\le Y\) at every prime factor \(q\) of \(n\). Also \(P^+(n)<Y/2\) by a retained odd witness at the largest prime index. The effective periodic argument now uses a modulus at most \(4qD_q<2Y^2\), improving the earlier quasi-polynomial bound for that modulus. Namely choose a reduced class \(3\bmod4,\ 1\bmod q\) with Jacobi symbol \(-1\) modulo \(D_q\). All prime factors of \(D_q\) are \(1\bmod12\) and exceed \(q\). By Linnik's theorem some prime \(h\) in that class has \(h\ll(2Y)^{2L}\), where \(L\) is any effective admissible Linnik exponent. Put \(t_q=\operatorname{ord}_{h+1}(q)\le h+1\). The periodic denominator character detects a new odd prime of exact order \(q^e\) whenever \(e\equiv1\bmod t_q\). Start with the first such \(e\) for which \(2q^e>Y\). If \(q^e\mid n\), that witness must exceed \(Y\), contradicting the prime-power inequality. Thus \(v_q(n)<h+2+\log(2Y)/\log q\). Multiplication over \(q<Y/2\) gives, effectively,
\[
        \log n\ll (2Y)^{2L+1}.
\]
The finite census that enumerates divisors of this exponent box therefore takes \(\exp((2Y)^{O(1)})\) bit operations, using even exact coordinates and trial division. ## An effective bound stronger than every power of the logarithm

The fixed-curve version of the effective Mumford and Vojta inequalities also makes the constant in the ordered-prefix bound effective:
\[
  \log n\ \le\ (1+\omega(D_n)) A^{1+\omega_1(D_n)}\log D_n
\]
for an effectively computable constant \(A>1\); \(\omega_1\) counts primes \(1\bmod24\). In effect this gives \(\log n\le D_n^{O(1/\log\log D_n)}\) at large \(D_n\), and provides an effective index bound at any specified kernel cutoff. No numerical value for \(A\) is supplied.

Indeed \(C_d:d^2 Z^4=f(U,V)\) is isomorphic to the fixed plane quartic \(C_1\) under \(Z\mapsto\sqrt d\, Z\). Choose once an algebraic branch point on \(Z=0\) as origin for the Abel–Jacobi embedding. If \(P_0\in C_d(\mathbf Q)\), the images of all rational points of \(C_d\), viewed on that fixed embedded curve, belong to a group generated by the image of \(J_d(\mathbf Q)\) and one translate coming from \(P_0\). Its rank is at most \(1+\operatorname{rank}J_d(\mathbf Q)\), which is explicitly \(O(1+\omega_1(d))\) by the two-isogeny descent. All the images have uniformly bounded algebraic degree. Effective Mumford–Vojta cone packing on the fixed embedded curve bounds the number of large points by \(\exp(O(1+\omega_1(d)))\). Small points in bounded degree have an effective uniform count by Northcott's bound for projective height, using the effective height comparison for a fixed symmetric polarization. The ordered-prefix argument and \(P^+(n)<D_n/2\) now give the displayed estimate. ## Three good primitive primes at every rank

For every positive odd \(n>1\), with the four arms \(a,c,d,w\) of the orbit, the actual abc radical \(R=\operatorname{rad}(6acdw)\) satisfies
\[
          R>\prod_{s\mid n,\ s>1}(2\sqrt{s}-1)^6>n^{3\tau(n)/2}.
\]
If all the index primes are \(3\bmod4\), then \(R>n^{2\tau(n)}\).

For each odd scalar \(s>1\) consider \(w_s,d_s,\max(a_s,c_s)\). These are three distinct denominator sequences evaluated at \(s\). The translated generator has integral coordinates in each case and the odd terms are \(F_j=B(j(G+T))\) for the corresponding rational two-torsion translate \(T\). Each selected \(F_s\) is at least \(\sqrt{2/3}\,w_s\), since \(3d_s^2=2w_s^2+a_s^2\) and \(a_s^2+c_s^2=2w_s^2\). At every odd \(j\) all four arms are at most \(\sqrt2\,w_j\). Suppose there is no good prime of order exactly \(s\) on the corresponding generator. Every good prime of \(F_s\) then occurs in some \(F_{s/\ell}\) with \(\ell\mid s\) prime. Its exponent rises by at most \(v_p(\ell)\) from that index. For the bad primes, all arms are odd and only the \(d\)-arm can contain 3, with \(v_3(d_s)=v_3(s)\). Consequently
\[
     \log F_s\le\sum_{\ell\mid s,\ \ell\ {\rm prime}}\log F_{s/\ell}
                   +\log\operatorname{rad}(s)+\log s.
\]
Use \(H>1/3,\ H j^2-2.2\le\log w_j\le H j^2+1.5\) and \(\sum_{\ell\ge3,\ \ell\ {\rm prime}}\ell^{-2}<1/4\). This implies \(s^2/4<2.41+3.69\log s\), impossible for \(s\ge7\). For \(s=3\) the three values are \(37,33,47\), with good primes \(37,11,47\). For \(s=5\) all three exceed 1 and are prime to 6, each generator is integral, and 5 is prime. Thus there are three good primitive witnesses at every \(s\).

At \(s\mid n\), all witnesses divide their respective arms at \(n\), by odd-index divisibility. Different arms at \(n\) are coprime, and different orders in the same arm also give distinct primes. Each witness \(p\) obeys \(4s\mid\#E(\mathbf F_p)\) by rational two-torsion and Hasse gives \(p\ge(2\sqrt s-1)^2>s\). Taking all \(s\mid n\) gives the claimed bound. In the inert-index case the \(w\)-arm prime is split in the Gaussian field and its Gaussian annihilator is \((s)\), giving \(4s^2\mid\#E(\mathbf F_p)\) and \(p\ge(2s-1)^2>s^2\) in this arm.
