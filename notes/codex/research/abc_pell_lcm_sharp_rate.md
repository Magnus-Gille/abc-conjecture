# A logarithmic saving in the overlap of Pell values

Let \(\epsilon=1+\sqrt2\), \(g=\epsilon^4\), \(b_n+a_n\sqrt2=\epsilon^n\) and \(F_n=|F_{11}(a_n,b_n)|\). Then, with effective constants for this fixed sequence,

\[
 0\le \log\frac{\prod_{n\le N}F_n}{\operatorname{lcm}_{n\le N}F_n}
       \ll\frac{N^2}{\log(2N)}.                                     \tag{1}
\]

The same error applies to the 482 nonlinear targets of the positive Pell triple. In the notation \(W_n=8b_{2n}a_{2n+1}T_n\) for its entire geometric support, this gives

\[
 \log\operatorname{lcm}_{n\le N}W_n
    =\left(482+\frac{16}{\pi^2}\right)N^2\log\epsilon
           +O\!\left(\frac{N^2}{\log(2N)}\right).                    \tag{2}
\]

## The arithmetic cost of covering a slope

A layer is a prime power \(p^j\); its logarithmic weight is \(\log p\). At any fixed good place in a common splitting field, a term that contains this layer has \(g^n\equiv u\bmod p^j\) for exactly one target \(u\) from the finite list. The order \(r_{p,j}\) of \(g\bmod p^j\) bounds the number of occurrences by \(d(1+N/r_{p,j})\), for a list of \(d\) targets. The combined weight of visited layers of order at most \(R\) is \(O(R^{3/2})\). Indeed a target in the group generated by \(g\) satisfies a nontrivial relation modulo \(p^j\) among the more than \(R\) monomials \(g^i u^l\), \(0\le i,l\le\lfloor\sqrt R\rfloor\). No such nontrivial relation holds in the number field. The product of numerator-ideal norms for all differences \(g^{i'}u^{l'}-1\), with \(|i'|,|l'|\le\sqrt R\) and \((i',l')\ne(0,0)\), has logarithm \(O(R^{3/2})\). This absorbs the largest qualifying power of each prime. Dyadic summation gives \(O(N^{3/2})\) for all occurrences of layers with order at most \(N\). The finitely many omitted primes give \(O(N)\) in total by the fixed-prime spacing bound. [Proof of the local estimates](abc_cm_pell_lcm.md)

Each remaining layer has at most \(d\) hits in \(1,\ldots,N\). If it occurs at \(n<m\), let \(x=n/m\). For any integers \(b\ge1,\ 0\le a\le b\), put \(h=bn-am\). The layer divides the numerator-ideal norm of \(u^b-v^ag^h\) for the two targets at the hits. These expressions are nonzero for realized triples, except that an identically zero expression belonging to an unrealized target pair may be omitted. The sufficient nonvanishing condition here is that equality forces \(a=b,\ h=0\), which contradicts \(n<m\). For the full set of 482 targets the ten orbit calculations verify this condition, and for \(F_n\) the same follows from its target relations. The log of the product of these norms over all nonzero target pairs is \(O(1+a+b+|h|)\). Thus for a fixed \((a,b,h)\) all the distinct layers it captures together have weight at most that quantity. This bound counts each prime through its highest captured power, even when the individual layers arise from different index pairs. Since order greater than \(N\) limits each layer to \(d\) occurrences, this union estimate also bounds their total overlap up to a fixed factor. ## Stopping the continued fraction

Only large \(N\) need be considered. Use the usual positive partial quotients of a continued fraction in \((0,1)\). Write \(a/b\) for the rational endpoint defined by a prefix and \(b'\) for the preceding denominator. The empty prefix has \(a/b=0/1,\ b'=0\). The open cylinder of this prefix has interval length \(1/[b(b+b')]\). With \(u=b'/b\in[0,1]\), the fraction of its length occupied by the child with next digit \(j\) is

\[
       \frac{1+u}{(j+u)(j+1+u)}\le\frac{2}{j(j+1)}.                \tag{3}
\]

In particular the children with the next digit \(j>K\) fill one interval, up to endpoints, of absolute length \(1/[b((K+1)b+b')]\) adjacent to \(a/b\). Its fraction of the parent is \((1+u)/(K+1+u)\ge1/(K+2)\).

Choose \(B=N^{1/8}\), and put

\[
 C_*=\sum_{j\ge1}\frac{2(j+1)^{1/2}}{j(j+1)},\qquad
 H=\left\lfloor\frac{\log N}{32\log C_*}\right\rfloor .
\]

Here \(1<C_*<\infty\) and \(H\asymp\log N\). On step \(i=1,\ldots,H\), let \(l=H-i+1\) be the number of steps left, and let \(K_i=\max(2,\lfloor l/4\rfloor)\). Start from the empty prefix. From any active prefix of length \(i-1\) and denominator \(b\le B\), process the next digit as follows.

* If \(j>K_i\), cover the whole corresponding interval using the current endpoint \(a/b\), and stop.
* If \(j\le K_i\), the child denominator is \(b_{\rm new}=jb+b'\). When \(b_{\rm new}>B\), move the entire child cylinder to the exceptional set and stop. When \(b_{\rm new}\le B\), continue, or move it to the exceptional set upon reaching depth \(H\).
* If the rational slope ends at an active prefix without requiring a next digit, use the exact endpoint \(a/b=x\), giving \(h=0\). Use the canonical finite expansion. Endpoints already belonging to another interval can be included in either cover.

Prefixes for which the first rule applies will be called stopping intervals. Including endpoints gives a cover for every rational \(x=n/m\) with \(1\le n<m\le N\), even when the canonical expansion has length exactly \(H\), or its final digit creates a denominator crossing.

## Total cost at the stopping intervals

Let \(t_i\) be the total length of the cylinders still active after \(i\) steps, counting those with bounded denominator and digits at depth \(H\) before they are transferred to the exceptional set. Their interiors are disjoint. With \(t_0=1\), removal of the large next digits on every active parent gives

\[
 t_i\le t_{i-1}\left(1-\frac{1}{K_i+2}\right).
\]

Further removals by the denominator cutoff only decrease \(t_i\). Since \(1/(K_i+2)\ge4/(l+16)\), summation of the logarithms gives

\[
 t_{i-1}\le \exp\left(-4\sum_{s=l+1}^H\frac1{s+16}\right)
       \le \left(\frac{l+17}{H+17}\right)^4 ,
 \qquad t_H\ll H^{-4}.                                              \tag{4}
\]

At a stopping interval \(|x-a/b|\le1/(K_i b^2)\), hence \(|h|=m|bx-a|\le N/(K_i b)\). Summing the arithmetic costs over the integers \(h\) in this range gives

\[
   O\left(b+\frac{N}{K_i}+\frac{N^2}{K_i^2b^2}\right)
\]

per such prefix. A reduced rational endpoint has at most two finite continued-fraction expansions with positive digits. Across **all** depths there are therefore \(O(B^2)\) active prefixes, since the endpoints have denominators at most \(B\). Their first two cost terms sum to \(O(B^3+NB^2)\). All exact rational endpoints with \(h=0\) add at most \(O(B^3)\).

Within one depth the active prefix cylinders have lengths \(1/[b(b+b')]\ge1/(2b^2)\). Thus the sum of \(1/b^2\) over the active parents of depth \(i-1\) is at most \(2t_{i-1}\). Since \(K_i\ge(l+1)/8\), (4) gives the total remaining cost

\[
 N^2\sum_{i=1}^H\frac{2t_{i-1}}{K_i^2}
       \ll \frac{N^2}{H^4}\sum_{l=1}^H(l+17)^2
       \ll \frac{N^2}{H}.                                         \tag{5}
\]

## Length and boundary count of the exceptional set

On the full continued-fraction tree with Lebesgue measure on \((0,1)\), let \(q_i\) be the \(i\)-th denominator. The formula for \(b_{\rm new}\) and (3) give \(\mathbb E[q_i^{1/2}\mid\text{prefix}]\le C_*q_{i-1}^{1/2}\). Iteration gives \(\mathbb E[q_H^{1/2}]\le C_*^H\le N^{1/32}\). Denominators are nondecreasing, so the union of the first-crossing cylinders has length at most

\[
    \Pr(q_H>B)\le B^{-1/2}\,\mathbb E[q_H^{1/2}]
             \le N^{-1/32}.
\]

The endpoints have measure zero and are included in the eventual closed cover. The other exceptional cylinders have total length \(t_H\), so the entire exceptional set has length \(t\ll H^{-4}+N^{-1/32}\).

Each first crossing comes from a retained digit \(j\le K_i\) and a parent denominator \(b\le B\); hence its endpoint denominator is at most \((H+2)B\). A depth-\(H\) noncrossing endpoint has denominator at most \(B\). The two-expansion property at rational endpoints therefore bounds the total number of exceptional intervals by \(J\ll B^2(H+2)^2\), independent of their overlap at endpoints. This avoids replacing a crossing by all its deeper descendants.

Use \(Q=\lfloor\sqrt N\rfloor\). For each exceptional \(x\), Dirichlet approximation gives \(1\le b\le Q,\ 0\le a\le b\) and \(|bx-a|\le1/Q\). Thus \(a/b\) lies in the \(1/Q\) neighborhood of the exceptional set. This neighborhood is a union of at most \(J\) intervals of total length at most \(t+2J/Q\). For a fixed \(b\) it permits \(O(bt+bJ/Q+J)\) integer numerators. There are \(O(tN+J\sqrt N)\) candidate \((a,b)\) in total. For each, summing the arithmetic cost through \(|h|\le N/Q\) gives \(O(N)\). Therefore the exceptional cost is

\[
 O(tN^2+JN^{3/2})
   \ll N^2 H^{-4}+N^{2-1/32}+N^{7/4}(H+2)^2
   \ll N^2/H.                                                       \tag{6}
\]

Equations (5) and (6), the smaller endpoint costs, and the short-order and fixed-prime estimates prove (1), since a layer occurring \(k\) times contributes exactly \((k-1)\log p\) to the overlap.

## The whole geometric support

For \(T_n\) the same cover gives \(\log L_T=482N^2\log\epsilon+O(N^2/\log(2N))\), where \(L_T=\operatorname{lcm}_{n\le N}T_n\). The coefficient uses \(\log T_n=964n\log\epsilon+O(1)\). For \(L_{\rm lin}=\operatorname{lcm}_{n\le N}\{b_{2n},a_{2n+1}\}\), the cyclotomic factors have indices odd \(3\le d\le2N+1\) or \(4\mid d,\ d\le4N\). Their sum of logarithms differs from \(\log L_{\rm lin}\) by \(O(N\log(2N))\); each factor has log \(\varphi(d)\log\epsilon+O(1)\). The odd-index totient sum equals \(2X^2/\pi^2+O(X\log(2X))\) by Möbius inversion. For the multiples of four, \(\varphi(4a)=4\varphi(a)-2\boldsymbol{1}_{a\ {\rm odd}}\varphi(a)\). Combining the ordinary totient sum \(3Y^2/\pi^2+O(Y\log(2Y))\) with the odd-index sum at \(Y=X/4\) gives \(X^2/(2\pi^2)+O(X\log(2X))\). Thus

\[
      \log L_{\rm lin}=(16/\pi^2)N^2\log\epsilon+O(N\log(2N)).
\]

Every odd prime power in \(L_{\rm lin}\) has \(g\)-order at most \(2N+1\). The common layers between \(L_{\rm lin}\) and \(L_T\) therefore give \(\log\gcd(L_{\rm lin},L_T)=O(N^{3/2})\), including the fixed exceptional primes by the elementary height bound on their maximum valuation. Since \(\gcd(b_{2n},a_{2n+1})=1\), their product at any one index divides \(L_{\rm lin}\). Consequently

\[
 \operatorname{lcm}(L_{\rm lin},L_T)
    \ \big|\ \operatorname{lcm}_{n\le N}W_n
    \ \big|\ 8L_{\rm lin}L_T .
\]

Taking logarithms yields (2). [Cyclotomic index sets, valuations and local orders](abc_pell_whole_lcm.md)
