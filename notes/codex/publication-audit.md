# Publication audit for “Radicals in iterated quadratic abc-transfers”

Date: 2026-07-26

## Scope and method

This was a bounded, non-exhaustive prior-art and venue audit. It combined:

- exact-formula searches for the transfer
  \((a,b,a+b)\mapsto(4ab,(a-b)^2,(a+b)^2)\);
- searches for iterations of that transfer and radical identities;
- searches for the associated dyadic Chebyshev and Lucas sequences;
- backward and forward checks through the citations of the closest sources;
- checks of theorem statements in the primary papers used for the
  Baker--Wüstholz, Lucas-valuation, and radical estimates; and
- current journal instructions and AI-use policies.

The search was continued through July 26, 2026. It cannot establish
absolute novelty, but it located and compared the closest sources found by
two independent agents.

## Closest prior work

1. Oesterlé (1988), p. 170, and Martin--Miao (2016), section 2.4, contain
   the classical quadratic transfer.
   - <https://www.numdam.org/item/SB_1987-1988__30__165_0/>
   - <https://doi.org/10.7169/facm/2016.55.2.2>

2. Ohana--Spicer--Stein (2013), Proposition 1, gives the exact one-step
   radical formula for odd \(c\).
   - <https://cocalc.com/share/download/c1f4c5685b89bae0dfa24156574398b8c8172a3a/briefing/brief.pdf>

3. Holsztyński (2017) describes the same iterated map on the
   Pythagorean-square locus. Guninski (2020) gives a general hit-preserving
   iteration and one-step quality control.
   - <https://mathoverflow.net/questions/263463/>
   - <https://mathoverflow.net/questions/356295/>

4. Bolvardizadeh (2023), Chapter 2, studies the encompassing Lucas
   \(abc\)-triple construction and identifies the negative-discriminant
   archimedean limit as future work.
   - <https://hdl.handle.net/10133/6591>

5. Hajdu--Tijdeman (2025), Theorem 2.1 and Remark 1, subsequently give
   stronger all-index non-real Lucas growth estimates. They already imply
   the dyadic archimedean limit in the manuscript; the manuscript now
   states this explicitly and makes no novelty claim for that limit.
   - <https://doi.org/10.1007/s11139-025-01041-6>

6. Ribenboim (2002) gives related dyadic Lucas factorizations and
   square-free-kernel results.
   - <https://doi.org/10.4064/aa101-2-9>

7. Sun (2013), Theorem 3(ii), supplies the per-prime Lucas valuation law
   used in the specialization. The manuscript claims novelty only for the
   aggregation of these contributions across the \(abc\)-orbit.
   - <https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.3511>

8. Stewart (1983), Theorem 1, supplies the unconditional radical estimate
   used in section 4.2.
   - <https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms/s2-28.2.211>

Anitha--Fathima--Vijayalakshmi (2023) is additional recent context for
conditional \(abc\)-to-Lucas non-Wieferich results in the
positive-discriminant setting. It does not overlap the claimed
whole-orbit identity.
- <https://doi.org/10.1515/math-2022-0563>

## Residual contribution judged defensible

No checked source was found that contains the following combination for
every primitive positive opposite-parity seed:

1. the disjoint prime-support factorization along the entire orbit;
2. the exact sine/cosocle radical identity;
3. the effective two-sided \(O(n)\) control of the archimedean factor;
4. eventual entry of every such orbit into the \(abc\)-hits; and
5. the aggregate if-and-only-if criterion relating a fixed quality gap to
   repeated-prime growth.

The contribution is modest and is best described as a narrowly scoped
short note. It neither proves nor disproves the \(abc\) conjecture.

## Venue audit

### Recommended: Mathematika

Mathematika is the best policy-compatible journal target found. Its
official scope includes number theory and it accepts a single PDF at
initial submission:
<https://www.lms.ac.uk/publications/mtk/submission>.

The London Mathematical Society's current policy expressly permits
declared AI use in generating ideas, performing calculations, and
constructing or verifying proofs, while placing full checking and
responsibility on the human author:
<https://www.lms.ac.uk/publications/policies/AI>.

The paper is at the lower edge of Mathematika's likely significance
threshold, so editorial acceptance is uncertain. The cover letter should
describe it as an exact short note and disclose the full AI workflow.

### Preprint: arXiv math.NT

An arXiv preprint would establish a public timestamp and invite specialist
feedback. It remains subject to arXiv moderation and any endorsement
requirement. No claim is made here about an arXiv-specific AI policy that
could not be verified on an official policy page.

### Venues ruled out for this manuscript as written

- INTEGERS says it will not consider work using AI to produce mathematics,
  code, bibliography, or other content:
  <https://math.colgate.edu/~integers/submit.html>.
- The Journal of Integer Sequences prohibits using an LLM to write the
  English text:
  <https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/journals/JIS/>.
- Notes on Number Theory and Discrete Mathematics likewise prohibits LLMs
  from writing manuscripts:
  <https://nntdm.net/instructions-to-authors/>.
- The Fibonacci Quarterly is mathematically well matched, but the current
  Taylor & Francis policy says submitted content must be created by the
  author and AI must not replace core researcher responsibilities. Given
  the disclosed depth of assistance here, it is a policy-risky target
  without advance editor confirmation:
  <https://taylorandfrancis.com/our-policies/ai-policy/>.

## External human gate

The reciprocal AI review establishes only an AI-audit record. Before any
submission, the named human author must personally check the proofs,
sources, code, final PDF, and AI-use statement and must be willing to
assume responsibility for all of them. Journal peer review remains
necessary and may uncover errors or closer prior art missed by this audit.
