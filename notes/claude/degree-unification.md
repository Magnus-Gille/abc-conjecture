## 1. The quadratic case ℓ = 2 subsumes paper 1

Take the transfer
\[
t_2:(a,b)\longmapsto(4ab,\ (b-a)^2),\qquad c\mapsto c^2 .
\]

CLAIM U1 (semiconjugacy). With \(x=(b-a)/c\): \(x'=T_2(x)\), so
\(x_n=\cos(2^n\theta)\), \(\cos\theta=(b_0-a_0)/c_0\). [Direct algebra:
\(((b-a)^2-4ab)/c^2=2x^2-1\).]

CLAIM U2 (single tower). Opposite parity is preserved
(\(4ab\) even, \((b-a)^2\) odd), \(E_n:=|b_n-a_n|\) is odd, coprime to
\(a_nb_nc_n\), and the collection \(E_0,E_1,\dots\) is pairwise coprime;
\(R_{n+1}=R_n\operatorname{rad}(E_n)\). The second coordinate
\(4a_nb_n\) contributes NO new primes — the odd-degree A/B branch pair
degenerates to one tower.

CLAIM U3 (unified atom formula). With the SAME quadratic Lucas pair as
the odd case, \(\alpha,\beta=(b_0-a_0)\pm2\sqrt{-a_0b_0}\),
\(\alpha\beta=c_0^2\):
\[
\boxed{\ \ell\,E_n=\bigl|\Phi_{\ell^{\,n+1}}(\alpha,\beta)\bigr|
\ \ \text{holds verbatim at }\ell=2,}
\]
i.e. \(2E_n=|\alpha^{2^n}+\beta^{2^n}|\). Verified for four seeds
through level 3 via the integer Lucas recursion (A2 block of the
script). Consequently Theorem 13's congruence becomes
\(2^{\,n+1}\mid p-\bigl(\tfrac{D_K}p\bigr)\) for \(p\mid E_n\), and
Proposition 14's criterion becomes: \(p^2\mid E_n\iff
u^{2^{n+1}}\equiv1\pmod{\mathfrak p^2}\) — exactly the Lucas–Wieferich
obstruction of paper 1. Paper 1's orbit \((1,8,9)\) is the seed example:
\(2E_0=|\alpha+\beta|=2\cdot7\).

CLAIM U4 (same telescope). With \(W_n=\prod_{j<n}E_j/\operatorname{rad}(E_j)\):
\[
\frac{R_n}{c_n}
=\frac{R_0}{c_0}\,
\frac{|\sin(2^n\theta)|}{2^n\sin\theta\,W_n}
\]
— the exact statement of Theorem 5 with \(\ell=2\). Verified on
\((1,8,9)\) through \(n=4\) (A1 block). Proof identical: telescoping
\(\prod\cos(2^j\theta)=\sin(2^n\theta)/(2^n\sin\theta)\).

CLAIM U5 (NEW: programmed squares for quadratic orbits). The level-\(n\)
atom in seed coordinates is a degree-\(2^n\) integer form (level 1:
\(X^2-6XY+Y^2\)); the Cayley classification applies with
\(m=2^{n+2}\): roots mod \(p\) exist iff \(2^{n+2}\mid p\mp1\), and the
Hensel+CRT realization goes through with no \(\ell\)-adic side
condition. Executed instance \((p,n,h,\chi)=(7,1,2,-1)\): three
primitive seeds with \(v_7(E_1)=2\) exactly, \(7\nmid a_0b_0c_0E_0\),
\(\chi=-1\) (A3 block). Paper 1 verified squarefreeness computationally
and treated square occurrence as an open obstruction; the realization
machinery CONSTRUCTS such squares to order. This is new content relative
to paper 1 and strengthens the case that Theorem 17 is the promotable
result of the draft.

Consequences for the manuscript: the draft's restriction to odd primes
is a presentational choice, not a mathematical one. A uniform statement
"for every prime \(\ell\ge2\)" is available, with the odd-only branch
structure (two towers) replaced at \(\ell=2\) by one tower and the
systematic prime \(2\) playing the role of \(\ell\).

## 2. Composite odd degree: reduction to prime chains

For ANY odd \(\ell\ge3\), \(S_\ell,C_\ell\) and the transfer identity
hold verbatim (de Moivre uses no primality), and
\(x_{n+1}=T_\ell(x_n)\).

CLAIM U6 (chain factorization). If \(\ell=\ell_1\ell_2\) (odd), then,
with \((a',b')=t_{\ell_1}(a,b)\) the \(\ell_1\)-transfer image,
\[
S_{\ell}(a,b)=S_{\ell_1}(a,b)\,S_{\ell_2}(a',b'),
\qquad
C_{\ell}(a,b)=C_{\ell_1}(a,b)\,C_{\ell_2}(a',b'),
\]
and the degree-\(\ell\) step is the composition of the prime steps in
any order (\(T_m\) commute; the orbit maps agree exactly:
\(t_{\ell_2}\circ t_{\ell_1}=t_{\ell_1}\circ t_{\ell_2}=t_\ell\)).
Verified exactly with signs for \(\ell=15\) on four seeds, both orders,
including the full transferred triples (B1 block). [Sketch: quotient of
sine/cosine products; degrees and leading structure match, and the
identity is polynomial, so finitely many numeric checks plus degree
bounds already pin it — full symbolic proof for the record is a short
induction via \(z^{\ell}=(z^{\ell_1})^{\ell_2}\).]

Consequently: the composite-degree orbit is a SUBSAMPLING of the
multi-prime semigroup orbit generated by the prime-degree transfers
\(\{t_q:q\mid\ell\}\), which pairwise commute. The invariant object is
the commutative semigroup orbit; every composite-degree genealogy
statement decomposes along any prime chain.

CLAIM U7 (layer structure). At one composite step of degree \(\ell\),
\[
S_n=\prod_{d\in D_n}\Phi_d(\omega,\bar\omega),\quad
C_n=\prod_{d\in D_n}\Phi_{2d}(\omega,\bar\omega),\quad
D_n=\{d:\ d\mid\ell^{\,n+1},\ d\nmid\ell^{\,n}\},
\]
with all indices across levels and branches pairwise distinct
(\(d\in D_n\cap D_{n'}\) is impossible for \(n<n'\) since
\(d\mid\ell^{n+1}\mid\ell^{n'}\)). Each atom is symmetric of even
degree, hence an INTEGER form in the seed (a polynomial in
\(e_1^2=4Y\) and \(e_2=X+Y\)); rationality spot-checked at
\(\Phi_3=S_3\), \(\Phi_5=S_5\) (B3 block). So the genealogy refines: a
prime \(p\nmid2\ell\) divides at most one atom EVER, its label being the
exact multiplicative order \(d\) (or \(2d\)) of the reduced \(v\), and
the order congruence reads \(d\mid p-(\tfrac{D_K}p)\) with \(d\) now
ranging over the full divisor lattice new at its level.

CLAIM U8 (corrected normalization; where the naive theory breaks). The
draft's Lemma 1 (\(v_\ell(S_n)=1\)) is a PRIME-degree fact. For
composite or prime-power degree the correct statement comes from the
chain: e.g. for \(\ell=9\),
\(S_9(a,b)=S_3(a,b)\,S_3(a',b')\) gives
\(v_3(S_9)=v_3(S_3(a,b))+v_3(S_3(a',b'))\); the generic admissible
value is \(1+1=2=v_3(9)\), but seeds with \(v_3(3b-a)\ge2\) give
\(v_3(S_9)\ge3\) (both branches verified, B2 block; degenerate example
\((12,1)\): \(v_3(S_9)=3\)). The admissibility condition for composite
degree is therefore the CONJUNCTION of the prime-step conditions along
the chain — for the 3-part, the \(\ell=3\) condition at the first
3-step only, since \(v_3(a)\ge3\) afterwards makes later 3-steps
automatic (paper-draft Lemma 1 mechanism). For \(q\ge5\) no side
condition arises. Binomial-valuation route
(\(v_3\binom93=1<2\)) confirms that the naive "\(v_q(S_\ell)=v_q(\ell)\)
identically" is false without these conditions.

Consequence: nothing fundamentally new happens at composite degree —
the theory REDUCES to iterated prime-degree theory with bookkeeping.
The honest framing for the manuscript is a short section stating U6–U8
and deriving the multi-layer congruence dictionary, not a parallel
composite theory. The "overlap graph" question raised in 0077 resolves
as: no overlaps — index sets are disjoint; the graph is a disjoint
union of labeled chains.
